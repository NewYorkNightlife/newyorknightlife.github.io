Cocktail dens: where NYC still leads the world

NYC's cocktail rooms are the city's most reliable "great night in one stop." The trade-off is cost and friction: $18–$25 per drink and, at the famous rooms, a wait.

  • Attaboy (Lower East Side) — no menu, no reservations; bartenders build a drink around what you like. Cocktails run right around $20. Expect a doorbell, a wait on Eldridge Street on weekends, and one of the best drinks of your year.
  • Death & Co (East Village) — the modern-cocktail institution, dark and civilized, with a deep menu. Runs a same-day waitlist; go early on a weeknight to walk in.
  • Please Don't Tell (PDT) (East Village) — enter through the phone booth inside Crif Dogs. Yes it's a bit of theater; the drinks hold up, and you can order a hot dog at the bar.
  • The Dead Rabbit (Financial District) — Irish pub downstairs, refined cocktail parlor upstairs. One of the few destination bars in FiDi, and great before 10pm.
  • Employees Only (West Village) — late-night bartender's bar with a psychic in the window and a kitchen open into the small hours.

If the hidden-door genre is your thing, the deeper cut list lives at Speakeasies in NYC — including The Back Room, a Prohibition-era holdover on the Lower East Side that serves cocktails in teacups.

Dive bars: the best value in NYC nightlife

Dives are where the $8 beer-and-shot combo still exists and nobody cares what you're wearing. Reliable names: 169 Bar (Chinatown/LES border, leopard-print pool table), Rudy's Bar & Grill (Hell's Kitchen, famous free hot dogs), Doc Holliday's (East Village, country jukebox), and The Levee (Williamsburg, free cheese balls). Downsides are real: some are cash-preferred, bathrooms are grim, and weekend crowds at the famous ones can be shoulder-to-shoulder. Dives are also the correct backbone for a group night — our NYC bar crawl guide builds full routes around them.

Wine bars: the conversation-first option

NYC's natural-wine wave gave the city a deep bench of rooms where the point is talking, not shouting. The Four Horsemen (Williamsburg) is the standard-bearer — a Michelin-starred natural wine bar where walk-in seats go fast after 6pm. Ruffian (East Village) is a tiny, adventurous list; June (Cobble Hill) is the date-night sleeper. Expect $15–$22 a glass and kitchens that close by 11pm — wine bars are a first stop, not a last one.

Beer bars: craft depth without the scene

Tørst (Greenpoint) is the city's most serious draft list in its most serene room. Spuyten Duyvil (Williamsburg) leans Belgian and funky with a good backyard; Proletariat (East Village) is small, loud, and rare-release focused. Pints run $8–$12, nobody rushes you, and most wind down well before last call — pair one with a dive to finish the night.

Hotel and rooftop bars: pay for the view, plan for the door

Rooftops are NYC's most bookable "wow" moment and its most price-inflated pour: $22–$30 cocktails, dress codes, and sunset-hour lines. The ones worth the premium:

  • Westlight (Williamsburg, 22nd floor) — the best skyline-facing view of Manhattan, period.
  • Le Bain (Meatpacking) — part rooftop, part club, occasionally a hot tub; goes late and gets loose.
  • Harriet's Rooftop (Brooklyn Heights) — bridge-and-skyline views at 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge.
  • Ophelia Lounge (Midtown East) — art-deco tower room, more polished than party.
  • 230 Fifth (NoMad) — huge, touristy, heated igloos in winter; easiest big-group rooftop.
  • Mr. Purple (Lower East Side) and PHD Rooftop (Meatpacking) — scene-forward rooftops where the door tightens after 10pm.
  • Magic Hour (Times Square) and Somewhere Nowhere (Chelsea) — big, theatrical, and club-adjacent late on weekends.

Full price-and-policy breakdowns are in our rooftop bars guide. For a dressed-up lounge night at street level, Beauty & Essex (behind a pawn-shop front on the LES) splits the difference between restaurant, lounge, and scene.

Where the bars cluster: pick one zone and stay in it

The single best bar-night decision is geographic. Every type above exists within a 15-minute walk in these zones:

  • Lower East Side — highest bar density in the city; dives, cocktail dens, and late rooms on top of each other.
  • East Village — the best all-rounder: dives, Death & Co, PDT, wine and beer bars within blocks.
  • Williamsburg — wine bars, craft beer, Westlight up top, and music venues to pivot to.
  • Bushwick — cheaper, weirder, later; the bar night that turns into a club night.
  • Meatpacking — rooftops and dressed-up lounges; the priciest zone on this page.

What a bar night actually costs (2026)

Real math, per person, before food: a three-stop dive night is $30–$45 with tips. A mid-range mixed night (one beer bar, one cocktail room, one nightcap) is $70–$100. A cocktail-den crawl is $90–$140, and one rooftop round for two can hit $70 by itself. Most bars have no cover; if your night includes anything with a door charge, run it through the cover charge calculator first. Prices last verified: 2026-08-26.

FAQ

How much does a night of bar-hopping cost in NYC?

Budget $60–$120 per person for three to four drinks plus tip at mid-range bars. A dive-bar night can come in under $40; a cocktail-den night runs $90–$140 once each drink is $18–$25 plus 20% tip.

Do NYC bars charge cover?

Most NYC bars have no cover. Covers are mainly a club and live-music thing, though a few lounges and speakeasy-style rooms add a fee or minimum on weekends — usually $10–$20 when they do.

What time do NYC bars close?

New York State's last call is 4am, and many dives and neighborhood bars pour right up to it on weekends. Plenty of cocktail and wine bars close earlier — midnight to 2am — so check hours before building a late route, or see our after-hours guide for what's open latest.

Do I need a reservation at NYC cocktail bars?

Depends on the room. Attaboy is walk-in only with a street wait; Death & Co runs a same-day waitlist; rooftops like Westlight and Harriet's take (and often need) reservations for sunset slots. Dives, beer bars, and most wine bars are walk-in.
