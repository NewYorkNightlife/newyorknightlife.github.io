JFK vs LGA vs EWR: Which Airport Is Best for NYC Nightlife?

If going out is the point of your trip, your airport choice quietly decides how much of night one you get to keep. This guide compares all three NYC airports on cost and time to the five zones nightlife visitors actually go — Lower East Side, Williamsburg, Bushwick, Midtown, and Meatpacking — with verified 2026 fares.

The one-line verdict: fly into JFK for Brooklyn nights, LGA for cheap Manhattan access, and avoid landing at EWR late if you can help it.

Airport by Airport: the Nightlife Case

JFK — best rail, best Brooklyn access

JFK is the only NYC airport where the trains never stop: AirTrain runs 24/7 and hands you off to the 24/7 A and E subway lines. For nightlife, the underrated weapon is the A train to Broadway Junction, where you transfer to the L (Williamsburg/Bushwick) or J/Z — meaning JFK connects to Brooklyn's club corridor without touching Manhattan. Rideshares to Bushwick are also the cheapest of any airport-to-club run in the city ($35–$60 typical).

LGA — cheapest, closest, but bus-dependent

LaGuardia is physically closest to both Midtown and north Brooklyn, so rides are the cheapest of the three airports. The free, 24/7 Q70 bus links the terminals to the E/F/M/R/7 subway hub at Jackson Heights–Roosevelt Av in about 10 minutes — making LGA-to-anywhere possible for $3.00. The tradeoff: no rail link, so a bad traffic day on the BQE eats your evening. If you land 4–7pm on a weekday, read rush-hour routes to avoid before choosing a car.

EWR — fine for Midtown, weak for nightlife

Newark's NJ Transit link to Penn Station is quick (about 40–45 minutes, $17.25 including AirTrain), and for a Midtown hotel it's genuinely competitive. But the system shuts down roughly 1am–5am, AirTrain Newark has had shuttle-bus substitutions during its 2026 reconstruction, and car fares to Brooklyn run $80–$120 before surge. If your trip is built around Bushwick warehouse parties, EWR is the wrong airport.

Late-Night Transit From Each Airport

  • JFK: AirTrain 24/7 ($8.75) + A/E trains 24/7. Overnight trains run local, every 15–20 minutes.
  • LGA: Q70 free bus 24/7 to E/F/7 trains (all 24/7). The M and R don't run overnight.
  • EWR: nothing on rails after roughly 1am. Plan a car or don't book the red-eye.

Landing between 9pm and 3am? The play-by-play — taxi lines, surge timing, and the arrivals-hall hustlers to ignore — is in our late-night airport arrival guide.

Congestion Pricing: What It Adds to Your Ride

Manhattan's congestion-pricing zone (below 60th St) adds a per-trip passenger charge to for-hire rides: $0.75 in yellow taxis, $1.50 in Uber/Lyft. That stacks on the older NY State congestion surcharge ($2.50 taxi / $2.75 rideshare) applied south of 96th St. Practical takeaways: Midtown, Meatpacking, and LES drop-offs carry a few dollars of unavoidable surcharges; Williamsburg and Bushwick drop-offs don't. It's never the deciding factor — but it explains the line items on your receipt. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

Luggage Storage: Landing and Going Straight Out

Red-eye in, party immediately? App-based storage networks — Bounce, Vertoe, and Radical Storage — hold bags at shops and hotels across the LES, Williamsburg, and Midtown for roughly $5–$8 per bag per day (book in-app; prices vary by location). Two honest caveats: most storage points close before midnight, so if you need the bag back tonight, check hours before booking; and none of the three airports offers cheap public left-luggage, so store near the venue, not at the terminal. Smarter still: pick a hotel in your party zone and drop bags first — see where to stay for nightlife or the deeper dive on staying near the nightlife.

FAQ

Which NYC airport is best if nightlife is the priority?

JFK in most cases: it's the only one with genuinely 24/7 rail, and the A-train corridor reaches Brooklyn nightlife via Broadway Junction. LGA wins on cheap short rides; EWR is weakest because trains stop around 1am and car fares are the highest.

How much is the AirTrain at JFK in 2026?

$8.75 per ride, tap-to-pay via OMNY, running 24/7. With the $3.00 subway, total transit from JFK is $11.75.

Does congestion pricing change what I pay from the airport?

Slightly: +$0.75 per taxi trip and +$1.50 per Uber/Lyft trip below 60th St, on top of the $2.50–$2.75 state surcharge south of 96th St. Brooklyn destinations avoid it entirely.

Can I store luggage and go straight out?

Yes — Bounce, Vertoe, and Radical Storage run $5–$8 per bag per day at locations across nightlife neighborhoods. Check closing times: many shut before midnight.
