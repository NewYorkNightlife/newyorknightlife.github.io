NYC Nightlife Transport Hub

Every New York night out is really two trips: getting there sober and getting home at 3am. The second one is where plans fall apart. This hub collects the transport guides that keep both ends of the night cheap and low-stress.

  • JFK / LGA / EWR to Nightlife — landing and going out the same night: what each airport connection really costs in time and money.
  • Rush-Hour Routes to Avoid — for early starts: which crosstown and bridge routes eat an hour of your evening.
  • Bridges, Tunnels & Tolls — driving to a night out (or being the designated driver): what the crossings cost and when they jam.

Related planning guides

The short version of everything above

The subway is the right answer more often than visitors think — it's the cheapest ride in the city and runs all night. The catch is overnight headways: at 3am you may wait 15–20 minutes, so factor the wait, not just the ride. Rideshare wins on group math (four people splitting a car often beats four fares) and on trips the subway does badly, like crosstown or borough-to-borough diagonals — but expect surge pricing exactly when everyone leaves at once at 4am. The last-call trick locals use: leave 20 minutes before close or 30 after, never at the whistle.

Plan it into the night, not after

The night planner asks about your ride home for a reason, and the budget planner counts it, because a $60 surge ride can double a cheap night. If you're picking between venues, distance-to-train is a real tiebreaker — venue pages in our facts library note the nearest lines.

Visitors: one warning worth repeating

Never take an unsolicited "taxi" offer inside an airport terminal or outside a club — solicited rides are both against the rules and the single most reliable way to overpay in this city. Real yellow/green cabs use the taxi line and the meter; rideshares are booked in the app, with the plate matched before you get in.
