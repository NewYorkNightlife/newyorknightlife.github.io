NYC Bridge & Tunnel Tolls for a Night Out: the 2026 Driver's Guide

Driving into the city for a night out means three separate bills before your first drink: the crossing toll, the congestion toll, and parking. This page gives you every 2026 rate — E-ZPass vs Tolls by Mail for each crossing, the overnight congestion discount that actually rewards nightlife hours — plus the honest parking math and the one rule that isn't negotiable.

The short version: get E-ZPass before you come (mail billing costs 40–60% more), arrive after 9pm to pay $2.25 instead of $9 in Manhattan, and decide now who isn't drinking.

The Congestion Relief Zone: the Toll That Shapes Your Night

Manhattan at or below 60th Street is a tolled zone. Enter its local streets in a passenger car and you pay — once per day, no matter how many times you cross the line. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

  • Peak (5am–9pm weekdays, 9am–9pm weekends): $9.00 with E-ZPass.
  • Overnight (9pm–5am weekdays, 9pm–9am weekends): $2.25 with E-ZPass — a 75% discount that happens to match nightlife hours perfectly.
  • Without E-ZPass: both rates jump by 50% via Tolls by Mail ($13.50 peak).
  • Crossing credit: arrive during peak via the Lincoln, Holland, Queens-Midtown, or Hugh L. Carey tunnels and up to $3.00 comes off the congestion toll. No credit overnight — the $2.25 rate is already the deal.

Practical read for a night out: rolling into the zone at 9:05pm instead of 8:45pm saves $6.75. If dinner's at 8, park just outside the zone or eat uptown first. The rate is slated to rise to $12 in 2028, so enjoy the $9 era. The FDR Drive, West Side Highway, and Battery Park Underpass are excluded — but the moment you exit onto a local street below 60th, you've entered.

Coming From New Jersey: Port Authority Crossings

The George Washington Bridge, Lincoln Tunnel, and Holland Tunnel share one toll schedule, charged eastbound (into New York) only — the ride home is free. 2026 passenger-car rates: $16.79 peak / $14.79 off-peak with E-ZPass, $23.30 by mail. Peak hours are weekdays 6–10am and 4–8pm, weekends 11am–9pm — so a Saturday 9:30pm arrival through the Lincoln Tunnel pays the off-peak $14.79, plus $2.25 congestion toll, minus nothing (no credit overnight): about $17 in tolls for the whole night. The same trip billed by mail with a peak arrival: $23.30 + $13.50 = $36.80. That difference is your first two drinks. Note the off-peak discount starts phasing out in 2027, and Jersey drivers heading to Brooklyn should weigh the Verrazzano route via Staten Island against Manhattan traffic — see rush-hour routes to avoid if you're arriving before 8pm.

The Free Bridges — and the Catch

The Brooklyn, Manhattan, Williamsburg, and Queensboro bridges have no crossing toll, and they're the reason a Brooklyn night out can be genuinely toll-free. The catch: the first three drop you inside the Congestion Relief Zone, so "free bridge" still means the congestion toll if you're going to the Lower East Side or anywhere below 60th. The Queensboro lands at the zone's northern edge — head uptown from it and you can avoid the toll entirely. Driving out of Manhattan over any East River bridge is always free. Best of all: a night in Williamsburg or Bushwick from Queens or Long Island can involve zero tolls, full stop.

Parking Reality Near Nightlife Zones

Parking is usually the biggest line item of a driving night — bigger than every toll combined.

  • Lower East Side / downtown Manhattan: expect roughly $40–$60 for an evening in a garage, more for oversized vehicles (SUV surcharges of $10–$15 are standard).
  • Midtown: similar or higher at drive-up rates, but evening/overnight packages booked ahead often come in around $25–$45.
  • Williamsburg / Bushwick: garages run meaningfully cheaper than Manhattan, and free overnight street parking exists if you read the signs carefully — alternate-side rules and fire hydrants generate real tickets ($65+).

Booking ahead on a parking app almost always beats the drive-up rate, sometimes by half: compare garages on SpotHero at spothero.com/city/nyc-parking before you leave. Confirm the exit time on your reservation — leaving at 3am on a reservation that ends at 1am means paying the difference at the drive-up rate. Some garages also close overnight; a 24-hour exit matters more than a $5 saving.

The Non-Negotiable: Someone Isn't Drinking

If the car comes in, the night needs a designated driver — named before the first bar, not negotiated at last call. New York enforces DWI at 0.08% BAC (and impairment charges below that), with license revocation, four-figure fines, and possible jail on a first offense; checkpoints around bridge and tunnel approaches on weekend nights are routine. Nobody sober enough to insist? The car stays parked overnight (most garages sell the extra hours cheap compared to the alternative) and everyone goes home by train or a split ride — the math and safety playbook is in safe late-night transport. Run your whole night's numbers — tolls, parking, covers, drinks — through the trip cost calculator or budget planner before deciding the car is cheaper. Sometimes it genuinely is; sometimes it only looks that way until the garage receipt.

FAQ

Which NYC bridges are free in 2026?

The four East River bridges — Brooklyn, Manhattan, Williamsburg, and Queensboro. But the first three land inside the Congestion Relief Zone, so entering local streets at or below 60th St still triggers the congestion toll ($9 peak / $2.25 overnight with E-ZPass).

How much is the congestion toll at night?

$2.25 for passenger cars with E-ZPass — 75% off the $9 peak rate. Overnight hours: 9pm–5am weekdays, 9pm–9am weekends. Charged at most once per day.

Is E-ZPass worth it for one night out in NYC?

Yes, decisively. MTA crossings: $7.46 vs $12.03 by mail. Port Authority crossings: $16.79 peak vs $23.30 by mail. The congestion toll also jumps by half without it. One round trip can pay for the tag.

Where should I park for a night in Williamsburg or the Lower East Side?

Williamsburg garages run cheaper than Manhattan and some street parking is free overnight — read the signs. LES garages run roughly $40–$60 for an evening; booking ahead on SpotHero usually beats drive-up rates. Or park outside the zone and take the subway in.

Can I drive home after drinking in NYC?

No. New York enforces DWI at 0.08% BAC with license revocation and possible jail. Bring a designated driver, or park-and-subway and collect the car in the morning.
