3-Day NYC Nightlife Itinerary

This is a complete three-night plan — rooftop sunset and speakeasies on night one, Lower East Side bars on night two, a Brooklyn club on night three — with hour-by-hour timing and honest costs. It's paced so you're still standing on night three, which is where most three-day itineraries fall apart.

The logic: each night stays in one neighborhood cluster, energy builds from cocktails to dive bars to a dance floor, and every outdoor plan has an indoor backup. If you're new to the city, skim the first-time NYC nightlife guide first, then come back.

Day 1: Arrival + the Classic Manhattan Night

Don't overschedule arrival day. Check in, eat something real, and rest — the night ahead runs on its own clock.

  • 5:30–6:00pm — Head to a rooftop before sunset. 230 Fifth is the easy first-timer pick: direct Empire State Building views and no cover, though it's crowded and touristy — that's the trade. For a sceneier option, Le Bain at The Standard, High Line covers the Hudson side, or pick from the best rooftops in NYC. One or two drinks max ($18–$25 each at rooftop prices); you're here for the light, not the bill.
  • 7:30–9:00pm — Dinner near your next stop. Book the East Village or Flatiron so you're not crossing town after. A solid sit-down dinner runs $40–$80 per person with a drink; reserve 1–2 weeks out for weekend nights.
  • 9:30pm–midnight — Speakeasy round. Attaboy (Lower East Side) is walk-in only with no menu — the bartender builds a drink around what you like. PDT hides behind a phone booth in Crif Dogs on St. Marks and books out fast. Death & Co is the East Village standby with deep menus and a manageable wait early in the week. Cocktails at this tier run roughly $20–$24. Two bars is plenty.
  • Midnight–1am — Wind down. One nightcap or a slice, then back. Night three is the late one; spend your stamina there.

Honest downside: Friday and Saturday speakeasy waits can hit 60–90 minutes. Go Thursday if your dates allow, or put your name in at Attaboy before dinner and eat nearby while you wait for the text.

Day 2: Downtown Day + Lower East Side Bar Night

Daytime: walk SoHo and the Lower East Side — shops, galleries, and a long lunch. Stay downtown all day so the night starts with zero transit.

  • 7:00–8:30pm — Casual dinner in the East Village or LES. This is the cheap-eats capital of Manhattan; $20–$40 per person gets you fed well.
  • 8:30–10:00pm — One proper cocktail stop. The Back Room serves drinks in teacups in a genuine Prohibition-era space — touristy, yes, but the room is real. Employees Only in the West Village is the pick if your group skews cocktail-nerd.
  • 10:00pm–1:00am — Bar-hop the grid. The Lower East Side around Ludlow and Orchard Streets is the densest bar cluster in the city; the East Village along Avenue A is its scruffier sibling. Dive-bar beers run $7–$9, cocktails $14–$18. Three bars, one drink each, is the sweet spot — our bar crawl guide has ready-made routes.
  • 1:00–2:00am — Late food. Dollar-ish slices and 24-hour spots are everywhere down here; see the late-night food map.

Pacing note: this is deliberately a no-cover night. Two nights of $25 cocktails in a row breaks most budgets and most livers.

Day 3: Brooklyn Day + Williamsburg/Bushwick Club Night

Daytime: take the F to York Street for Dumbo — the Manhattan Bridge photo spot, the waterfront park, lunch at Time Out Market. Then the ferry or the G up to Williamsburg for the afternoon. April through October, Smorgasburg runs Saturdays 11am–6pm in Marsha P. Johnson State Park on the waterfront — it's the easiest big group lunch in the borough.

  • 6:00–8:00pm — Golden hour at Westlight. Westlight, on the 22nd floor of the William Vale, gives you the Manhattan skyline from the Brooklyn side — and it's indoors, so weather can't cancel it.
  • 8:00–10:30pm — Dinner and warm-up bars in Williamsburg. Bedford and Grand Street corridors have everything from taquerias to tasting menus. Keep it to one or two relaxed drinks.
  • 10:30pm — Ride to Bushwick. The L train is the spine: Jefferson Street or Morgan Avenue stops put you in club territory in about 15 minutes.
  • 11:00pm–late — The main event. House of Yes is the signature pick: circus-cabaret-club hybrid with themed parties and a consent-forward door; covers run $0–$40 depending on the event, and advance tickets are strongly recommended for weekends. Prefer straight-up dance music? Bossa Nova Civic Club is a small, sweaty techno room, and Elsewhere is the multi-room warehouse option. Doors mean it: most Bushwick floors don't fill until midnight and run past 4am.

Getting home: the L runs all night but with long late-night headways; a rideshare back to Manhattan at 3am typically runs $30–$50. Details in safe late-night transport.

When to Book What

  • 2–3 weeks out: Day 1 dinner reservation; House of Yes or Elsewhere tickets if a specific weekend event is announced.
  • 1 week out: PDT or Death & Co reservation attempts (release windows vary — check their sites and Resy the week of); rooftop table if you want guaranteed seating rather than standing room.
  • Day of: Attaboy and The Back Room are walk-in; check what's on tonight for one-off events before you commit.

Bad-Weather Swaps

  • Rainy Day 1: skip the open-air rooftop; 230 Fifth's covered, heated section still works, or go straight to a longer speakeasy circuit.
  • Rainy Day 3 daytime: trade the Dumbo walk for Williamsburg record shops, vintage stores, and a brewery taproom afternoon.
  • Freezing snap: Westlight and The Back Room are fully indoors; the club night is unaffected — Bushwick warehouses don't care about weather.

Budget Reality

  • Subway: $3.00 per ride with an OMNY tap; you're automatically capped at $35 for any rolling 7-day window, so a 3-day trip almost never hits the cap. Last verified: 2026-08-26.
  • Drinks: rooftop/speakeasy cocktails $18–$25; standard bars $14–$18; dive beers $7–$9. Last verified: 2026-08-26.
  • Covers: $0 for nearly all bars on this plan; $0–$40 for the Bushwick club night depending on event.
  • Three-night total: roughly $330–$580 per person for nightlife alone, before hotel and daytime meals.

FAQ

How much does a 3-day nightlife trip to NYC cost per person?

Plan on roughly $120–$250 per night out, on top of hotel and daytime food: about $150–$250 for the classic Manhattan night, $80–$150 for the Lower East Side bar night, and $100–$180 for the Brooklyn club night. Subway rides are $3.00 with an OMNY tap, capped at $35 per rolling week.

Do I need reservations for NYC speakeasies?

For the famous ones, yes — or arrive early. Attaboy is walk-in only, so show up before 7pm or expect a wait. PDT and Death & Co release reservations online and prime slots go fast, so book as soon as your dates are set and keep a walk-in backup on the same block.

What should I do if it rains on my rooftop night?

Swap the rooftop for an indoor bar with a view or a cocktail den. 230 Fifth keeps a covered, heated rooftop section running year-round, and Westlight in Williamsburg is indoors with floor-to-ceiling windows. Otherwise flip the schedule and move your speakeasy stop earlier in the evening.

Is the subway safe for getting back late at night?

Trains run 24/7 and weekend late-night ridership in nightlife corridors is steady. Wait near the turnstile area rather than the platform edge, ride the busier cars, and budget for a rideshare back if you are leaving Bushwick after 3am.
