Airport to Manhattan at Night: What Actually Works After a Late Arrival

Land at JFK, LaGuardia, or Newark between 9pm and 3am and your options shrink fast: some trains keep running all night, one airport's rail link shuts down around 1am, and the arrivals hall fills with people who want to overcharge you. This page gives you the real late-night options and verified 2026 costs so you can pick a route before wheels-down.

Short version: JFK has genuine 24/7 rail. LaGuardia has a free 24/7 bus to the subway. Newark goes quiet after the last NJ Transit train, and that's when it gets expensive.

What Actually Runs Late (and What Doesn't)

JFK: the only airport with true 24/7 rail

AirTrain JFK runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and costs $8.75 per ride, tapped with any contactless card or OMNY when you exit at Jamaica or Howard Beach. From there, two subway lines run all night:

  • E train from Jamaica–Sutphin Blvd — runs 24/7, into Midtown along 53rd St.
  • A train from Howard Beach — runs 24/7, up the west side (useful for Chelsea, West Village, Harlem).

Total cost: $11.75 (AirTrain $8.75 + subway $3.00). Budget 60–90 minutes to a Manhattan hotel, because overnight trains run local and come every 15–20 minutes. The LIRR from Jamaica to Penn Station or Grand Central is faster (about 20 minutes) but runs a much thinner overnight schedule — check departure boards at Jamaica before committing.

LaGuardia: free bus, then the subway

The Q70 LaGuardia Link is free and runs 24 hours, with luggage racks, from Terminals B and C to the Jackson Heights–Roosevelt Av/74 St subway hub. Overnight, your reliable connections there are the E, F, and 7 trains (all 24/7). So LGA to Manhattan can genuinely cost $3.00 at 1am — if you're up for roughly an hour of travel with bags.

Newark: mind the ~1am cutoff

NJ Transit trains from Newark Airport to New York Penn Station cost $17.25 including the AirTrain and take about 40–45 minutes door to door — but NJ Transit is not 24/7. Service generally winds down around 1am and doesn't resume until about 5am, and exact last trains vary by day. On top of that, AirTrain Newark has been undergoing major replacement work in 2026 with periodic shuttle-bus substitutions, so check NJ Transit and Port Authority alerts the day you fly. Land at EWR after midnight and you should assume you're paying for a car.

Taxi From JFK: the Flat Fare, Honestly Itemized

The yellow-cab flat fare between JFK and Manhattan is $70, but that's not what you'll pay. Here's the late-night stack (last verified: 2026-08-26):

  • $70.00 flat fare (JFK–Manhattan, either direction)
  • $1.75 airport access fee on JFK pickups
  • $0.50 MTA state surcharge + $1.00 improvement surcharge
  • $2.50 NY State congestion surcharge (any trip south of 96th St)
  • $0.75 congestion-pricing charge (entering Manhattan below 60th St)
  • Tolls if your driver takes a tunnel (roughly $7–$12)
  • Tip (15–20% is standard)

Realistic all-in at night: $85–$105. One genuine night bonus: the $5 rush-hour surcharge only applies 4–8pm on weekdays, so late arrivals skip it. LGA and EWR taxis are metered, not flat — figure $40–$60 all-in from LGA to Midtown and $95–$120 from EWR after tolls and tip.

Rideshare at Late Arrivals: the Surge Trap

Uber and Lyft from JFK to Manhattan typically run $45–$85 for a standard car. The catch is timing: international arrival banks between 9pm and 1am dump hundreds of riders into the app at once, and surge multipliers of 1.5–2.5x are common at exactly the moment you land. Add the walk to the rideshare pickup area and a 10–15 minute wait, and the yellow-cab line — with its fixed $70 fare and no surge — often beats the app on both price and hassle at JFK. Check the app price against ~$95 all-in for the cab and take whichever wins.

From LGA, rideshare is usually $30–$55 to Manhattan off-peak; from EWR, $65–$120+ depending on surge, plus the $1.50 congestion-pricing charge if you're headed below 60th St.

The "Taxi? Taxi?" Hustle — Never Follow Anyone

At all three airports, people work the arrivals hall offering rides. Legitimate NYC taxi and rideshare drivers never solicit inside the terminal — anyone approaching you with "taxi? taxi?" or "need a car?" is an unlicensed driver, and quoted-then-inflated fares of $150+ are the standard play. Walk past, follow the official signs to the taxi stand (staffed at JFK) or the app pickup zone, and you're fine. More patterns to recognize in our guide to NYC tourist scams to avoid.

Luggage on the Subway: the Honest Reality

One carry-on and a backpack? The subway is easy. Two large checked bags? Think twice: Jamaica and Howard Beach have elevators, but many Manhattan stations still greet you with stairs, and overnight local service means more stops and more time hauling. The rule of thumb: one bag you can lift over a turnstile = train; more than that, or a party of 3+, = car, because the per-person math flips anyway. First-Night Energy: Don't Overreach

The classic late-arrival mistake is landing at 11pm and trying to salvage a "real" night out. Don't. Drop your bags, walk to one good bar within a few blocks of your hotel, have one or two drinks, and bank your energy — NYC nightlife rewards people who show up fresh on night two. If you booked smart, there's something worth walking to; if you haven't booked yet, start with where to stay for nightlife, then check what's on tonight for the block you're sleeping in. If you do end up out past 2am on arrival night, read up on safe late-night transport before you head back out.

FAQ

Is the subway from JFK safe at 1am?

The A and E run 24/7 and plenty of airport workers ride them overnight. Sit in a populated car, keep bags in front of you, and expect 15–20 minute waits. Solo with lots of luggage and feeling uneasy? The flat-fare cab is the low-stress alternative.

How much is a taxi from JFK to Manhattan at night in 2026?

$70 flat fare before extras; with surcharges, tolls, and tip, most late-night riders pay $85–$105. The $5 rush-hour surcharge doesn't apply at night.

Does the AirTrain run all night?

Yes — AirTrain JFK is 24/7 at $8.75 per ride. AirTrain Newark also runs around the clock but has had shuttle-bus substitutions during 2026 reconstruction, so check alerts before relying on it.

What's the last train from Newark Airport to Manhattan?

NJ Transit generally stops running around 1am and restarts around 5am; exact times vary by day. After that, budget $95–$140 for a car.

Should I pre-book a car for a late arrival?

Worth it for EWR arrivals after the last train and for groups of 3–4 with luggage. At JFK and LGA, the taxi line and app pickups work fine; pre-booking buys certainty more than savings.
