Pick two rooms and hit Compare — the verdict line tells you which to choose for which kind of night.

How to read the scores

Dress strictness (1–5) runs from “wear whatever” (Bossa Nova, Elsewhere, Good Room) to enforced upscale codes (Nebula and Marquee, where reports consistently describe collared shirts and dress shoes for men and refusals for athletic wear). Door difficulty (1–5) is your realistic chance of friction: a 1 means walk in (The Dead Rabbit, Public Records), a 3 means waits or ticket-timing games (Attaboy's 30–60 minute weekend queue, Pacha's headliner sellouts), and a 5 means a genuinely selective door (Marquee). Both scores are our editorial reading of the verified facts on each venue page — the pages themselves carry the sourcing.

Cover figures are the ranges each venue actually charges: ticketed rooms tier their presales, so the low end means buying early and the high end means Saturday at the door. Where a venue publishes its policy — The Back Room's “Always Free,” Le Bain's written no-cover-for-standing-room line, 230 Fifth's $15 weekend cover — we quote it directly.

How to actually decide

When two venues survive the comparison, weight the criteria by what tonight is for. Date night: dress strictness and music matter more than size — a 4/5-dress room your date didn't sign up for kills momentum at the door. Group night: door difficulty is everything; a selective door that admits six of eight people is a failed night, so favor ticketed rooms where presales guarantee entry. Music-first night: ignore the glamour scores entirely and compare music and best-night rows — a Tuesday at the “wrong” venue with the right resident beats a Saturday anywhere else.

And override the table when live conditions demand it: an unexpected closure, a weather-killed rooftop, or a line around the block beats any score. The comparison is a decision aid, not a rigid rule — keep one backup within a short ride of your first choice, ideally from the same borough column.

FAQ

Which NYC clubs have the strictest dress codes?

Marquee and Nebula — upscale attire enforced (collared shirts, dress shoes, no athletic wear or distressed sneakers, per reports) with selective doors. Le Bain runs a dress-to-impress discretionary door that gets harder late. The Bushwick rooms — House of Yes, Bossa Nova, Elsewhere — have no formal code; effort and costumes beat labels there.

Which good venues have no cover?

Attaboy, The Back Room (“Always Free” per the official site), The Dead Rabbit, and Westlight; Le Bain has no cover for standing room, and 230 Fifth is free outside its $15 Fri–Sat evening cover. You pay in $16–$22+ cocktails instead — the drinks are the cover.

Should I book in advance or decide same day?

Ticketed rooms (Pacha, Knockdown Center, Good Room, Public Records, House of Yes) sell tiered presales that beat the door — book those ahead. Cocktail bars and most rooftops stay walk-in flexible for a same-day call.

How do I reduce late-night transit risk?

Compare venues within one borough when you can, use major station corridors, avoid last-minute cross-borough jumps, and settle the end-of-night plan before midnight — the tool flags cross-borough pairs for exactly this reason.
