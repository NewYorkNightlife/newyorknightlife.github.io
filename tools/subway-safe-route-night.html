The Overnight Rules This Tool Is Built On

New York's subway genuinely runs 24 hours — one of the only systems on earth that does — but "24/7" is not the same as "same as daytime." After roughly midnight, headways stretch to about 20 minutes on most lines, and the network quietly reshapes itself: the B, C, and W don't run overnight, the Z only exists during weekday rush hours, the M shrinks to a short Brooklyn shuttle, the 3 and 5 mostly stop running, and express trains go local. The cheat sheet above only uses lines that hold up in the overnight pattern, which is why its answers sometimes differ from what a daytime map suggests. Last verified: 2026-08-26 — verify current patterns at mta.info, since these can change.

The fare side is simple: $3.00 per ride with an OMNY tap, no night surcharge, free subway-bus transfers within two hours, and a $35 cap per rolling 7-day window on the same card or device. The full economics — including when a shared rideshare beats two subway fares and 40 minutes of waiting — are in our Uber/Lyft vs. subway at night breakdown.

Three habits cover most late-night platform safety: wait near the turnstiles until your train is a few minutes out, ride the center cars where the conductor sits, and treat an empty car at 2am as a car with a reason to be empty. The rest — station-by-station judgment, the walk from your exit to your door, and solo-specific tactics — lives in our safe late-night transport guide, solo traveler safety guide, and late-night safety by borough.

One planning note this tool can't see: the L train — the single most nightlife-critical line, linking the East Village, Williamsburg, and Bushwick — is a repeat weekend track-work offender. When it's down, the J from Essex/Marcy and the G from Metropolitan–Lorimer are the standard backups, and this tool already knows both. FAQ

Does the subway really run all night?

Yes, 24/7 — but overnight trains typically come about every 20 minutes, the B/C/W don't run at all, the Z is rush-hour-only, the M becomes a Brooklyn shuttle, and express trains run local. Weekend track work can reroute whole lines.

How much does it cost at night?

Same as daytime: $3.00 per OMNY tap, free subway-bus transfers within two hours, $35 weekly cap on one card or device. No night surcharge. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

When should I switch to a car?

After 2am if the trip needs a transfer, after roughly 3am if you're solo, and whenever your line is hit by track work. A cross-borough rideshare at 3am runs about $30–$50 — split four ways, it often beats two 20-minute platform waits.

What are the safest late-night platform habits?

Wait near the turnstiles or booth until your train is close, stand back from the platform edge, ride the center cars near the conductor, pick occupied cars, and grip your phone near closing doors.
