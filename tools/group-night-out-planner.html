How the planner builds a night

Every route follows the same skeleton because it's the one that survives contact with a real group: a pregame with seats and conversation until about 10:30, one main event you've committed to, and food before the ride home. Three stops is the ceiling — every plan with five stops becomes a plan with two stops and an argument.

The venue pool is deliberately small and verified. Main venues come from our venue fact pages (all checked against official sources on 2026-08-25); food stops come from our late-night food ranking. One important status note baked in: Brooklyn Mirage no longer exists — its East Williamsburg complex relaunched as Pacha New York in June 2026, which is what the planner recommends instead.

Cost ranges include what listicles skip: 20% tips, the realistic two-drinks-per-stop pace, and $6 of subway. The low end assumes presale tickets and restraint; the high end assumes Saturday pricing and a third round. Nobody's night lands outside the range because of the venue — only because of the after-party.

Group physics: the honest part

Group size changes what's possible more than budget does. Four to six people fit anywhere. At eight, cocktail dens stop working — rooms like Attaboy or The Back Room can't seat you, which is why the planner warns you instead of pretending. At ten-plus, you need venues built for volume: big clubs with presale tickets, rooftops that take group reservations (230 Fifth books 10+ up to 30 days out), or a karaoke room that swallows twenty. And whatever the size: buy every ticket in one order, announce each move 20 minutes early, and leave on time with whoever's ready. Pricing a specific door? FAQ

What's the ideal group size for a NYC night out?

Four to six — one cab, one corner table, one guestlist entry. Above that, pick volume-friendly venues and accept the trade: more logistics, better chaos.

How do you keep a group of 10 moving?

Name the exact next address before anyone needs it, call each move 20 minutes ahead, and leave on schedule with whoever's ready. Keep stops within 15 minutes of each other. Stragglers catch up; waiting kills nights.

Do we need reservations?

Pregame: only at sit-down spots (Magic Hour reserves free with a card hold; 230 Fifth takes big groups 30 days out). Main event: buy presales for everyone in one order — never walk 10 people to a door that might be sold out. Food: never.

What does a group night actually cost per person?

About $55–$90 for a Bushwick warehouse route, $95–$140 for a Manhattan club night with a real pregame, and $170–$380+ once dinner-plus-mega-club or bottle service enters the picture — tips, one ticket, and transit included.
