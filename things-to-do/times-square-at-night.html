Times Square at Night: What’s Actually Worth It

Here’s the honest playbook: go after dark, give it 45–60 minutes, catch the billboards and the 11:57pm Midnight Moment, then walk five minutes west into Hell’s Kitchen for a real drink. Times Square at night is genuinely worth seeing once — the trick is knowing exactly what to do there and what to refuse.

This page covers the handful of things that justify the crowds, the hustles to sidestep, and how to exit into an actual good night out. If you’re building a full evening, start with the things-to-do hub or the first-time NYC nightlife guide.

Worth Doing in Times Square After Dark

The billboards and the Red Steps (free)

The spectacle itself is the attraction, and it costs nothing. Stand mid-bowtie between 44th and 47th Streets, then climb the red TKTS steps at 47th for the elevated view down Broadway. The screens only read properly in full darkness — in summer that means after roughly 9pm.

Midnight Moment, 11:57pm nightly (free)

Three minutes before midnight, every night, dozens of the big electronic billboards sync to display a single video artwork curated by Times Square Arts, with a new artist featured each month. It’s the largest ongoing public digital-art program in the world, it’s free, and almost nobody standing there knows it’s about to happen. If you’re nearby late, time your visit to it.

TKTS booth: same-day Broadway at 20–50% off

The TKTS booth under the Red Steps (Broadway and 47th St) sells same-day tickets to Broadway and Off-Broadway shows at 20–50% off face value, plus a per-ticket service fee of about $5–6. Evening-show windows generally run from 3pm on Monday, Tuesday and Friday, and from 11am most other days, until around 8pm. The best inventory is usually right when the booth opens; the shortest lines are in the last hour. Check the live board on the TKTS app or TDF site before you commit to the line. Get above it: rooftops with the Times Square view

The smartest Times Square move is looking down at it. St. Cloud Rooftop at the Knickerbocker Hotel (6 Times Square, 17th floor) is open daily 4pm–11pm and looks straight up the bowtie — cocktails are hotel-rooftop priced, but you’re paying for the view, and it’s one of the few places you can sit down in the neighborhood without regret. The Times Square EDITION (701 7th Ave) runs Paradise Club, a ticketed cabaret/party venue with weekend DJ nights — a show room rather than a walk-up bar, so book ahead. For more options with prices and door notes, see our ranking of the best rooftops in NYC.

Walk 5 minutes west to Hell’s Kitchen

Ninth Avenue between 42nd and 57th is where the neighborhood actually drinks. The anchor is Rudy’s Bar & Grill (627 Ninth Ave at 44th), a dive open since 1933: cheap beer, a free hot dog with every drink, cash-friendly, open until 4am. It’s everything the Times Square chain restaurants aren’t. Full bar-by-bar breakdown in our Hell’s Kitchen nightlife guide.

What to Skip (and Why)

  • Costumed characters. Tips are “voluntary” until you take the photo — then several characters pile into the frame and each expects cash. If you want the shot, agree on a tip with one character first. Otherwise a firm “no thanks” and forward motion ends it.
  • CD-pushers and “free” anything. Anyone placing a CD, mixtape, or bracelet in your hand will demand payment and apply loud social pressure. Never accept an object handed to you on the street. It is not rude to refuse; it’s the local default.
  • Chain restaurants on the bowtie. You’ll pay a Times Square premium for food you can get in any American mall. Walk one avenue west to Ninth Ave and eat better for less.
  • Street ticket hawkers. Anyone selling comedy-show or bus-tour tickets on the sidewalk is working on commission for products with steep in-club minimums. Buy Broadway from TKTS or the box office, nothing from a clipboard.

Best Photo Times

  • 9–11pm: full billboard glow, maximum energy, maximum crowds. Best for the classic look.
  • 11pm–1am: thinning crowds, same light. The sweet spot for clean shots — and you catch the Midnight Moment.
  • Before 8am: nearly empty pavement if you want the “alone in Times Square” frame; screens are on but daylight dulls them.
  • Rain: genuinely the best version — wet asphalt doubles every billboard as a reflection.

Late-Night Logistics and the Safety Reality

Times Square–42nd St is a 24-hour subway station with more lines than any other stop in the system. The base fare is $3.00 as of January 4, 2026, paid by tapping a contactless card or phone (OMNY) — the MetroCard is gone. After 1am, expect 10–20 minute waits between trains; the platforms stay populated, which is what you want. If you’d rather not wait, yellow cabs are easy to hail here at any hour. Full options, costs, and judgment calls in our safe late-night transport guide.

On safety: Times Square is one of the most policed, camera-covered, pedestrian-dense places in America, and it stays busy well past midnight. Violent crime against tourists is rare. The real risks are economic — hustles, inflated menus, and pickpockets working distracted crowds. Phone gripped, wallet in a front pocket, nothing accepted from strangers’ hands: that covers it.

Where Locals Actually Go

Locals treat Times Square as a transfer station, not a destination — but they drink minutes away. Hell’s Kitchen (Ninth Ave) has the dives, gay bars, and late kitchens; our neighborhood guide maps it. If you want a proper night out afterward, the Lower East Side is a 15-minute train ride with ten times the bar density. FAQ

Is Times Square safe at night?

Yes, by big-city standards. It’s heavily policed and crowded past midnight. The realistic risks are hustles — costumed characters, CD-pushers, pickpockets — not violence. Standard street awareness covers it.

What time should I go for photos?

At least an hour after sunset for full billboard glow; after 11pm on a weeknight for thinner crowds; before 8am for empty-street shots. Rainy nights photograph best of all.

What is the Midnight Moment?

A free, nightly synchronized artwork across dozens of Times Square billboards from 11:57pm to midnight, with a new artist each month. Three minutes, no ticket, most visitors miss it.

Do you have to pay the costumed characters?

No. Tips are voluntary, but expect aggressive cash demands after any photo — often from multiple characters at once. Agree a tip first or decline and keep walking.

Does the subway run all night from Times Square?

Yes, 24 hours. Base fare is $3.00 via OMNY tap-to-pay as of January 2026. Late-night waits run 10–20 minutes.
