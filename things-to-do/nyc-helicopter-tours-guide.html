NYC Helicopter Tours Guide (2026)

A 12–15 minute shared helicopter flight over Manhattan runs about $250–300 per person in 2026 from the Downtown Manhattan Heliport — before the $40–45 heliport fee most operators add at checkout — or roughly half that per seat from New Jersey if you’ll trade a 30–45 minute transfer to Kearny or Linden. Here is who is actually flying this year, what each route really costs, and the safety history you should read before you book.

Who Is Actually Flying in 2026

  • HeliNY — flying since 1994; Bell 407 fleet; sightseeing tours from the Downtown Manhattan Heliport. Watch for its $45-per-person facility surcharge added at checkout on top of headline prices.
  • Zip Aviation — Bell 407s from the Downtown Manhattan Heliport, live pilot commentary; a $40-per-person heliport fee is due at check-in. Runs New Jersey departures (including night-skyline flights) on Sundays and evenings.
  • Manhattan Helicopters — classic doors-on Manhattan tours from the Downtown Manhattan Heliport.
  • FlyNYON — the doors-off photo-flight specialist, operating from Kearny, NJ in 2026 with both doors-off and doors-on options and aggressive online promo pricing.
  • Gone: New York Helicopter Charter — the operator of the Bell 206 that crashed in the Hudson in April 2025 — shut down days after the accident under FAA pressure and is out of the tour business.

Manhattan vs. New Jersey Departures: The Big Price Fork

Downtown Manhattan Heliport (Pier 6, East River): you lift off next to the skyline with zero transfer time, and pay for the privilege — higher per-seat prices plus that $40–45 heliport/facility fee. One quirk that wrecks itineraries: the heliport is closed to sightseeing tours on Sundays by city rule, so Sunday flyers are going to New Jersey whether they planned to or not.

Kearny / Linden, NJ: per-seat prices can be less than half of Manhattan’s, and FlyNYON’s off-peak online promos ($99–149 per seat for a ~16-minute doors-off flight) all fly from here. The honest math: there’s no useful transit link, so add a rideshare of roughly $40–60 and 30–45 minutes each way from Midtown. For two or more people on promo seats the savings still win; for one person on a regular fare, the transfer eats most of the discount.

Weight Rules, Booking Windows, and Fine Print

  • Weight limits are real: operators cap passengers at roughly 275–300 lbs, and most charge a surcharge — or require a second seat — above about 250 lbs. Everyone is weighed at check-in and seats are assigned by weight to balance the aircraft; you cannot buy your way into a specific seat.
  • Book early in your trip, not on your last day. Flights cancel for wind, low cloud, and fog with no ability to fly around it. Booking your flight for day one or two gives you free rebooking room; a last-morning slot that cancels is just gone.
  • Lead time: weekday off-peak seats are often available a few days out; weekend and sunset slots sell out 1–2 weeks ahead in summer. Promo-priced FlyNYON seats go fastest.
  • ID and check-in: government ID required, arrive 60–90 minutes early for the safety briefing (FlyNYON’s harness fitting takes longest). Phones and cameras must be tethered on doors-off flights; loose items including hats stay on the ground.

Safety, Honestly

You should know two accidents before you book, because they shape every rule you’ll encounter:

  • April 10, 2025: a New York Helicopter Charter Bell 206 broke up and crashed into the Hudson during a sightseeing flight, killing all six aboard, including a family of five. The NTSB attributed the crash to a migratory bird strike; the FAA issued an emergency grounding order and the company shut down permanently.
  • March 2018: a doors-off charter sold by FlyNYON and operated by Liberty Helicopters went down in the East River; five passengers drowned when their harness tethers could not be released. That crash drove the FAA’s tighter rules on doors-off restraint systems still in force today.

The aftermath is reshaping the industry: New York City has moved to push the oldest, loudest helicopters out of the Downtown Manhattan and East 34th Street heliports starting in 2029, and City Council scrutiny of the tour industry has only intensified since the 2025 crash. Statistically, tens of thousands of tour flights operate without incident each year, and the surviving operators are the established ones with long records — but this is a real-risk activity in a way a harbor cruise is not. If that math doesn’t sit right, the skyline is spectacular from sea level too: compare the night cruises and $4.50 ferry routes or the best rooftops in NYC.

Is It Worth It? A Straight Answer

Fifteen minutes goes by fast — figure 2–3 minutes of that over the money shots. As a once-per-lifetime splurge, especially for photographers on a doors-off flight, it earns its price: nothing else gives you Central Park and the harbor in one frame. As a casual add-on at $320+ per person all-in from Manhattan, it competes badly with an observation deck plus a night cruise plus a great dinner — combined. Decide which trip you’re on before you pay. FAQ

Are NYC helicopter tours safe?

Flights are FAA-regulated and the established operators have long track records, but the risk is not zero: a sightseeing helicopter crashed in the Hudson in April 2025 (six deaths, attributed by the NTSB to a bird strike, operator shut down), and a doors-off flight crashed in the East River in 2018. If the added risk of doors-off flying bothers you, book a standard doors-on tour with an established operator, or skip the flight entirely.

Should I fly from Manhattan or New Jersey?

Manhattan (Downtown Manhattan Heliport) costs more — roughly $250–300+ per person for 12–15 minutes plus a $40–45 heliport fee — but there is no transfer. New Jersey departures (Kearny, Linden) are often half the per-seat price, and doors-off promo seats start near $99–149, but budget 30–45 minutes and about $40–60 each way to get there from Manhattan. Note the Manhattan heliport is closed to tours on Sundays.

How much does a NYC helicopter tour really cost after fees?

Take the advertised price and add the heliport or facility fee ($40–45 per person at Downtown Manhattan Heliport, often collected at check-in or checkout), a possible weight surcharge if you are over roughly 250 lbs, and a customary pilot tip. A “$259” Manhattan tour realistically lands around $320–350 per person.

Is a doors-off flight worth it?

For serious photographers, yes — no window glare and straight-down shots you cannot get any other way. For everyone else it is colder, louder, windier, and carries the harness-dependent risk profile that contributed to the 2018 East River crash. Doors-on windows are perfectly good for memories.

Do helicopters fly at night in NYC?

True night tours are rare. Some operators sell twilight or night-skyline flights, mostly from New Jersey heliports; Manhattan sightseeing slots concentrate in daylight hours. If your goal is the skyline lit up, a night cruise or a rooftop bar delivers it for a fraction of the price.
