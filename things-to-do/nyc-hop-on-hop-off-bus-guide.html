NYC Hop-On Hop-Off Bus Guide (2026): Prices, Routes, and Whether It’s Worth It

A 24-hour hop-on hop-off ticket now costs $39–$65 depending on the operator, and a full downtown loop can take three hours in traffic. Here’s who’s actually running in 2026, what tickets really cost, and an honest call on when the bus beats the subway — and when it absolutely doesn’t.

Which Operators Are Actually Running in 2026

Big Bus New York

The biggest name and the one with the most consistent service. Big Bus runs two loops in 2026 — a Downtown Loop (Red, 13 stops, roughly 9:00am–4:30pm, buses every ~30 minutes) and an Uptown Loop (Blue, 7 stops, roughly 10:00am–4:00pm, hourly). It no longer runs a Brooklyn loop. Tickets: 24-hour Discover from about $62, 48-hour Essential from about $83 (adds Uptown Loop plus the 2-hour night tour), 72-hour Explore from about $99 (adds a Circle Line cruise). The standalone 2-hour Night Tour is about $67. The whole fleet is wheelchair accessible, kids 3–12 pay a child fare, and under-3s ride free. Buses run daily except Thanksgiving and December 31.

TopView Sightseeing

The budget-to-midrange challenger with the red-and-purple buses. TopView advertises hop-on hop-off tours “from $39” online, covering downtown and uptown routes with around 23 stops, and it’s the operator that still runs a separate Brooklyn tour and a nightly skyline night tour (about $60 at full price, frequently discounted). Its bigger passes bundle a Statue of Liberty-area cruise, night tour, Brooklyn tour, and bike rental. Reviews are more mixed than Big Bus’s — frequency complaints are common in the off-season — but the entry price is genuinely lower.

Gray Line / New York Sightseeing

Complicated. Gray Line New York still sells double-decker hop-on hop-off tours through its site (newyorksightseeing.com), advertising Uptown, Downtown, Brooklyn, and Night loops with live guides. But its longtime Eighth Avenue visitor center is listed as closed, and several travel guides now report the classic Gray Line street operation as discontinued, with some hop-on hop-off sales redirected to Big Bus since 2021. If a street seller offers you a “Gray Line” ticket, verify exactly whose bus you’ll be boarding before you pay. Booking direct online is the only version of Gray Line we’d trust in 2026.

The Honest Verdict: Bus vs. Subway + Walking

As transportation, hop-on hop-off buses lose to the subway every single time. Midtown crawls at walking pace for much of the day, loops officially take 2–3 hours and stretch longer in December, and a 30–60 minute wait after hopping off means one museum stop can eat half your ticket’s value. The subway costs $2.90 a ride, caps around $34 for a week with OMNY, and doesn’t care about traffic.

As a seated orientation tour, though, the bus does something the subway can’t: it shows you how the city fits together, above ground, with commentary, while you rest your feet. The trick is to treat it as a one-day attraction — ride a full loop without hopping off much, take your photos, then switch to the subway for the rest of your trip.

It’s right for you if…

  • It’s your first visit and you want the lay of the land before committing to neighborhoods. Pair it with our first-time NYC nightlife guide for the after-dark half of the plan.
  • Mobility is limited. Big Bus’s entire NYC fleet is wheelchair accessible, and the bus spares you subway stairs — many stations still lack elevators.
  • You’re herding kids who are done walking. Under-3s ride free and the top deck is legitimately fun for them.
  • You have one jet-lagged day and want maximum landmarks with minimum decisions.

Skip it if…

  • You’re able-bodied, under time pressure, and comfortable with a subway map — you’ll see more, faster, for a tenth of the price.
  • You’re visiting in late November or December: Midtown gridlock turns loops into parking lots.
  • You planned to use it to get to dinner or a show. Loops stop by late afternoon — your night runs on the subway anyway. Budget both halves of the day with our budget planner.

Warning: The Street-Seller Hustle

The aggressive ticket hawkers around Times Square, Battery Park, and the Empire State Building are the single biggest source of hop-on hop-off complaints. Common moves: charging above online price, selling a pass for a different company than the bus they point you to, claiming attractions are “included” when they aren’t, and selling night-tour access that doesn’t exist. Buy online, from the operator’s official site, before you’re standing on the sidewalk. Full rundown of this and related hustles in our guide to NYC tourist scams to avoid.

Better Ways to See the Skyline at Night

The 2-hour night tours are the strongest thing the bus companies sell — crossing the Manhattan Bridge on an open top deck at dusk is genuinely great. But if skyline-at-night is the goal, compare these first:

  • Staten Island Ferry — free, 24/7, and the classic harbor view of Lower Manhattan and the Statue of Liberty. Unbeatable per dollar.
  • A rooftop bar — for the price of a night tour ticket you can get a cocktail and an hour of skyline instead of two hours of traffic. Start with our list of the best rooftops in NYC.
  • Brooklyn Bridge Park or the East River ferry — $4.50 gets you the skyline from the water; the Dumbo waterfront at night is free.
  • The subway itself — any train crossing the Manhattan Bridge (B/D/N/Q) gives you a 60-second skyline show for $2.90.

If you land on a bus night tour anyway, book the version bundled into a 48/72-hour Big Bus ticket rather than standalone — it’s effectively free at that point. Then plan the rest of your evening around what’s happening in NYC nightlife this weekend.

FAQ

How much does a hop-on hop-off bus cost in NYC in 2026?

Big Bus starts around $62 for 24 hours, $83 for 48 hours (with night tour), and $99 for 72 hours (with a Circle Line cruise). TopView advertises online fares from $39. Kids’ tickets are cheaper; under-3s ride Big Bus free.

Do the buses run at night?

No — the loops stop by late afternoon. Night sightseeing is a separate 2-hour non-stop tour: about $67 from Big Bus (free inside its 48/72-hour tickets) and about $60 full price from TopView, which runs one nightly.

Is hop-on hop-off worth it in New York?

For first-timers wanting a seated orientation day, travelers with limited mobility, and families with worn-out kids — yes, as a one-day attraction. As day-to-day transportation, no; the subway is faster, runs all night, and costs a fraction of the price.

How long does a full loop take?

Plan on 2–3 hours for the downtown loop without hopping off, longer in holiday traffic. With buses every 30–60 minutes, each hop-off realistically costs you an hour-plus of ticket time.

Should I buy from a street seller?

No. Buy online from the official operator. Street sellers routinely overcharge and misrepresent what’s included — details in our tourist scams guide.
