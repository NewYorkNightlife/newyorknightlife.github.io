Williamsburg Nightlife: The Music Rooms, the Rooftops, and What They Cost in 2026

Four mid-size music venues inside a ten-minute walk, a 22nd-floor rooftop bar with no cover, a honky-tonk open until 4am, and a bar that hands you a free pizza with every drink. Here is what is actually open in Williamsburg this year — including the flagship that closes in December — and which famous names on other lists shut in 2025.

Williamsburg is the best live-music neighborhood in New York, and it is smaller than it was two years ago. Both halves of that sentence matter. Brooklyn Bowl, Music Hall of Williamsburg, National Sawdust and Baby's All Right are all inside a walkable box, and no other neighborhood in the city gives you that much stage capacity on foot. But Williamsburg also lost Black Flamingo, TBA and Macri Park between March 2025 and January 2026, and Music Hall of Williamsburg itself closes at the end of this year. A guide that has not been checked since 2024 will send you to at least one door that no longer opens.

The rough geography: North 3rd to North 12th between Kent and Bedford is the music-and-rooftop corridor. Metropolitan Avenue from Berry east to Lorimer is the cheap, late, unpretentious strip. Union Avenue and Meeker hold the good restaurants and the neighborhood bars. Anything described as a "Williamsburg mega-club" is actually in East Williamsburg or Bushwick, which is a real subway ride, not a walk. For the wider map, see the Brooklyn nightlife by zone guide, neighboring Bushwick and Greenpoint, or the neighborhoods hub.

The music rooms, and the one that closes in December

This is the reason to come to Williamsburg rather than anywhere else in Brooklyn, and it is also the part of the neighborhood changing fastest.

Music Hall of Williamsburg (66 North 6th St) is the flagship, a 650-capacity room that has anchored the corridor since 2007 — and it is closing. The owners of the building declined to renew the lease, the closure was announced in December 2025, and Bowery Presents will run shows through the end of 2026. It is fully operating right now, with a calendar listing dates into November. Go while you can; just do not plan a 2027 trip around it, and treat any list that does not carry this caveat as unchecked.

Baby's All Right (146 Broadway) is the small room that matters — the one where you see a band eighteen months before they play a theater. Its calendar carries around ninety-five upcoming shows at the time of writing, which is a healthier booking pipeline than almost anything else in the borough.

Brooklyn Bowl (61 Wythe Ave) is the concert-plus-bowling hybrid, and it is one of the few venues in the neighborhood that publishes hard numbers. Lanes are $30 per lane per half-hour Monday to Thursday and $35 Friday to Sunday, for up to eight guests, plus $5.95 for shoe rental. The important operational detail most guides miss: it is closed Monday through Wednesday, opening Thursday and Friday at 6pm and noon on Saturday and Sunday. Its own age policy reads "usually 21+ after 6pm. All ages are welcome to join us during the daytime on weekends." Concert tickets are sold through Ticketmaster and no prices are published.

National Sawdust (80 North 6th St) is the strangest and best of them — a nonprofit new-music room in a converted sawdust factory, programming contemporary classical, experimental and jazz rather than indie rock. Tickets are per-event through its own box office; nothing is published site-wide, so check the specific show. More across the city in our NYC live music guide.

Bars: free music, free pizza, and 4am

Williamsburg's bar scene rewards people who ignore the rooftop lists and walk east.

Radegast Hall & Biergarten (113 North 3rd St) is the best-value night in the neighborhood on paper: its own site advertises live music five days a week with no cover, in a beer hall with a retractable roof and long communal tables. Tuesday is trivia at 7:30pm. It is loud, it is not intimate, and that is the point.

Alligator Lounge (600 Metropolitan Ave) does the single most famous trick in Brooklyn: a free personal wood-fired pizza with every drink you buy. NYC Tourism's listing puts the pizza service at 6pm to 2am; the bar itself runs Monday to Friday 3pm–4am and weekends from 1pm, with karaoke from 10pm Wednesday through Sunday (11pm on Saturdays). It is a dive, the pizza is genuinely fine, and the economics are unbeatable.

Skinny Dennis (152 Metropolitan Ave) has been the city's honky-tonk since February 2013 — live country, bluegrass and western swing seven nights a week, open daily noon to 4am, with a frozen coffee drink that has its own following. Union Pool (Union Avenue at Meeker) runs Monday to Thursday 4pm–2am, Friday to 4am, Saturday 2pm–4am and Sunday 2pm–2am, takes no reservations, and has the backyard that made it famous.

Maison Premiere (298 Bedford Ave) is the absinthe-and-oyster bar that every Williamsburg list opens with, and it comes with a correction worth more than the recommendation: its oyster happy hour is not currently offered. That is stated on the venue's own site. Dozens of guides still promise you dollar oysters here. The bar runs Monday to Thursday to 1am, Friday and Saturday to 2am, with oysters served until roughly half an hour before close. It is still excellent; it is just no longer the cheap trick it was.

Hotel Delmano (82 Berry St) is the quieter cocktail room — marble bar, no sign, a hundred-bottle wine list and a raw bar, open Monday to Thursday 4pm–1am, Friday to 2am, Saturday from 2pm to 2am and Sunday 2pm–1am. Night of Joy is the other correction on this page: it is at 667 Lorimer Street, not Grand Street, where several guides put it. It opens at 5pm Sunday and Tuesday to Thursday until 2am, runs to 4am Friday and Saturday, closes Mondays, and has a seasonal roof patio.

Rooftops and dinner

Westlight sits on the 22nd floor of The William Vale at 111 North 12th Street and is the rooftop worth the elevator queue: a wraparound indoor-outdoor room with a full Manhattan panorama, cocktails and small plates from the NoHo Hospitality group. There is no cover. Two policies to know: reservations run through the venue's own booking system and sunset slots disappear, and under-21s are admitted only until 6pm, after which it is 21+. Hours are Monday to Thursday 4pm–midnight, Friday 4pm–2am, Saturday noon–2am and Sunday noon–midnight. Our Westlight venue facts page has the door detail. For the rest of the city, see NYC rooftop bars.

Laser Wolf is the other rooftop, on the roof of The Hoxton at 97 Wythe Avenue — an Israeli skewer house cooking over live coals with the same skyline in front of it. It takes Resy bookings for dinner. Neither rooftop publishes drink prices.

On the dinner side, the useful thing to know is that Williamsburg's best restaurants close early, which changes how you sequence a night. Lilia (567 Union Ave) serves Monday to Thursday 5pm–9:30pm and Friday to Sunday from 4pm, and its sibling Misi (329 Kent Ave) keeps the same 9:30pm finish. Both are Resy-only and both are genuinely hard to book. St. Anselm (355 Metropolitan Ave) runs later — to 11pm every night — and, crucially, accepts walk-ins at the bar on a first-come basis, which makes it the practical choice when you did not plan ahead. Sunday in Brooklyn (348 Wythe Ave) serves dinner 5pm–10pm daily. Plan dinner at 6pm and music at 9pm, not the reverse.

Nitehawk Cinema (136 Metropolitan Ave) is the alternative evening: a dine-in cinema with table service that screens, in its own words, films 365 days a year. Ticket prices are not published on the pages we could reach.

The clubs are not in Williamsburg

If you came for a dance floor, you are going to leave the neighborhood, and it is better to know that before 1am than after.

Pacha New York (140 Stewart Ave, East Williamsburg) is the big one — the rebuilt site of the old Brooklyn Mirage, relaunched under the Ibiza Pacha brand in June 2026. General admission is listed on the official site at $50–130 depending on the headliner, with backstage packages far above that. Two caveats: the open-air main stage is seasonal, running roughly June to October, with the indoor Great Hall year-round, and the venue is a fifteen-minute walk from the L at Jefferson Street or Morgan Avenue through a dark industrial stretch. Plan that leg.

Elsewhere (599 Johnson Ave) is in Bushwick, not Williamsburg, whatever the listicles say — a multi-room venue with a hall, two smaller rooms and a summer rooftop, tickets typically $15–40, running to 4am on Fridays and Saturdays. Bossa Nova Civic Club (1271 Myrtle Ave, Bushwick) is the small, sweaty, cheap techno room under the M train; note that its old .com domain now redirects to an unrelated sports-streaming site, so find it through Resident Advisor or Instagram rather than a search result. The Sultan Room (234 Starr St, Bushwick) is the mid-size alternative, with a live calendar running most nights. Further out, Public Records (233 Butler St) is in Gowanus — a genuinely different trip.

One venue we are deliberately not recommending: Good Room in Greenpoint. Its official domain currently fails with an expired security certificate, its public calendar shows only two events across the next four months, and a major listings site still carries an old "currently closed" note. We could not establish that it is operating normally, so we are flagging it rather than listing it. Call before you go. See the Greenpoint guide for alternatives.

The honest downsides

  • Williamsburg has no nightclub. Not one. Every "Williamsburg club" on a listicle is either a music venue with a DJ night or is physically in Bushwick or East Williamsburg. If a 3am dance floor is the plan, budget for the ride and read the Bushwick guide instead.
  • The venue list is shrinking. Black Flamingo closed in March 2025, TBA Brooklyn at the end of that same month, Macri Park in January 2026, Club Lambda in February 2026, and Music Hall of Williamsburg goes at the end of this year. Operators have publicly blamed rent, insurance and margins. This is not nostalgia — it changes what is actually bookable.
  • The good restaurants shut at 9:30pm. Lilia and Misi both stop serving at 9:30. If you plan dinner at 8 and a show at 10, you will be rushed at both. St. Anselm to 11pm with walk-in bar seats is the fix.
  • Bedford Avenue on a Saturday is a crush. The L station handled nearly 8.8 million riders in 2024, making it the busiest in Brooklyn outside Downtown Brooklyn, and a good share of them arrive between 9pm and midnight on weekends. Tuesday through Thursday is the same neighborhood with room to stand.
  • Almost nothing here publishes prices. Brooklyn Bowl's lanes and Pacha's tickets are the exceptions. No music venue in Williamsburg lists standard ticket prices on its own site, and neither rooftop posts a drink menu with numbers. Any specific figure you find elsewhere is a third-party estimate.
  • East Williamsburg is a different place. The walk from the L at Morgan or Jefferson to Stewart Avenue is fifteen minutes of warehouses with little foot traffic. The L runs all night, which helps at 4am, but the last stretch is worth walking as a group or splitting a car for.

Recently closed: how to spot a stale Williamsburg guide

North Brooklyn lost an unusual number of nightlife rooms between 2024 and 2026. If a guide lists any of these as current, it has not been checked this year — discount the rest of it accordingly.

  • Brooklyn Mirage (140 Stewart Ave) no longer exists under that name. Avant Gardner, its operator, filed for Chapter 11 in August 2025 after the city pulled its temporary certificate of occupancy and the 2025 outdoor season was cancelled; the structure was demolished in early 2026 and the site relaunched as Pacha New York in June 2026. Anything sold as a "Brooklyn Mirage ticket" is not real. Our Brooklyn Mirage page has the full history.
  • Music Hall of Williamsburg (66 N 6th St) closes at the end of 2026 after the building's owners declined to renew the lease. Open now; gone in a few months.
  • Macri Park (462 Union Ave), a long-running LGBTQ+ bar, closed unexpectedly in mid-January 2026.
  • Black Flamingo in South Williamsburg closed on 1 March 2025 after about ten years, and TBA Brooklyn closed at the end of that month after roughly twelve. Both cited rising costs and insurance.
  • Club Lambda (1031 Grand St, East Williamsburg), a queer-POC space opened in 2022, closed permanently at the end of February 2026.
  • Paragon in Bushwick closed in April 2025, and Brooklyn Made closed in February 2025. Saint Vitus shut its Greenpoint room in early 2024 and is targeting a Bushwick reopening at 428 Troutman Street — so "closed" and "reopening" are both partly true, and neither is a place you can walk into today without checking.
  • Maison Premiere's oyster happy hour is not currently offered. Not a closure, but the most-repeated stale price claim in the neighborhood. If a guide promises you cheap oysters there, it copied that from an old post.
  • Night of Joy is at 667 Lorimer Street, not Grand Street. A surprising number of lists have the wrong address.
  • Elsewhere in the city, the same rot applies: Up&Down in Meatpacking is closed, The Press Lounge now operates as Hudson VU, and Therapy, Copacabana Times Square, Fort Defiance, Port Morris Distillery, Minton's Playhouse and Barbetta are all shut. Across the river, Rockwood Music Hall closed in November 2024 — see our Lower East Side guide.

FAQ

What is Williamsburg known for at night?

Live music and range. Williamsburg has more mid-size music rooms in walking distance than any other New York neighborhood — Brooklyn Bowl on Wythe, Music Hall of Williamsburg and National Sawdust both on North 6th, Baby's All Right on Broadway — plus a 22nd-floor rooftop bar at Westlight, a genuine honky-tonk at Skinny Dennis, and a bar that gives you a free pizza with every drink. What it does not have is a big club; for that you cross into East Williamsburg or Bushwick.

How much does a night out in Williamsburg cost?

A bar night runs roughly $50–90 per person with no covers anywhere — Radegast Hall puts on live music five nights a week and charges nothing, and Alligator Lounge includes a personal pizza with each drink. A show night adds the ticket. Bowling at Brooklyn Bowl is published at $30 per lane per half-hour Monday to Thursday and $35 Friday to Sunday for up to eight people, plus $5.95 shoe rental. A big-room club night is the expensive one: Pacha New York lists general admission at $50–130 depending on the headliner.

Is Music Hall of Williamsburg closing?

Yes. The owners of 66 North 6th Street declined to renew the lease, and Music Hall of Williamsburg — a 650-capacity room that has been the neighborhood's flagship since 2007 — will close at the end of 2026. It is open and booking shows now, with dates listed into November 2026, so you can still see something there. Just do not build a 2027 trip around it, and treat any guide that does not mention the closure as out of date.

How do you get to and from Williamsburg late at night?

The L train is the answer and it runs 24 hours. Bedford Avenue is the first stop in Brooklyn after First Avenue in Manhattan, and it is the busiest subway station in Brooklyn outside Downtown Brooklyn — nearly 8.8 million riders in 2024. The J, M and Z at Marcy Avenue cover the south side. The one genuinely awkward trip is East Williamsburg: Pacha New York on Stewart Avenue is a fifteen-minute walk from the L at Jefferson Street or Morgan Avenue through a dark industrial stretch, so plan that leg before you are tired.

Is Brooklyn Mirage still open?

No — not under that name. Avant Gardner, which operated the Brooklyn Mirage at 140 Stewart Avenue, filed for Chapter 11 in August 2025, and the open-air structure was demolished in early 2026. FIVE Holdings, owner of the Ibiza Pacha brand, rebuilt the site and relaunched it as Pacha New York in June 2026. Anything sold as a "Brooklyn Mirage ticket" is not real. The open-air season runs roughly June to October; the indoor Great Hall runs year-round.
