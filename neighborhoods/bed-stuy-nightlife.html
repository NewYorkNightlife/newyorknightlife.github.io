Bed-Stuy Nightlife: Live Music Every Night, Prices You Can Actually Check

A jazz room that puts a band on stage 365 nights a year and asks for a $10 cash donation instead of a cover. A fried chicken counter that posts every price. And five bars that closed since 2025, one of them in a fire — all still sitting on other people's "best of Bed-Stuy" lists. Here is what is genuinely open in 2026.

Bed-Stuy is the largest neighborhood on this site and the one where a stale guide does the most damage. In the last eighteen months it lost Dynaco to a fire, Bed-Vyne Brew to enforcement pressure, Lovers Rock, Basquiat's Bottle and The Wilky outright — and one widely circulated bar roundup still lists three of those as open. What is left is not thin, but it is different from what the listicles describe.

The geography is four corridors, not one strip. Tompkins Avenue and Lewis Avenue carry the newer restaurants and the cantinas. Fulton Street holds the late bars and the two rooms with actual stages, and it is where Bed-Stuy blurs into Clinton Hill. Bedford Avenue has the dives. Malcolm X Boulevard is the dinner run. Nothing here is a nightclub; the biggest stage in the neighborhood fits a jazz trio. For the wider map see the Brooklyn nightlife by zone guide, neighbouring Crown Heights and Bushwick, or the neighborhoods hub.

The music rooms, and the $10 rule

Bar LunÀtico (486 Halsey St) is the single best reason to spend an evening in Bed-Stuy, and it is also the clearest example of how this neighborhood prices things. It is open nightly 5pm to 1am, with happy hour 5–7pm, dinner 6–11pm, and live music starting at 9pm every night — jazz, Latin, folk, a rotating cast of working musicians. There is no cover. In its place the bar states plainly that the musicians are supported through donations and recommends $10 per person, in cash. Bring cash. That is not a suggestion the venue makes lightly and it is the difference between the band being paid and not.

C'mon Everybody (325 Franklin Ave, on the Clinton Hill line) is the other real venue: a queer-owned bar with a stage and a full cocktail list, open from 5pm seven nights a week and running shows and DJ nights most of them. Tickets are sold per event and they are cheap by New York standards — one on sale when we checked was priced at about $13. There is no standard price published, so check the specific show.

Sisters (900 Fulton St) sits just over the Clinton Hill line and runs a small back room with a stage and DJ booth. Its calendar is populated well into autumn 2026 and it advertises DJ nights at 9pm with no cover, which makes it the cheapest live-music option on this page. One practical note: only the www version of its web address works — the bare domain fails a certificate check — so a link without the www will look broken.

What Bed-Stuy does not have is a club. There is no late dance venue here in the Bushwick sense, and the neighborhood's one former rooftop option, Our Wicked Lady, closed permanently in July 2025. If a 3am dance floor is the plan, read the Bushwick guide instead. More stages across the city in our NYC live music guide.

Bars: dives, cocktails and one that came back

Do or Dive (1108 Bedford Ave) is the neighborhood dive of record — no prices published anywhere, which is exactly on brand. Its own site is a little stale, still headlining an old events season, so treat any hours you find as approximate and call ahead if the timing is tight. The Coyote Club (417 Throop Ave) is the other one: cash only, beer-and-shot specials, pinball, and no working website at all. Several guides put it at the wrong address; 417 Throop is the correct one.

Cocktail Bedstuy (305 Halsey St) is the cocktail room, and it needs a name correction more than a review: it used to be Bed-Vyne Cocktail, and it changed ownership and direction back in 2021. Any list still calling it Bed-Vyne Cocktail has not looked since. It publishes its own hours — open from 4pm Monday to Thursday and from 3pm Friday to Sunday — with happy hour 4–7pm midweek and 3–6pm at weekends.

Dolores (397 Tompkins Ave) is a Mexico City–style cantina from the Winona's team, open Wednesday to Sunday from 4pm to midnight. Opening coverage reported cocktails at around $16 and food from $5 to $25; that is a 2025 figure from launch write-ups rather than a current menu, so treat it as an order of magnitude. Doris (1088 Fulton St) is the backyard bar, and it is a good-news story: it reopened on 30 June 2026 after a robbery forced a closure, and has been running DJ nights since. Its own domain is not usable for planning — it currently serves only a merchandise page under the owners' company name — so use its Instagram for hours and events.

And one more that cuts the other way. Chilo's (323 Franklin Ave) closed on 30 September 2025 after ten years, which was widely reported — and then reopened in December 2025, which was reported much less. If you read that Chilo's is gone, that article is from the six-week window when it was true.

Dinner, and the venues that post their prices

Bed-Stuy is unusually good at publishing numbers, which is rare enough in New York to be the main reason to eat here rather than one neighborhood over.

Peaches HotHouse (415 Tompkins Ave) posts a full menu: fried chicken, shrimp and catfish at $17–18, sandwiches at $15, small plates around $10–11 and sides at $3–7, with happy hour 4–7pm on weekdays. It runs 11am to 10pm Sunday to Thursday and to 11pm Friday and Saturday. One operational note straight from the venue: it cannot process cards without a working chip.

Chez Oskar (310 Malcolm X Blvd) is the other one with real numbers, and it also needs an address correction — a lot of guides still list it at 211 Lewis Avenue, which it left years ago. The kitchen runs 11am to 10pm Sunday to Thursday with last call at 10:30, and to 10:30 with last call at 11pm on Friday and Saturday. Its published deal is all-you-can-eat mussels for $28 on Tuesdays between 5pm and 8pm.

L'Antagoniste (238 Malcolm X Blvd) is the French room — dinner 5:30–9:30pm Sunday to Thursday and to 10pm Friday and Saturday, Resy-driven, no prices published. Saraghina is the neighborhood institution and comes with the most common address error on any Bed-Stuy list: the restaurant is at 350 Lewis Avenue, open 5–10pm on weekdays and from 10am at weekends. The 435 Halsey Street address that circulates was the original pizzeria and later Saraghina Tapas, which is closed; the bakery at 433 Halsey is a daytime business.

Badaboom (421 Bainbridge St) is the newer French rotisserie, dinner-only roughly Wednesday to Sunday, with opening coverage reporting starters at $10–20 and mains at $25–45. Over in Clinton Hill, Otway (930 Fulton St) serves dinner Wednesday and Thursday 5–10pm, Friday and Saturday 4–10pm and Sunday 4–9pm, and The Fly (549 Classon Ave) — rotisserie chicken and natural wine from the Hart's team — runs 5:30 to 11pm Sunday to Thursday and to midnight Friday and Saturday.

Two places that are not bars but are genuinely part of the evening. Gladys Books & Wine (306 Malcolm X Blvd) opened in September 2025 as a bookstore upstairs with a gathering space below, built as a home for Black queer women, open to 10pm midweek and midnight Friday and Saturday — note that its wine service was still pending a licence at the time of the coverage we could verify, so check before you plan around drinks. Bed-Stuy Art House (198a Lewis Ave) is a small rentable art and event space rather than a nightly venue, and its programming is calendar-by-calendar. Its website currently fails a certificate check, so use its Instagram.

Getting home: the G, and why Bed-Stuy is luckier than Greenpoint

Bed-Stuy sits on the same G train that makes Greenpoint fragile, but it has something Greenpoint does not: alternatives.

The G runs 24 hours through Bedford–Nostrand Avenues, and it runs short trains that stop mid-platform — the single most useful thing to know if you have not used it before is to stand in the middle of the platform. The line has been through a long signal-modernisation programme: it was shut in phases from late June to early September 2024 for communications-based train control installation, resumed full service on 3 September 2024, and has continued to generate weekend and segment outages through 2026, with further shutdowns planned for December 2026 that local officials and business owners publicly protested in August 2026. Check before you rely on it on a weekend night.

The backups are the reason this matters less here. On Fulton Street — Clinton–Washington, Franklin Avenue, Nostrand Avenue, Kingston–Throop — the C is a daytime-only service and stops overnight, but the A takes over and makes all the local stops, so those stations keep 24-hour coverage on a less frequent train. On Broadway — Myrtle, Kosciuszko, Gates, Halsey — the J runs 24 hours and stops everywhere overnight. The Z is rush-hour peak-direction only and never runs at night or weekends. The M needs the precise version rather than the usual shorthand: it technically operates overnight, but only as a shuttle between Metropolitan Avenue in Queens and Myrtle Avenue–Broadway — it does not run its normal route and does not reach Manhattan. Overnight on Broadway, take the J.

The honest downsides

  • The closure run has been brutal. Five bars gone since 2025, one of them to a fire. A widely circulated Bed-Stuy bar roundup still lists three closed venues as current. Assume any inherited list is wrong until checked.
  • No club, no big room. The largest stage in Bed-Stuy fits a jazz trio. Our Wicked Lady, the nearest rooftop venue, closed permanently in July 2025.
  • Addresses drift here more than anywhere. Chez Oskar is at 310 Malcolm X Boulevard, not 211 Lewis Avenue. Saraghina's restaurant is at 350 Lewis Avenue, not 435 Halsey Street. The Coyote Club is at 417 Throop Avenue. Bed-Stuy Art House is at 198a Lewis Avenue. Every one of those is wrong on lists we checked.
  • Websites are unreliable in both directions. Doris is open and its domain serves a merch page; Lovers Rock is closed and its domain now serves an unrelated content-farm magazine. Neither tells you anything about whether the door is open.
  • Bring cash. The Coyote Club is cash only, Bar LunÀtico asks for cash donations for the band, and Peaches HotHouse warns it cannot take chipless cards.
  • The corridors are far apart. Bed-Stuy is big. Malcolm X Boulevard to Fulton Street is a real walk, not a hop. Pick one corridor a night.
  • The G is still under construction. More outages are planned for December 2026. The A and the J are the fallbacks; know which one you need before you start drinking.

Recently closed: how to spot a stale Bed-Stuy guide

This is the section that matters most on this page. If a guide lists any of the following as current, it has not been checked this year.

  • Dynaco (1112 Bedford Ave) was destroyed by a multi-alarm fire on 10 January 2026. The building was vacate-ordered and the bar has not reopened; a fundraiser toward the rebuild was still running months later. It was a beloved room and it is not there. This is the highest-stakes error on any current Bed-Stuy list.
  • Bed-Vyne Brew (370 Tompkins Ave) closed on 31 May 2025 after twelve years, following police and liquor-authority pressure. Note that the related Bed-Vyne wine shop is a separate, still-operating business — the brew bar is the one that closed.
  • Lovers Rock (419 Tompkins Ave) closed in 2025; its own Instagram bio now reads "Established 2015, Closed 2025." Its former web domain still resolves, but it now serves an unrelated general-interest content site rather than anything to do with the bar — a particularly effective trap for anyone checking by search result.
  • Basquiat's Bottle (1198 Fulton St — not the Nostrand Avenue address several lists give) and The Wilky (108 Patchen Ave) are both marked permanently closed. We could not pin exact dates for either, so we are stating that limitation rather than inventing one.
  • Our Wicked Lady (153 Morgan Ave, East Williamsburg) closed permanently on 21 July 2025, rooftop included. The name survives as a booking brand presenting shows at other venues, which is why it still appears on event listings.
  • Saraghina Tapas at 435 Halsey Street is closed. The Saraghina restaurant at 350 Lewis Avenue and the bakery at 433 Halsey are open — three addresses, one name, and most guides pick the wrong one.
  • Hunky Dory (747 Franklin Ave) closed back in October 2021, and the bar that replaced it, Winner on Franklin, has since closed too. Both still circulate.
  • "The Billionaire Room" does not exist. We could find no evidence that a Brooklyn business by that name has ever operated. If it appears on a Bed-Stuy list, that list was not checked.
  • Elsewhere in the city, the same rot applies: Brooklyn Mirage no longer exists under that name — the site relaunched as Pacha New York in June 2026 — and Up&Down, Therapy, Copacabana Times Square, Fort Defiance, Port Morris Distillery, Faro, Barbetta, Rockwood Music Hall, Pig and Khao, Paris Blues, The Rookery and Ippudo Westside are all shut. The Press Lounge now operates as Hudson VU, and Music Hall of Williamsburg closes at the end of 2026. Minton's Playhouse in Harlem is status-contested — we will not call it open or permanently closed until that resolves.

FAQ

Where can you see live music in Bed-Stuy?

Bar LunÀtico at 486 Halsey Street is the answer, and it is one of the best-value music rooms in New York. It is open nightly 5pm to 1am with shows starting at 9pm every night, and instead of a cover it asks for a donation to the musicians — its own site recommends $10 per person and asks you to bring cash. C'mon Everybody at 325 Franklin Avenue is the other real venue, a queer-owned bar with a stage running shows most nights; tickets are per-show and modest, with one on sale at about $13 when we checked. Sisters at 900 Fulton Street runs DJs at 9pm with no cover.

Which Bed-Stuy bars have closed recently?

More than any other Brooklyn neighborhood in this stretch. Dynaco at 1112 Bedford Avenue was destroyed by a multi-alarm fire on 10 January 2026 and has not reopened; a fundraiser for the rebuild is still running. Bed-Vyne Brew closed on 31 May 2025 after twelve years. Lovers Rock on Tompkins Avenue closed in 2025 — its own Instagram bio now reads Established 2015, Closed 2025. Basquiat's Bottle on Fulton Street and The Wilky on Patchen Avenue are both marked permanently closed. Nearby, Our Wicked Lady in East Williamsburg closed permanently on 21 July 2025, taking its rooftop with it. Chilo's is the exception that goes the other way: it closed on 30 September 2025 and reopened that December.

How much does a night out in Bed-Stuy cost?

Bed-Stuy is one of the few neighborhoods where several venues publish real numbers. Bar LunÀtico recommends a $10 cash donation per person for the musicians rather than charging a cover. Peaches HotHouse lists fried chicken, shrimp and catfish at $17 to $18, sandwiches at $15 and sides at $3 to $7, with happy hour 4pm to 7pm on weekdays. Chez Oskar advertises all-you-can-eat mussels for $28 on Tuesdays between 5pm and 8pm. Add drinks and a bar night lands around $45 to $85 per person; a dinner-led evening runs $70 to $130.

Is the G train a problem for a night out in Bed-Stuy?

Less than in Greenpoint, because Bed-Stuy has alternatives. The G runs 24 hours through Bedford–Nostrand Avenues but in short trains that stop mid-platform, so stand in the middle. It has also been repeatedly disrupted by the signal-modernisation programme that shut it down for ten weeks in summer 2024 and continued producing weekend outages through 2026, with more planned for December 2026. The backup is what saves you: the A runs 24 hours on Fulton Street, making all local stops overnight when the C stops running, and the J runs 24 hours on Broadway.

What is Bed-Stuy nightlife actually like?

Neighborhood bars and restaurants rather than clubs, spread across four corridors instead of concentrated on one strip. Tompkins Avenue and Lewis Avenue carry the newer restaurants, Fulton Street holds the late bars and the music rooms, Bedford Avenue has the dives, and Malcolm X Boulevard has the dinner rooms. There is no nightclub and no big venue — the closest thing to a stage is Bar LunÀtico's, which seats a jazz trio. What Bed-Stuy does better than its neighbours is price: several venues publish what they charge, and the covers are either small or absent.
