SoHo Nightlife: Great Until Midnight, Then Everyone Leaves

SoHo is a retail and dining district that happens to contain a few excellent bars — not a nightlife district. Here is what is actually open in 2026, what the menus charge, which "SoHo" venues are not in SoHo at all, and where to go when the neighborhood goes quiet.

The honest summary of SoHo after dark is that it is the best first-half-of-the-night neighborhood in downtown Manhattan and one of the worst second-half neighborhoods. The bars here are genuinely good — two hotel rooftops, a set of cocktail rooms with published menus in the low twenties, three bars old enough to predate the Civil War era of the cast-iron district — but they are embedded in a shopping district. When the stores shut and the restaurant covers turn over, the sidewalks empty fast. That is not a criticism, it is a scheduling fact, and planning around it is the difference between a good SoHo night and standing on Prince Street at 1am wondering where everyone went.

The geography matters more here than in most neighborhoods, because a large share of the venues that appear on "SoHo nightlife" lists are not in SoHo. SoHo proper runs from West Houston Street down to Canal Street, between Sixth Avenue and Lafayette Street. East of Lafayette is Nolita. West of Sixth Avenue and Varick is Hudson Square. North of Houston is NoHo and then the West Village. Those edges hold most of the genuinely late rooms, so the practical route is usually SoHo for dinner and drinks, then a short walk east or west for anything after midnight. If you want a neighborhood that stays busy on its own until 4am, you want the Lower East Side or the East Village; the full picture is in our Manhattan nightlife by zone guide and the neighborhoods hub.

The cocktail rooms

SoHo's drinking reputation rests less on volume than on a handful of rooms that take the drinks seriously and charge accordingly. Two are worth building a night around.

Temple Bar (332 Lafayette St) is the one with the history. The original closed in 2017; the room was revived in late 2021 and is now the most atmospheric dark-lacquer cocktail bar within walking distance of SoHo. Its own Spring/Summer 2026 drink list prices shaken and citrus cocktails at $22, martinis and stirred drinks at $23, "minis" at $13 and zero-proof cocktails at $15; beer runs $8–13 and wine by the glass starts at $20. Hours are Monday to Wednesday 5pm–1am and Thursday to Saturday 5pm–2am — and at the time of writing the venue's own site lists Sunday as closed for the summer, which is exactly the kind of detail that ruins a Sunday plan. Reservations go through Resy. One honest caveat for the address-conscious: Temple Bar is in NoHo, not SoHo.

The Crosby Bar at the Crosby Street Hotel (79 Crosby St) is the most reliable drink inside SoHo's actual boundaries, and it is not a rooftop despite frequently being described as one — it is a ground-floor bar with a courtyard garden. The bar runs 9am–1am daily and the restaurant 7am–11pm. Signature and sparkling cocktails are listed at $23, zero-proof cocktails with spirit alternatives at $20, and non-alcoholic cocktails at $13. There is a live caveat worth knowing before you book dinner: the hotel's own page states that from 7 July to 20 September 2026 it is serving reduced menus due to kitchen renovations. Drinks are unaffected; the food offer is not what it normally is.

For wine specifically, the nearest serious option sits on the Nolita seam at 249 Centre Street, where the natural-wine bar long known as La Compagnie des Vins Surnaturels now trades as La Compagnie Centre St under the Experimental Group. It runs 5pm–midnight Monday to Wednesday, to 1am Thursday and Friday, 3pm–1am Saturday and 3–10pm Sunday. It publishes no prices, so we are not quoting any.

The three old bars

Strip out the hotels and the restaurants and SoHo still has three bars that have been serving since long before anyone used the word SoHo. They are the cheapest and the latest rooms in the area, and they are the reason the neighborhood is not a total write-off at 2am.

Fanelli Cafe (94 Prince St, at Mercer) has occupied its corner since 1847 and is routinely described as the second-oldest continuously operating food-and-drink address in the city. Its own site publishes hours only as "open 11am–late," which is charmingly useless for planning, and third-party listings disagree with each other on the closing time — assume midnight midweek and later on weekends, and do not build a 3am plan around it. The kitchen prices are published: a burger and fries is $24, steak frites $48. Drink prices are not published anywhere, so we are not quoting them.

The Ear Inn (326 Spring St) claims 1817 and is open noon to 4am, seven days — one of the few reliably late rooms on this side of downtown. It runs live music, including a long-standing Sunday-evening jazz session. It is technically in Hudson Square rather than SoHo, which is a ten-minute walk west and, at 3am, a meaningful one. Food is priced on the site (burger $20, NY strip $36); drinks are not.

Milano's (51 E Houston St) is the narrow, loud, cash-friendly holdout on Houston, open 8am–4am Monday to Saturday and 10am–4am Sunday. Its own site files it under NoHo; other listings call it Nolita; the block is genuinely a seam. No prices are published, and we are not inventing any.

Hotel bars and rooftops

SoHo's hotels do a lot of the neighborhood's nightlife heavy lifting, which is why the rooftop question comes up so often. Two are worth naming, and both come with dress codes and weekend queues.

JIMMY at ModernHaus SoHo is the eighteenth-floor rooftop at 15 Thompson Street (the hotel's own mailing address is 27 Grand Street — same building, and a lot of listings put JIMMY on the wrong street entirely). Hours are Monday, Tuesday and Sunday 5pm–midnight, Wednesday to 1am, Thursday and Friday to 2am, and Saturday 3pm–2am. The published cocktail list runs $23–26, beer $11 and wine by the glass $20–45. The venue describes itself as "upscale but casual" and reserves door discretion when it is full, which in practice means weekends are a queue.

A.R.T. SoHo is the rooftop of the Arlo SoHo hotel at 231 Hudson Street — despite the name, that address is in Hudson Square, not SoHo. It runs Monday to Friday 4–11pm, Saturday 2pm–midnight and Sunday 4–11pm, with the kitchen closing at 10pm and happy hour daily 4–7pm. It publishes no drink prices. It is the earlier, calmer of the two rooftops and closes before most people consider the night finished.

One that does not belong on this page at all, despite the name: Soho House New York is at 29-35 Ninth Avenue in the Meatpacking District, roughly a mile and a half north, and it is a private members' club. If a guide lists it as a SoHo option, that guide checked nothing.

Nolita spillover, and why the labels matter

Two of the best walk-in bars near SoHo are in Nolita, five minutes east across Lafayette, and both are open until 4am — which is later than anything in SoHo proper.

Spring Lounge (48 Spring St, at Mulberry) is the red-awninged corner bar with the stuffed sharks, open 8am–4am Monday to Saturday and 10am–4am Sunday. Its published drinks list puts draft beer at $8 and wine by the glass from $12, which by 2026 downtown standards is close to a public service.

Mother's Ruin (18 Spring St) is the other one, open daily 11am–4am, with signature cocktails published at $16 (one at $17), beer at $6–8 and wine at $12 a glass. There is a happy hour, but the venue does not publish its times or prices, so ask at the bar rather than trusting an aggregator.

Getting these labels right is not pedantry. If you book a hotel "in SoHo for the nightlife" and the bars you actually wanted are Nolita and Hudson Square rooms, you have bought the wrong walk. Our hotel neighborhood matcher is built for exactly that mistake.

Dinner-led nights

More SoHo evenings are built around a table than a bar, and the restaurants here are the neighborhood's real draw. All of the following were confirmed operating on 2026-08-26.

Balthazar (80 Spring St) has been the default since 1997 and serves dinner daily 5pm–midnight, which makes it one of the later kitchens in the neighborhood. Raoul's (180 Prince St) has been on its block since 1975 and remains the more clubbable, low-lit option; note that its own website currently throws a security-certificate warning, so book by phone or a reservation platform rather than assuming the site is compromised. The Dutch (131 Sullivan St) and Jack's Wife Freda (226 Lafayette St — not 224, as several listings have it) cover the mid-range; Jack's Wife Freda closes at 10pm most nights and 11pm on Friday and Saturday, so it is a dinner stop, not a late one.

Lure Fishbar (142 Mercer St, at Prince) is worth a specific note because the last two years of coverage were wrong by the time most guides copied it. Reports in December 2024 said Prada was pushing the restaurant out of its underground space when the lease expired. As of June 2026 that was reported resolved in the restaurant's favour, with the owner describing a long-term sublease and no Prada café planned. Lure is open and taking reservations. If you read that it is closing, you are reading 2024.

Bar Mercer is a genuinely new room — 25 West Houston Street, just off Mercer, opened in 2025 by Mercer Street Hospitality with Preston Clark as executive chef, dinner Tuesday to Sunday from 5pm and weekend brunch from noon. Do not confuse it with the Mercer Kitchen at 99 Prince Street, which is a different restaurant and has been closed since the end of 2022.

The late option, and the walk out

There is essentially one club-style room in the SoHo orbit. Paul's Casablanca (305 Spring St) opened in the former Sway space and runs Wednesday to Sunday with the door open 10pm–4am. It publishes no cover or door policy, which in practice means the door is discretionary — arrive early, arrive small, or arrive on a list. It is in Hudson Square.

Everything else late is a walk. Eastward, Spring Lounge and Mother's Ruin run to 4am in Nolita, and ten more minutes gets you to the Lower East Side, which is the real answer to "where is everyone at 2am." Westward, the Ear Inn runs to 4am. Northward, the Village rooms are ticketed rather than late: Blue Note Jazz Club (131 W 3rd St, Greenwich Village) publishes a $20 consumption minimum per show plus a $35 corkage and $30 cake fee, with ticket prices set per show rather than published as a range; Comedy Cellar (117 MacDougal St, Greenwich Village) publishes tickets at $15 Monday to Wednesday, $18 Thursday and Sunday, $25 Friday and Saturday, with a stated "two item minimum per person — food or drink or bottle of water." Joe's Pub at The Public Theater (425 Lafayette St, NoHo) is the closest thing to a proper small music room in walking distance.

The honest downsides

  • It empties early. This is the single most important thing to know. Balthazar's dinner service ends at midnight, the Crosby Bar closes at 1am, Temple Bar closes at 1am midweek. By 1:30am on a Wednesday, SoHo is a quiet grid of closed shops. Plan the last hour of your night somewhere else.
  • It is expensive without being exclusive. Three of the four best bars in the area list cocktails at $22–26. You are paying downtown-hotel prices for rooms that mostly do not have a door policy worth queuing for. The value play is walking east into Nolita, where the same night costs about a third less.
  • Half the "SoHo" venues are not in SoHo. Spring Lounge and Mother's Ruin are Nolita. Ear Inn and Paul's Casablanca and A.R.T. SoHo are Hudson Square. Temple Bar and Milano's are NoHo. Soho House is Meatpacking. Blue Note and Comedy Cellar are Greenwich Village. City Winery is not remotely nearby — it now operates at 25 11th Avenue on Pier 57, up by Chelsea. Any list that files all of those under SoHo was assembled from other lists.
  • Weekends are a retail hangover. Saturday evening in SoHo carries the day's shopping crowd, which means restaurant waits and rooftop queues driven by people who were never planning a night out. Tuesday to Thursday is the same neighborhood with room to sit down.
  • There is no dance floor. One club, discretionary door, five nights a week. If dancing is the point of the evening, start in SoHo and finish in the Lower East Side or Chelsea.
  • Some famous names close before dinner. Sadelle's at 463 West Broadway shuts at 3pm on weekdays and 5pm at weekends. It appears on a startling number of "SoHo nightlife" lists.

Stale-listicle check: what has actually closed

Use this as a freshness test on any other SoHo guide you are reading. If it still lists these as current, it has not been checked in years.

  • The Mercer Kitchen, 99 Prince Street — Jean-Georges Vongerichten's restaurant in the Mercer Hotel closed at the end of 2022 after 25 years. The name survives in listings; the restaurant does not. (And it is not the same thing as the new Bar Mercer on West Houston.)
  • Pegu Club — the cocktail bar that trained half of downtown announced permanent closure on 30 April 2020 after fifteen years. It is still cited constantly as a SoHo destination.
  • Lucky Strike, 59 Grand Street — Keith McNally closed it permanently in April 2020 after 31 years. The space has since been taken by a different operator.
  • Gitano Garden of Love, 76 Varick Street — the Hudson Square outdoor venue is gone; the brand's own site describes it as "formerly" at that address and now operates seasonally as Gitano Island on Governors Island. City listings mark the Varick Street address out of business.
  • Citywide, for the same reason: the old Brooklyn Mirage no longer exists under that name — the site relaunched as Pacha New York in June 2026 — Up&Down in Meatpacking is closed, The Press Lounge now trades as Hudson VU, and Therapy, Copacabana Times Square and Fort Defiance are all closed. A guide still listing those as current was not checked this year.

One live watch item rather than a closure: the Crosby Street Hotel is running reduced menus until 20 September 2026 while its kitchen is rebuilt.

FAQ

Is SoHo actually good for nightlife?

It is very good for the first half of a night and weak for the second. SoHo is a shopping and dining district that happens to hold a handful of excellent bars, not a nightlife district. You get serious cocktail rooms, two rooftops, three genuinely old bars and a dense run of restaurants — and then, around midnight, the sidewalks empty. If your plan ends at 1am, SoHo is a strong choice. If it ends at 4am, plan to leave the neighborhood.

What time does SoHo shut down at night?

Most of it stops between midnight and 1am. Balthazar serves dinner until midnight and the Crosby Bar runs to 1am. Temple Bar closes at 1am Monday to Wednesday and 2am Thursday to Saturday. The 4am rooms are all on the edges rather than in SoHo proper: Fanelli Cafe is open late, the Ear Inn in Hudson Square runs to 4am daily, Milano's on East Houston runs to 4am, and Spring Lounge and Mother's Ruin in Nolita both run to 4am. Paul's Casablanca on Spring Street is the one club-style room, with the door open 10pm to 4am Wednesday to Sunday.

How much does a night out in SoHo cost?

Budget roughly $70–120 per person for a cocktail-led night. The published menus tell the story: Temple Bar lists most cocktails at $22–23, the Crosby Bar lists signature cocktails at $23, and JIMMY at ModernHaus lists specialty cocktails at $23–26. The cheaper version of the same night sits just east in Nolita, where Mother's Ruin publishes signature cocktails at $16 and beers at $6–8 — closer to $45–70 per person. Dinner at a SoHo landmark is a separate line item entirely.

Are Spring Lounge and Mother's Ruin in SoHo?

No. Both are in Nolita, and a lot of guides get this wrong. Spring Lounge at 48 Spring Street and Mother's Ruin at 18 Spring Street sit east of Lafayette Street, which is SoHo's eastern boundary. They are a five-minute walk from SoHo and worth including in any route, but calling them SoHo bars is a labelling error. The same applies to the Ear Inn at 326 Spring Street and Paul's Casablanca at 305 Spring Street, which are both in Hudson Square, and to Temple Bar and Milano's, which are in NoHo.

Can you get into Soho House without a membership?

Generally no, and it is not in SoHo either. Soho House New York is a private members' club at 29-35 Ninth Avenue in the Meatpacking District, roughly a mile and a half north of SoHo. Access is through membership or by booking a room in the house's hotel. Treat any guide that lists it as a SoHo nightlife option as unchecked on two separate counts.
