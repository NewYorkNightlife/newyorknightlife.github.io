Upper West Side Nightlife: The Jazz, the 4am Bars, and the Clubs That Do Not Exist

The Upper West Side has a jazz supper club with published minimums, at least three bars that pour until 4am, and not one dance floor above 59th Street. Here is what is actually open in 2026, what it charges, and the honest answer to "can I go out up here?"

Best for: Jazz, dinner, long unhurried drinksPeak: Wed–Sat 7pm–1amWeak spot: Zero clubs, zero dance floors

The cliché about the Upper West Side is that it goes to bed early. That is half right, and the wrong half is the one most guides repeat. The neighborhood does have a real late-night bar row — The Dead Poet and the Dublin House both pour until 4am, seven nights a week — but it has no nightclub, no dance floor, and no venue with club programming of any kind. You can drink here as late as anywhere in Manhattan. You simply cannot dance.

That single fact should drive your whole plan. If you want a dance floor, the honest advice is to eat extremely well up here, catch a jazz set or a show, and then take the 1 train downtown — see the NYC clubs guide or the Hell's Kitchen guide for where that night actually happens. If you want a long conversation over a good drink, you are already in one of the better neighborhoods in the city for it.

The rough geography: Amsterdam Avenue from 72nd to 86th is the bar spine and where every 4am license sits. Columbus Avenue is the restaurant-and-wine-bar lane. Broadway is the transit artery and holds the big rooms — the Beacon, Symphony Space, and Smoke up at 106th. Lincoln Center and the blocks around 65th Street run on performance clocks, not bar clocks. For the wider map see the Manhattan nightlife guide, the neighbouring Upper East Side and Harlem, or the neighborhoods hub.

Smoke, and the real reason to come uptown at night

Smoke Jazz & Supper Club at 2751 Broadway is the single best argument for an Upper West Side night, and it is routinely mislabelled as Harlem. It is not — it sits between 105th and 106th Street, firmly on the Upper West Side, and the mistake matters because it sends people to the wrong subway stop.

It is also one of the very few New York music rooms that publishes its own minimums, so you can budget precisely instead of guessing. Sets run at 6pm, 8pm and 10pm. The 6pm and 8pm dinner shows carry a minimum of one entrée per person. The 10pm show has a $20 minimum per person, and standing room carries a $20 consumption minimum. The separate Bar and Lounge has no minimum at all, runs Wednesday to Sunday from 4pm, and pours $7 happy-hour cocktails. A ticket is required for the listening room itself. That structure gives you three genuinely different price points inside one address — the cheapest being to sit at the bar, pay nothing extra, and still hear the band.

For the rest of the city's rooms, see our NYC live music guide.

Beacon Theatre (2124 Broadway) is the other music anchor — a 2,894-seat, three-level room operated by MSG Entertainment, ticketed through Ticketmaster, running roughly seventy shows a year. It publishes no standard ticket price; every show is priced separately. Symphony Space (2537 Broadway at 95th) is the smaller, stranger, more literary sibling and also publishes no price floor.

The 4am bars, and what they actually charge

This is the part of the neighborhood that gets written off unfairly. Amsterdam Avenue has a genuine late-night row.

The Dead Poet (450 Amsterdam Ave) is the anchor: a narrow literary pub open Monday to Thursday 2pm–4am, Friday from 1pm, and Saturday and Sunday from noon — 4am every single night. Its house "Dead Poet" cocktail is $19, built from seven liquors, and you keep the glass.

The Dublin House (225 W 79th St) runs noon to 4am daily under a neon harp sign that has been there for decades. It publishes no drink prices at all — only merchandise ($20 hats, $35 sweatshirts) — so treat any specific figure you read elsewhere as someone's guess.

The Caledonia (424 Amsterdam Ave) is the whisky bar, open to 4am Friday and Saturday and 2am the rest of the week. It runs the best published deal on this page: $15 for any draft beer plus a smash burger with fries, Monday to Saturday until 7pm, with happy hour taking $2 off drafts and $3 off wines. Note that its Upper East Side sibling is on Second Avenue — a different bar, a different borough half.

Prohibition (503 Columbus Ave) runs to 2am Friday and Saturday with live music most nights; find it at prohibition.net, not the .nyc domain, which redirects elsewhere. George Keeley (485 Amsterdam Ave) is the beer bar. Nobody Told Me (951 Amsterdam Ave, up at 107th) is the neighborhood's best modern cocktail room and runs to 2am daily — and yes, it is genuinely Upper West Side, despite frequently being filed under Hell's Kitchen by lists confusing it with a similarly named bar. Owl's Tail (215 W 75th St) also runs to 2am on weekends, though one third-party listing still carries a stale closure note, so call before you make it the plan.

Da Capo (322 Columbus Ave) and Asset (329 Columbus Ave) round out the Columbus Avenue drinking options, both verified operating at their listed addresses.

Dinner, and the Lincoln Center clock

The Upper West Side's restaurants are its real strength, and the thing to understand is that a big share of them run on performance timing rather than bar timing. If there is a curtain at 7:30pm, every table around Lincoln Center turns over at 6pm and empties at 7:15pm, then fills again at 10pm. Book on the wrong side of that and you will either be rushed or sitting alone.

Café Luxembourg (200 W 70th St) is the enduring one — a proper bistro that has outlived most of its competition. Celeste (502 Amsterdam Ave) is the cash-conscious Italian favourite. RedFarm (2170 Broadway) is the dim sum room. Tavern on the Green sits at West 67th and Central Park West, technically in the park, and is the one genuine tourist set-piece up here that locals will still concede is pleasant on a summer evening.

Jacob's Pickles has moved and this trips up nearly every guide: it is now at 680 Columbus Avenue, having opened there in October 2025. Its old 509 Amsterdam address is a different bar entirely. Any listing pointing you to 509 Amsterdam for Jacob's Pickles is stale.

The Smith Lincoln Square (1900 Broadway) is the reliable pre-show default and publishes real numbers: cocktails $17.50, oysters $4 each, mains roughly $18–55. Lincoln Ristorante (142 W 65th St) is the fine-dining option physically on the Lincoln Center campus — note that it takes an annual closure from 30 August to 7 September, which is right now as this page publishes.

Culture that actually runs at night

New York Historical (170 Central Park West) is the best genuine evening-culture item in the neighborhood and almost nobody uses it. It renamed in October 2024, dropping both the word "Society" and the hyphen, so older listings look wrong. It opens Friday 11am–8pm — the only late night in its week — with pay-as-you-wish admission from 5 to 8pm. Standard admission is $24 for adults, $19 seniors, $13 students, $6 for children 5–13, free under 5.

Lincoln Center (10 Lincoln Center Plaza) runs free and choose-what-you-pay programming, most visibly through its Summer for the City season, alongside rush, student and military discounts. It names those discount categories but publishes no dollar floors, so check the specific event. Juilliard (60 Lincoln Center Plaza) is the genuinely free option that visitors overlook entirely — its Lunch Hour and Rush Hour performances and the outdoor Hearst Plaza series cost nothing, with a box office open Tuesday to Friday 11am–5pm.

The American Museum of Natural History (200 Central Park West) is a daytime anchor rather than a night one. We could not verify current evening programming this cycle, so check its own calendar before building an evening around it — and do not trust a third-party listing on this point.

The honest downsides

  • There is no club. At all. Not one venue we verified on the Upper West Side has a dance floor or club programming. The entire late-night inventory is Irish pubs, whiskey and wine bars, a jazz supper club and restaurant bars. Anything marketed as an "Upper West Side club" is either a private event space or is not actually on the Upper West Side.
  • Central Park West is a late-night trap. The B runs weekdays daytime only — no nights, no weekends — and the C stops running late nights. After roughly midnight the Central Park West local stops are served by the A train alone. Walk west to Broadway instead.
  • The restaurants close well before the bars. Kitchens broadly stop between 10 and 11pm while the Amsterdam Avenue bars run to 4am. There is a real dead zone around 10:30pm where you cannot get food but it is too early to give up.
  • Lincoln Center dictates the crowd. On a night with a big curtain, every restaurant within four blocks is unbookable at 6pm and empty at 8pm. On a dark night the same blocks are quiet. Check what is on before you assume a Tuesday is quiet.
  • Almost nothing publishes drink prices. Smoke, The Smith, The Caledonia and The Dead Poet are the exceptions on this page. Most Upper West Side bars post no numbers at all, so any specific figure you find on an aggregator is an estimate someone typed in.
  • The venue list has thinned. The neighborhood lost several long-running rooms across 2025 and 2026, including an 82-year-old bar. The section below exists so you can spot a guide that has not noticed.

Recently closed: how to spot a stale Upper West Side guide

If a guide lists any of these as current, it has not been checked this year — discount the rest of it accordingly.

  • The Emerald Inn (250 W 72nd St) closed permanently on 4 December 2025 after 82 years, following a 2024 health-department shutdown and a fundraiser that fell far short of its goal.
  • The Milling Room (446 Columbus Ave) closed in December 2025 amid reported back rent and unpaid vendors. Its old domain now sits in a redirect loop, which is its own warning sign.
  • Bar Boulud and Boulud Sud both closed on 23 June 2026. These were Upper West Side rooms, near Lincoln Center — not Upper East Side, despite the Boulud name being associated with the other side of the park.
  • Jing Fong (380 Amsterdam Ave) served its last meal on 8 March 2026, and The Leopard at des Artistes (1 W 67th St) closed on 15 February 2026.
  • Manhattan Cricket Club and Burke & Wills (226 W 79th St) are gone. Worse, the burkeandwillsny.com domain now serves gambling spam and manhattancricketclub.com no longer resolves at all — do not follow either link from an old listicle.
  • Ippudo Westside has closed.
  • Jacob's Pickles did not close — it moved to 680 Columbus Avenue. The 509 Amsterdam address is wrong everywhere.
  • The Rochard appears on Upper West Side lists and belongs on neither. It closed in October 2021, and it was on Lexington Avenue on the Upper East Side in the first place.
  • Sala Thai on Amsterdam Avenue we are deliberately not listing. Its site is live and shows hours, but carries an undated banner reading "We will open on May 4th," and the brand has multiple New York locations of differing status. We could not resolve the conflict, so we are flagging it rather than recommending it. Call first.
  • Calaveras (949 Columbus Ave) is worth a correction even though we are not listing it: it is Upper West Side, not Harlem, where it is frequently filed. We could not confirm its current operating status and it has no working website, so we have left it off the map rather than send you there.
  • Elsewhere in the city, the same rot applies: Brooklyn Mirage is gone and the site now operates as Pacha New York, The Press Lounge is now Hudson VU, and Up&Down, Therapy, Copacabana Times Square, Fort Defiance, Port Morris Distillery, Faro, Barbetta, Rockwood Music Hall, Pig and Khao, Paris Blues and The Rookery are all shut. Minton's Playhouse in Harlem has a contested status — we can confirm neither that it is open nor that it is permanently closed, so treat any confident claim either way with suspicion.

FAQ

Does the Upper West Side have any nightclubs?

No. Not one venue we could verify on the Upper West Side has a dance floor or club programming. The entire late-night inventory is Irish pubs, whiskey and wine bars, a jazz supper club and a handful of restaurant bars. If a dance floor is the point of your night, the honest advice is to eat and drink well up here and then take the 1 train downtown. Anything marketed as an Upper West Side club is either a private event space or is not actually on the Upper West Side.

How late do Upper West Side bars stay open?

Later than the neighborhood's reputation suggests. The Dead Poet at 450 Amsterdam Avenue runs to 4am seven nights a week, and the Dublin House at 225 West 79th Street is open from noon to 4am daily. The Caledonia on Amsterdam goes to 4am on Friday and Saturday and 2am the rest of the week. Prohibition, Nobody Told Me and Owl's Tail all run to 2am. The accurate summary is that the Upper West Side has plenty of 2am and 4am bars and zero clubs — you can drink as late as anywhere in Manhattan, you just cannot dance.

How much does a night at Smoke Jazz & Supper Club cost?

Smoke publishes its own minimums, which is rare for a New York music room. Sets run at 6pm, 8pm and 10pm. The 6pm and 8pm dinner shows carry a minimum of one entrée per person. The 10pm show has a $20 minimum per person, and standing room carries a $20 consumption minimum. The separate Bar and Lounge has no minimum at all and runs Wednesday to Sunday from 4pm, with $7 happy-hour cocktails. A ticket is required for the listening room.

What is the best late-night subway from the Upper West Side?

Broadway, not Central Park West. The 1, 2 and 3 on Broadway all run at every hour including overnight, so the 72nd, 79th, 86th and 96th Street stations are reliable at 3am. Central Park West is the trap: the B runs weekdays daytime only, with no night and no weekend service, and the C stops running late nights. After roughly midnight the Central Park West local stops are served by the A train only. If you are drinking until closing, plan to walk west to Broadway rather than east to the park.

Which Upper West Side restaurants and bars have closed recently?

Several, and stale guides still list them. The Emerald Inn at 250 West 72nd Street closed permanently on 4 December 2025 after 82 years. The Milling Room at 446 Columbus Avenue closed in December 2025. Jing Fong on Amsterdam served its last meal on 8 March 2026, and The Leopard at des Artistes closed on 15 February 2026. Manhattan Cricket Club and Burke & Wills on West 79th Street are gone, and Ippudo Westside has also closed. Separately, Jacob's Pickles did not close but did move — it is now at 680 Columbus Avenue, not 509 Amsterdam.
