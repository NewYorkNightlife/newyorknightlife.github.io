Greenpoint Nightlife: The Bars Worth the Trip, and the One Train That Can Ruin It

Greenpoint has one of the best bar-per-block ratios in Brooklyn, one 1,000-capacity concert hall, exactly one nightclub — and exactly one subway line, which the MTA keeps switching off on weekends. Here is what is genuinely open in 2026, what it costs, and the four rooms that closed while the listicles kept recommending them.

Greenpoint is a drinking neighborhood, not a clubbing one, and the distinction matters more here than almost anywhere in Brooklyn. Inside about fifteen walkable blocks you get a Danish beer bar with twenty-one taps, an Art Nouveau cocktail room named after Mae West, a listening bar built around turntables, a bar with a private karaoke room, a Michelin-starred taqueria and a 1,000-capacity Polish ballroom that has been booking rock shows since 2001. What you do not get is a strip. The good rooms are scattered across three corridors, and there is precisely one dance floor.

The geography: Manhattan Avenue is the spine, running north from the Nassau Avenue G stop to the top of the neighborhood, and it holds the most rooms. Franklin Street, one block from the East River, is quieter, newer and more restaurant-led. Nassau Avenue east of Manhattan is where the late bars are — Diamond Lil and Goldie's both run to 3 or 4am. Anything on Driggs or Bedford is already brushing the Williamsburg line. For the wider picture see the Brooklyn nightlife by zone guide, neighbouring Williamsburg and Bushwick, or the neighborhoods hub.

Read this before you pick a date: the G train

Greenpoint is served by exactly one subway line. The G stops at Greenpoint Avenue and Nassau Avenue and there is no redundancy — no second line, no express, no alternative if it goes down. That is the single most important planning fact about this neighborhood and almost no guide mentions it.

Through 2026 the MTA has been running signal-modernisation and Newtown Creek tunnel work that suspends G service north of Bedford–Nostrand Avenue on selected weekends, from 9:30pm Friday until 5am Monday, with free B93 shuttle buses replacing trains. On one of those weekends, Greenpoint is effectively bus-or-car-only from Friday night onward. Published dates have included weekends in June, August, September and December; they move, so check the MTA's planned service changes for your specific date rather than trusting any list, including this one.

Two more things worth knowing. First, the much-publicised payoff — computer-based train control and full-length trains — is not live and is not close. Activation has slipped to 2029 because the rolling stock still needs retrofitting, so any article telling you the G "now runs longer trains" is wrong. The G still runs five-car trains, which is why locals wait in the middle of the platform. Second, outside the outages the G runs roughly every ten minutes days and evenings, which is fine for a bar night and frustrating for a connection.

The practical version: if your date falls on an outage weekend, either plan a car home or move the night to Williamsburg, where the L runs 24 hours. Our late-night subway route tool is built for exactly this decision.

The bars, and what they actually charge

Black Rabbit (91 Greenpoint Ave) is the most useful room in the neighborhood because it publishes its prices. Its own site lists a happy hour running daily until 8pm: $6 for the house lager, $10 for a pint with a bourbon shot, and $10 for a rotating "cocktail du hour" — figures a third-party happy-hour tracker independently reports the same way. Beers otherwise run $6–9 and cocktails $9–30. It is open to 2am Sunday through Thursday and 4am Friday and Saturday, with a garden that shuts at 10pm and a kitchen that runs to midnight.

Tørst (615 Manhattan Ave) is the beer destination — twenty-one draft lines and a couple of hundred bottles, Monday to Thursday 3pm to midnight, Friday and Saturday noon to 1am, Sunday noon to midnight. It publishes no prices. Broken Land (105 Franklin St) is the Franklin Street counterpart, and its own site gives the widest hours on this page: Tuesday to Friday 2pm to 4am, Saturday from noon to 4am. Aggregators disagree with the venue about its weekend hours; believe the venue.

Diamond Lil (179 Nassau Ave) and Goldie's (195 Nassau Ave) are sixty seconds apart and both go late — Diamond Lil to 3am most nights, Goldie's to 4am Thursday through Saturday. Diamond Lil is the pretty one, an Art Nouveau room with a garden and a weekday 4–7pm happy hour. Goldie's is the opposite: pool table, backyard, and a private karaoke room that holds about twenty. Goldie's happy hour is reported at $2 off drafts Monday to Friday 4–7pm with free karaoke; it publishes nothing itself, and we could not find a working website for it, so treat that figure as third-party.

Eavesdrop (674 Manhattan Ave) is the one to book your evening around if you care about sound: a 36-seat audiophile listening bar with acoustic panels and turntables, open 5pm to 1am Monday to Thursday, to 2am Friday and Saturday, from 2pm at weekends. No cover, no published prices. Good Bar (1 Bedford Ave, at the Williamsburg edge) runs vinyl listening sessions Wednesdays 7pm–midnight, Fridays and Saturdays 9pm–2am and Sundays 3–8pm. Troost (1011 Manhattan Ave) is open every day 4pm to 2am with live jazz programming; its daily 4–8pm happy hour and $8 Negroni are reported by third-party listings rather than published by the bar.

Two rooms most lists have not caught up with. Sonny's Corner (142 Franklin St) opened in February 2026 in the space Pencil Factory vacated — same corner, different bar. And Me and Paul's (60 Greenpoint Ave) is what Paulie Gee's original restaurant became when it reopened in April 2026: a dive bar with a pool table, darts and a jukebox, still running the same pizza oven. Reported opening prices were $15 pizzas, $12–13 cocktails and $3 PBR; walk-ins only, no reservations.

Dinner, and the one Michelin star

Greenpoint's restaurants close earlier than its bars, which changes how you sequence. Oxomoco (128 Greenpoint Ave) is the headline — a wood-fired Mexican room and the only Michelin-starred restaurant in the neighborhood, serving dinner 5–10pm daily with brunch earlier. Resy is essential and it is genuinely hard to get. Glasserie (95 Commercial St) sits right up on the creek and runs 5:30–10pm Monday to Thursday and 11am–11pm Friday to Sunday, and unusually for a room of its quality it takes walk-ins.

Chez Ma Tante (90 Calyer St) does dinner 5–10pm on weekdays with weekend brunch; it is Resy-driven and its own homepage lists neither address nor hours, so the hours above come from listings rather than the restaurant. Le Fanfare (1103 Manhattan Ave) runs 5:30–10pm Monday to Thursday and to 11pm Friday and Saturday — still open, despite appearing on "closed" lists. Five Leaves (18 Bedford Ave) is the all-day room, 8am to 11pm seven days, and the most reliable no-plan option in Greenpoint. Rule of Thirds (171 Banker St) does 5–11pm daily in a big room that also takes private events. Taqueria Ramirez (94 Franklin St) is dine-in only, noon to 10pm Tuesday to Sunday, closed Mondays — and there is usually a line.

None of these publishes prices on its own site. If you are costing a night, the NYC nightlife budget planner is more honest than any figure we could invent.

Music, and the club with the broken website

Warsaw (261 Driggs Ave) is the reason Greenpoint shows up in touring itineraries: a 1,000-capacity ballroom inside the Polish National Home, a building that has stood since 1914 and has hosted rock shows since 2001. It is fully operating and booking — its calendar carries dates through November 2026 at the time of writing. Tickets are sold through Ticketmaster and no standard price is published, so any specific figure you see elsewhere is someone's estimate. The pierogi at the attached Warsaw Bistro are not a myth. More options across the city in our NYC live music guide.

Good Room (98 Meserole Ave), in the old Club Europa space, is the neighborhood's only nightclub — and it comes with a genuine warning rather than a recommendation. Its official domain currently fails with an expired security certificate. We checked it directly; it does not load over http or https. We are therefore not linking it, and neither should anything else. Three independent listing services do show the club carrying future-dated bookings into December 2026, which is why we still list it rather than removing it — but this is a "check Instagram, then go" venue, not a "turn up and assume" one. Our Good Room venue facts page carries the door detail and cover range we verified separately.

Scrappleland (1150 Manhattan Ave) is the wildcard: a two-storey pinball hall with a bar and a rooftop in the old Greenpoint Beer & Ale building, with what Brooklyn Paper calls the largest machine collection in the city. Reported counts range from fifty-odd to sixty-seven depending on which write-up you read, and no per-play or entry pricing is published. Open to 2am daily.

If you want an actual dance floor with certainty, leave. Bushwick and East Williamsburg are one or two stops away and that is where the clubs are.

The honest downsides

  • One subway line, and it is under construction. The G is it. Selected 2026 weekends suspend service north of Bedford–Nostrand from 9:30pm Friday to 5am Monday. If you do not check the date, you will find out at 1am.
  • The five-car problem. The G still runs half-length trains. Full-length trains depend on signalling that has slipped to 2029. Stand in the middle of the platform.
  • Greenpoint has one club. Good Room, and its website is broken. If a dance floor is the point of the evening, this is the wrong neighborhood.
  • The venue list shrank hard. Four rooms that appear on current listicles — Saint Vitus, Ponyboy, Pencil Factory and Achilles Heel — are gone. Two more, Paulie Gee's and Brooklyn Brewery's Williamsburg tasting room, changed identity or closed in 2026.
  • Almost nothing publishes prices. Black Rabbit is the honourable exception. No restaurant here posts a menu with numbers on its own site, and Warsaw publishes no ticket prices. Every specific figure elsewhere is a third-party estimate.
  • The good restaurants stop at 10pm. Oxomoco, Chez Ma Tante and Le Fanfare all finish around 10–11pm while the bars run to 4am. Eat at 7, drink at 10 — not the reverse.
  • Websites here rot unusually fast. Beyond Good Room's expired certificate, we found Greenpoint venue domains that no longer resolve at all and one that now redirects to its parent hospitality group. Check Instagram before you trust a search result.

Recently closed: how to spot a stale Greenpoint guide

North Brooklyn lost an unusual number of rooms between 2024 and 2026. If a guide lists any of the following as current, it has not been checked this year — discount the rest of it accordingly.

  • Saint Vitus (1120 Manhattan Ave) was shut mid-set by the Department of Buildings in February 2024 over permit and occupancy violations, and closed permanently that August. It is reopening — in Bushwick, at 428 Troutman Street in the old Brooklyn Made space, with autumn 2026 dates announced. It is not a Greenpoint venue any more and, as of today, it is not open yet.
  • Ponyboy (632 Manhattan Ave) closed quietly in August 2025 after seven years in the former Manhattan Inn space. Its website is still live, which makes it a particularly effective trap.
  • Pencil Factory (142 Franklin St) closed permanently in July 2025. The corner is not dead — the space reopened in February 2026 as Sonny's Corner. "Closed" is right for the old name and misleading if you are standing outside.
  • Achilles Heel (180 West St) closed abruptly in February 2026 after thirteen years. Its former domain now redirects to its parent restaurant group, whose own page for the bar reads "2013–2026."
  • Paulie Gee's original Greenpoint restaurant (60 Greenpoint Ave) closed in January 2026 for renovation and reopened on 30 April 2026 as Me and Paul's, a tavern. Same address, same oven, different name and a very different room.
  • Nura (46 Norman Ave) served its last day on 15 February 2026.
  • Brooklyn Brewery's Williamsburg tasting room at 79 North 11th Street held its final night, billed as "One Last Night at 79 N 11th," on 29 August 2026, ahead of a move to a location that had not been announced. Tours were already paused before then. Any guide sending you there is out of date.
  • Elsewhere in the city, the same rot applies: Brooklyn Mirage no longer exists under that name — the site relaunched as Pacha New York in June 2026 — and Up&Down, Therapy, Copacabana Times Square, Fort Defiance, Port Morris Distillery, Faro, Barbetta, Rockwood Music Hall, Pig and Khao, Paris Blues, The Rookery and Ippudo Westside are all shut. The Press Lounge now operates as Hudson VU. Music Hall of Williamsburg closes at the end of 2026.

FAQ

What is Greenpoint known for at night?

Bars, not clubs. Greenpoint is a drinking neighborhood with an unusually high density of good standalone rooms in about fifteen walkable blocks — Black Rabbit and Diamond Lil and Goldie's for late nights, Tørst for beer, Eavesdrop for listening, Troost for jazz. It has one 1,000-capacity concert hall in Warsaw, a Michelin-starred restaurant in Oxomoco, and exactly one dance club in Good Room. What it does not have is a strip: the good rooms are scattered across Manhattan Avenue, Franklin Street and Nassau Avenue rather than stacked on one block.

Is the G train reliable for a night out in Greenpoint?

Not on every weekend, and the G is the only subway that serves Greenpoint. Through 2026 the MTA has been suspending G service north of Bedford–Nostrand Avenue on selected weekends from 9:30pm Friday until 5am Monday, with free B93 shuttle buses replacing trains. On those weekends Greenpoint is effectively bus-or-car-only after Friday night. Check the MTA's planned service changes for your date before you commit to a Greenpoint plan. Outside the outages the G runs roughly every 10 minutes days and evenings, in five-car trains that stop short of where you are standing on the platform.

How much does a night out in Greenpoint cost?

A bar night runs roughly $45–85 per person, because almost nothing here charges a cover. Black Rabbit publishes its happy hour on its own site: $6 for its house lager, $10 for a pint with a bourbon shot, and $10 for a rotating cocktail, daily until 8pm. Beers there run $6–9 and cocktails $9–30. A dinner-led night at Oxomoco or Glasserie pushes past $100 a head. The only reliable cover in the neighborhood is Good Room, and Warsaw shows are ticketed through Ticketmaster with no standard price published.

Does Greenpoint have a nightclub?

One: Good Room at 98 Meserole Avenue, in the old Club Europa space. It comes with a warning. Good Room's own website currently fails with an expired security certificate, so do not trust any link to it, and we are not linking it. Three independent listing services show the club with future-dated bookings through late 2026, which is why we still list it — but check its Instagram before you build a night around it. If you want a guaranteed dance floor, Bushwick and East Williamsburg are one or two stops away.

Which Greenpoint bars have closed recently?

Four names that still appear on current listicles are gone. Saint Vitus was shut mid-set by the Department of Buildings in February 2024 and closed permanently that August; it is reopening in Bushwick at 428 Troutman Street, not in Greenpoint. Ponyboy on Manhattan Avenue closed quietly in August 2025. Pencil Factory on Franklin Street closed in July 2025 and the space reopened in February 2026 as Sonny's Corner. Achilles Heel on West Street closed abruptly in February 2026. Two more are renames rather than closures: Paulie Gee's original Greenpoint restaurant reopened in April 2026 as Me and Paul's, and Brooklyn Brewery's Williamsburg tasting room held its last night at 79 North 11th Street on 29 August 2026.
