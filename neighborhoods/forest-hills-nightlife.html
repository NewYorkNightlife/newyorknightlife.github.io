Forest Hills nightlife

Forest Hills has two completely different nightlife modes: roughly 40 nights a summer when 13,000 people pour out of Forest Hills Stadium and every pub within a ten-minute walk is slammed, and every other night, when this is a pleasant, quiet-by-midnight neighborhood of pubs, wine bars, and restaurant bars along Austin Street. Knowing which night you're walking into is the whole game.

This guide covers the 2026 concert season and how to run a show night without spending an hour in lines, the Austin Street and Station Square drinking map, and an honest read on what the neighborhood offers when the stadium is dark. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

Forest Hills Stadium: the neighborhood's nightlife engine

The historic 13,000-seat stadium at the edge of Forest Hills Gardens is what puts this neighborhood on a nightlife map at all. The 2026 season runs June 6 through October 10 with one of its biggest lineups yet — Bob Dylan, Paul Simon, David Byrne, Erykah Badu, Dave Matthews Band, Wilco, Sarah McLachlan, Jon Batiste, a Juneteenth gospel mini-festival, and the return of the All Things Go festival, among others.

Honest context: the stadium's relationship with its immediate neighbors is strained. In 2025, the NYPD briefly withheld sound permits amid a dispute between the venue and the Forest Hills Gardens Corporation over the private streets surrounding the stadium; the season ultimately proceeded, and the 2026 calendar went ahead as announced. Practical takeaway for you: shows end on time (hard curfews are part of the deal), the streets directly around the stadium are residential and privately owned — don't linger or pregame on them — and the party before and after happens in the commercial blocks, not at the gates.

Concert-night playbook

  • Arrive early and eat first. Every bar within five minutes of Station Square hits capacity 60–90 minutes before doors. Sitting down at 5:30 PM for a 7 PM show is the difference between a table and a 40-minute wait.
  • Pre-show anchor: Forest Hills Station House (106-11 71st Ave) — a craft-beer gastropub steps from the LIRR and the stadium walk, and the single most obvious pre-show room. It knows it, so expect a crowd.
  • Overflow tier: Irish Cottage (108-07 72nd Ave) and the pubs and restaurant bars along Austin Street between roughly 69th Rd and 72nd Ave.
  • Post-show: the exit crush lasts about 45 minutes. If you want a nightcap, walk to Austin Street rather than fighting for the first bar off Station Square, and check kitchen hours — many close by 10–11 PM even on show nights.

The everyday scene: Austin Street and Station Square

Austin Street is the commercial spine — a long retail strip where the drinking options are mostly pub-style bars and restaurants with real bar programs rather than destination cocktail rooms. Station Square, the Tudor-style plaza at the LIRR station, is the postcard: it looks like a European village square and holds the neighborhood's most atmospheric few hundred feet.

  • Forest Hills Station House: the neighborhood's best beer list and elevated pub food; works equally as a date spot and a pre-show staging area. Verified operating, 2026.
  • Irish Cottage: a family-run institution on 72nd Avenue dating to 1960, open to roughly midnight weeknights and 2 AM Friday–Saturday — about as late as Forest Hills gets. Verified operating, 2026.
  • Austin Street restaurant bars and wine bars: a rotating cast of wine-focused and cocktail-capable restaurants fills the strip; treat specific names as check-before-you-go, since Austin Street storefronts turn over steadily. Banter, the Irish bar many older guides still list, closed back in 2020.

The honest read: on a random Tuesday, Forest Hills is a two-stop night — dinner with drinks, one more round somewhere, home by midnight. That's not a flaw; it's what the neighborhood is for. Locals who want more hop the E/F eight minutes to Jackson Heights or ride into Astoria; see the Queens overview for how the borough's scenes ladder up.

Getting there and getting home

The E, F, M, and R all stop at Forest Hills–71st Av, in the middle of the action; the E and F run express and reach Midtown in roughly 25–30 minutes. The LIRR stops right at Station Square — about 15–20 minutes from Penn Station or Grand Central, and the fastest way in for a show if the timing lines up. After concerts, the subway is the higher-capacity escape when LIRR platforms jam. The subway runs 24 hours; overnight, the E is your reliable friend. Route details and late-night transfer strategy are in our subway-for-nightlife guide.

Safety is a non-issue by NYC standards — Forest Hills is one of the calmer nightlife environments in this guide. The bigger risk to your night is timing: kitchens close early, and a 10:45 PM "let's get one more" often meets a locked door.

FAQ

Is Forest Hills nightlife good on non-concert nights?

It's pleasant but quiet: pubs, wine bars, and restaurant bars, mostly done by 11 PM–midnight, with a couple of weekend spots running to about 2 AM. Come for a relaxed dinner-and-drinks night, not a crawl.

Where should I drink before or after a stadium show?

Forest Hills Station House is the pre-show anchor by the LIRR; Irish Cottage and the Austin Street strip take the overflow. Arrive 90 minutes before doors on big nights. Post-show, walk to Austin Street and check kitchen hours.

How late are bars open?

Mostly 11 PM–midnight weekdays; Irish Cottage and a few others reach roughly 2 AM Friday–Saturday. Nothing here runs to 4 AM.

How do I get to Forest Hills Stadium by transit?

E/F/M/R to Forest Hills–71st Av or LIRR to Station Square; both are a 5–10 minute walk to the gates. LIRR is about 15–20 minutes from Manhattan; the subway is the better post-show exit.
