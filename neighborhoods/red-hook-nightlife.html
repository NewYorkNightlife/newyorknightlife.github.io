Red Hook nightlife

Red Hook nightlife asks more of you than any neighborhood in Brooklyn — no subway, spread-out blocks, early-ish closings — and pays it back with the most distinctive night out in the city: a 130-year-old longshoreman's bar with live bluegrass, harbor sunsets over the Statue of Liberty, and one of New York's best burgers. The trade is simple: plan the transport, and everything else about the night takes care of itself.

Here's what's actually open in 2026 (several famous names are gone), what it costs, and how to get in and — more importantly — out.

The short version

  • Best for: a food-and-bar pilgrimage, live roots music, harbor sunsets, small-group nights that feel like a day trip.
  • Worst for: spontaneity, bar-hopping density, anyone who won't pre-plan the ride home.
  • Peak window: 5pm–midnight; Saturday evenings around Sunny's are the liveliest.
  • Budget: $7–12 beers, $30–50/person food stops, plus $20–40 in car fare if you're not busing or biking.

The anchors

Sunny's Bar

Sunny's (253 Conover St) has stood at the end of the waterfront since around 1890 and is the reason most people make the trip: a cluttered, warm, genuinely old barroom that runs live music most nights, most famously the Saturday-night bluegrass jam where musicians pack the back room. It survived Hurricane Sandy and near-loss after longtime owner Sunny Balzano's death, and it operates today as a community institution. Drinks are honest-bar priced; it has historically been cash-friendly, so carry some. Schedule at sunnysredhook.com. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

Red Hook Tavern — and its new cocktail bar

Red Hook Tavern (329 Van Brunt St), opened in 2019 by Hometown's Billy Durney, is the polished old-New-York room whose dry-aged burger routinely tops best-burger lists (expect roughly $30 for it, and a wait without a reservation). In May 2026 the team opened a dark, moody cocktail bar next door — reported by Brooklyn Magazine — giving Van Brunt Street its first proper cocktail-den option and a natural second stop. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

Hometown Bar-B-Que

Hometown (454 Van Brunt St) is a Michelin Bib Gourmand Texas-style smokehouse in a warehouse, and its evening scene is better than most bars: order brisket and ribs at the counter, grab a beer or whiskey, and catch the live music it hosts on many nights. Portions are big; $30–45 a head eats very well. Note it has historically taken Mondays off — check before trekking out. Last verified: 2026-08-26.

Distilleries — what's actually left

Red Hook's craft-spirits story needs a 2026 correction. Widow Jane, the bourbon maker on Conover St, remains the neighborhood's marquee distillery name; verify current tasting-room hours before building a stop around it. Van Brunt Stillhouse, however, closed in 2024 after 13 years (Brooklyn Eagle reported the shutdown and noted Brooklyn Gin taking over the site with plans to reopen a tasting room). Don't route a night around the Stillhouse. Status checked: 2026-08-26.

Gone but still Googled: Fort Defiance

Fort Defiance, the beloved cafe-bar that anchored Van Brunt St for 15 years, closed in late 2023. Pitt's, the Southern restaurant that took over the space in early 2025 to strong reviews, closed for good in early 2026 per Time Out New York. If a guide lists either as a stop, it's out of date.

The transit-desert reality

There is no subway in Red Hook. Your options, honestly ranked:

  • NYC Ferry to Red Hook/Atlantic Basin — the best arrival (harbor views, lands near the action), but evening service ends; it is an arrival plan, not an exit plan.
  • B61 bus — runs along Van Brunt St toward Downtown Brooklyn and the F/G; reliable but slow, and the realistic late-night exit if you're not paying for a car.
  • Citi Bike — 10–15 minutes from Carroll Gardens; great before drinks, judgment call after.
  • Walk — 15–20 minutes from the F/G at Smith–9th Sts or Carroll St; fine going in, long and dark coming back.
  • Rideshare — budget $15–30 to brownstone Brooklyn or $30–40+ to Manhattan, and expect a few minutes' wait; cars are scarcer out here.

Decide your exit before your first drink — it's the single rule that makes Red Hook easy. More options: safe late-night transport in NYC.

Why it's worth it anyway

The same isolation that makes the logistics annoying is what preserved the neighborhood: no through-traffic, no bar-crawl crowds, water on three sides, and rooms that have evolved on their own clock. Time your arrival an hour before sunset, watch the harbor light from Valentino Pier, and do a two- or three-stop night along Van Brunt St. Pair it with early-evening drinks in Cobble Hill or end near the F/G and continue in Park Slope. FAQ

How do I get to Red Hook without a car?

There's no subway stop in Red Hook. Take the NYC Ferry to Red Hook/Atlantic Basin, the B61 bus, a Citi Bike, or walk about 15–20 minutes from the F/G at Smith–9th Sts or Carroll St. Ferries stop in the evening, so plan your return as a bus, bike, or car.

Is Sunny's Bar still open?

Yes. The circa-1890 waterfront bar survived Sandy and ownership scares and continues to run live music most nights, including its famous Saturday bluegrass jam. Cash has historically been favored — bring some. Check sunnysredhook.com for the current schedule.

What happened to Fort Defiance and Van Brunt Stillhouse?

Both are gone. Fort Defiance closed in late 2023 after 15 years; its successor in the space, Pitt's, closed in early 2026. Van Brunt Stillhouse shut down in 2024, with Brooklyn Gin taking over the site. Widow Jane remains the neighborhood's operating distillery name — verify tasting-room hours before going.

Is Red Hook worth the hassle for one night?

Yes, if you want the least Manhattan-like night in New York: harbor views, a 130-year-old bar with live music, one of the city's best burgers, and genuine quiet between stops. Go before sunset, plan 2–3 stops on Van Brunt Street, and pre-book or budget a car home.
