Crown Heights Nightlife: What Actually Survived on Franklin Avenue

Franklin Avenue between Sterling Place and Eastern Parkway lost four venues in roughly two years. The rooms that are left are genuinely worth the trip — a live-music bar with published ticket prices, a beer garden, two late bars, and the cheapest good dinner in this part of Brooklyn. Here is what is open in 2026, what it costs, and which famous names on other lists are gone.

Crown Heights is the Brooklyn neighborhood most damaged by the 2024–2026 closure wave, and any guide that has not been rechecked this year will send you to at least two doors that no longer open. Barboncino, Mayfield, Franklin820 and Winner on Franklin were all on Franklin Avenue and all are gone. That is the honest headline. The second honest headline is that what remains is unusually good value: Friends and Lovers is one of the few venues in Brooklyn that publishes its cover prices, and almost nothing else here charges one at all.

The geography is simple. Franklin Avenue from Sterling Place south to Eastern Parkway is the corridor — bars, restaurants, and the Franklin Avenue–Medgar Evers College subway at the bottom. Classon Avenue, one block west, holds the live-music room. Bergen Street east of Franklin is where the late DJ bar sits. Anything on Fulton Street is technically Clinton Hill, and anything on Rogers Avenue or Flatbush Avenue is Flatbush — we label those rather than pretending otherwise. For the wider map see the Brooklyn nightlife by zone guide, neighbouring Bed-Stuy and Park Slope, or the neighborhoods hub.

The bars that are still here

Friends and Lovers (641 Classon Ave) is the reason to come, and the only venue on this page that publishes what it charges. It runs live music, comedy and dance parties Wednesday through Sunday — Wednesday 7pm to midnight, Thursday to 2am, Friday and Saturday 7pm to 4am, Sunday 5pm to midnight. Comedy is advertised around $8 as an early-bird ticket and $12 at the door, dance parties run roughly $5–10 general admission, and some nights are free with an RSVP. Its calendar carries dates into September 2026. If you read anywhere that it closed, that is wrong.

Franklin Park (618 St Johns Pl) is the beer garden — a big indoor-outdoor room open Monday to Thursday 1pm to 1am, Friday and Saturday noon to 2am, and Sunday noon to midnight, with no cover. One caveat worth stating plainly: a major city tourism directory currently carries an "out of business" flag on its listing, which contradicts the bar's own site, its active Instagram and a Yelp page updated this month. Three live sources against one stale database field is not a close call, so we list it — but that stale flag is why you may see it described as closed elsewhere.

The Crown Inn (724 Franklin Ave) is the long-hours neighborhood bar: 4pm to 2am Monday, to 2:30am midweek, to 4am Thursday through Saturday, and 1pm to 3am Sunday. It is open and busy. It also has no usable website — the domain that carries its name now redirects to a GoDaddy "for sale" page, so we are not linking it and neither should anything else.

King Tai (1095 Bergen St) has been running since March 2015 and is the DJ-and-food-pop-up option: Monday to Thursday 5pm to 1am, Friday 5pm to 3am, Saturday 4pm to 3am, Sunday 4pm to 1am, with a daily 5–8pm happy hour. It publishes no prices. Sisters (900 Fulton St) is over the line in Clinton Hill but is the closest thing to a proper music room in walking distance — a small stage and DJ booth in the back, DJs every weekend, open 11am to midnight Sunday to Thursday and to 2am Friday and Saturday. Its calendar advertises DJ nights at 9pm with no cover, which makes it the best free-music option within walking distance of Franklin Avenue. One technical note if you are searching for it: only the www version of its domain works — the bare domain fails a certificate check — so a link without the www will look broken.

One that we deliberately are not listing: Tip Top Bar & Grill on Franklin Avenue. It is a genuine old Brooklyn institution and it may well be open, but the only listing we could reach carries reviews more than a decade old and we found no 2025 or 2026 source at all. We would rather leave it off than assert it.

Dinner, and the free museum night

Franklin Avenue's restaurants are the reason a lot of people come south from Bed-Stuy, and they are cheaper than the equivalent rooms in Prospect Heights. Chavela's (736 Franklin Ave) has been the anchor for years and is open seven days, 11am to 11pm — wide enough hours that it works as a pre-bar dinner or a late plate. Cent'Anni (742 Franklin Ave) has been making pasta by hand since 2014 and runs 4–10pm Monday to Thursday, to 11pm Friday, and from 11am at weekends, with a garden patio. RAS Plant Based (739 Franklin Ave) is vegan Ethiopian, open to 10pm most nights and 11pm on Fridays and Saturdays. None of the three publishes prices online, so anything you read elsewhere is an estimate.

The best-value evening in the area is not a bar at all. Brooklyn Museum First Saturdays returned for a 2026 season that opened on 7 February, running 5pm to 10pm with free admission included — music, talks and gallery access on the first Saturday of the month. The catch that trips people up: registration is now required, with tickets released on a rolling schedule ahead of each edition. Turning up at the door is no longer the plan. The museum is at 200 Eastern Parkway, technically Prospect Heights, an easy walk from the Franklin Avenue bars.

Weeksville Heritage Center (158 Buffalo Ave) is the neighborhood's most significant cultural site — the preserved houses of one of America's first free Black communities. Be clear-eyed about the hours, though: it is open Tuesday to Friday 10am to 5pm and Saturday 11am to 5pm, closed Sunday and Monday. Evening programming happens, but it is occasional and ticketed per event, not a standing night out. Check its calendar before you build an evening around it.

Two places we list with their real neighborhood attached rather than quietly filing them under Crown Heights: The Rogers Garden (708 Rogers Ave) is a Caribbean rum bar with live music and a Friday fish fry, open Tuesday to Sunday 3pm to 2am and closed Mondays — but it is in Flatbush, in the area known as Little Caribbean, not Crown Heights. And Kings Theatre (1027 Flatbush Ave) is a restored 3,000-seat movie palace booking major tours through the end of 2026 — also Flatbush, and roughly four miles away. Both are worth the trip; neither is a walk.

Getting home: which trains actually run

Crown Heights looks well connected on a subway map and is less so at 2am, because three of the five lines that serve it thin out or vanish overnight.

At Franklin Avenue–Medgar Evers College, where Franklin Avenue meets Eastern Parkway, the 2 and the 4 run at all times. The 3 does not run late nights, and the 5 is weekdays only — overnight the 5 shrinks to a Bronx-only shuttle and the 3 short-turns in Manhattan without reaching Brooklyn at all. At Nostrand Avenue the 3 covers days and the 4 covers overnight. So after roughly midnight your realistic options on Eastern Parkway are the 2 and the 4.

The Franklin Avenue Shuttle does run 24 hours, which is worth stating because several guides claim it does not. It connects Franklin Avenue to Prospect Park via Park Place and Botanic Garden, and its weekend service was increased in December 2024. It is four stops and it is genuinely useful for reaching Prospect Park or the Brighton line late.

On Fulton Street, where Sisters is, the C stops running overnight — it is a daytime-only service. You are not stranded: the A takes over and makes all local stops on that stretch through the night, just less often. Our late-night subway route tool covers the same ground for other neighborhoods.

The honest downsides

  • The list is short, and it shrank. Four Franklin Avenue venues closed between February 2025 and February 2026. Plan five or six real options, not twenty, and treat any inherited listicle as stale until proven otherwise.
  • Websites lie in both directions here. Mayfield closed in February 2026 and its website is still live with current hours. The Crown Inn is open and busy and its domain is parked for sale. An official domain is not evidence of anything in this neighborhood.
  • Barely anyone publishes prices. Friends and Lovers is the exception. No restaurant on Franklin Avenue posts a menu with numbers on its own site.
  • Three trains disappear overnight. The 3 and the 5 do not serve Crown Heights late, and the C stops on Fulton Street. Know before you drink that you are going home on the 2, the 4, the A or the shuttle.
  • Half the "Crown Heights" list is not Crown Heights. Kings Theatre and The Rogers Garden are Flatbush, Sisters is Clinton Hill, and the Brooklyn Museum is Prospect Heights. We say so; most guides do not.
  • There is no big club. Nothing here is a late dance venue in the Bushwick sense. Friends and Lovers and Sisters are the closest you get, and both are bars with rooms rather than clubs.

Recently closed: how to spot a stale Crown Heights guide

Franklin Avenue between Sterling Place and Eastern Parkway lost four venues in roughly two years. If a guide lists any of these as current, it has not been checked this year — discount the rest of it accordingly.

  • Barboncino (781 Franklin Ave) served its last day on 28 February 2025 after fourteen years. It was the first unionised pizzeria in New York City, and its operators cited rising costs and stalled negotiations. There was never a separately named "Barboncino Back Bar" — that is an invention that has spread through copied listings.
  • Mayfield (688 Franklin Ave) closed after brunch on Sunday 22 February 2026, thirteen years in, over pandemic-era debt. Its website is still online and still displays hours. This is the single most effective trap on the avenue.
  • Franklin820 (820 Franklin Ave), which opened as Crow Bar and was renamed in 2017, appears to have closed around the end of 2025 — its social accounts went quiet in December 2025 and its listings are marked closed. We could not find press confirming a date, so we are calling it closed with that caveat rather than pretending to a certainty we do not have.
  • Winner on Franklin (747 Franklin Ave) is marked permanently closed. It occupied the space that had been Hunky Dory, which itself closed back in October 2021 — so two generations of that address are still circulating on lists. The Winner bakery in Park Slope is a separate, open business.
  • Butter & Scotch left 818 Franklin Avenue in 2023, and the Berg'n beer hall at 899 Bergen Street closed during the pandemic. Both predate our window and both are still widely listed.
  • "Chevela's" and a Franklin Avenue "Lalibela" do not exist. The first is a misspelling of Chavela's that has propagated into venue lists as if it were a second business; the second borrows the name of a real Ethiopian restaurant that is in Harlem, not Brooklyn. If you see either on a Crown Heights list, that list was assembled without checking.
  • Elsewhere in the city, the same rot applies: Brooklyn Mirage no longer exists under that name — the site relaunched as Pacha New York in June 2026 — and Up&Down, Therapy, Copacabana Times Square, Fort Defiance, Port Morris Distillery, Faro, Barbetta, Rockwood Music Hall, Pig and Khao, Paris Blues, The Rookery and Ippudo Westside are all shut. The Press Lounge now operates as Hudson VU, and Music Hall of Williamsburg closes at the end of 2026. Minton's Playhouse in Harlem is status-contested — we will not call it open or permanently closed until that resolves.

FAQ

Is Crown Heights still good for a night out in 2026?

Yes, but it is a smaller scene than the listicles suggest. Franklin Avenue between Sterling Place and Eastern Parkway lost four venues in roughly two years — Barboncino, Mayfield, Franklin820 and Winner on Franklin are all gone. What survived is genuinely good: Friends and Lovers for live music and comedy, Franklin Park for a beer garden, The Crown Inn and King Tai for late drinking, and Chavela's, Cent'Anni and RAS Plant Based for dinner. Plan for five or six real options, not twenty.

How much is a night out in Crown Heights?

Cheaper than most of Brooklyn, because the covers are small where they exist at all. Friends and Lovers is the only venue on this page that publishes prices: comedy shows run about $8 in advance and $12 at the door, dance parties are roughly $5 to $10 general admission, and some nights are free with an RSVP. Everywhere else in Crown Heights is drinks-only, so a bar night lands around $40 to $75 per person. A dinner-led evening at Cent'Anni or Chavela's adds $40 to $70.

How do you get home from Crown Heights late at night?

Know which trains actually run. At Franklin Avenue–Medgar Evers College the 2 and the 4 run at all times, but the 3 does not run late nights and the 5 is weekdays only — overnight in Brooklyn the 5 becomes a Bronx-only shuttle and the 3 short-turns in Manhattan. At Nostrand Avenue it is the 4 overnight. The Franklin Avenue Shuttle does run 24 hours, contrary to what several guides claim, connecting Franklin Avenue to Prospect Park via Botanic Garden. On Fulton Street the C stops running overnight and the A takes over making all local stops.

Which Crown Heights venues have closed recently?

Four on Franklin Avenue alone. Barboncino, the pizzeria at 781 Franklin Avenue and the first unionised pizzeria in New York City, served its last day on 28 February 2025 after fourteen years. Mayfield at 688 Franklin Avenue closed after brunch on 22 February 2026 after thirteen years. Franklin820 at 820 Franklin Avenue appears to have closed at the end of 2025. Winner on Franklin at 747 Franklin Avenue is marked permanently closed. Further back but still widely listed: Butter & Scotch left 818 Franklin Avenue in 2023, and the Berg'n beer hall on Bergen Street closed during the pandemic.

Is the Brooklyn Museum still free on First Saturdays?

Yes, and it is the best-value night in the area — but you cannot simply turn up any more. First Saturdays returned for a 2026 season that opened on 7 February, running 5pm to 10pm with free admission included. Registration is required and tickets are released on a rolling schedule ahead of each edition, so book before the date rather than arriving at the door. The museum is at 200 Eastern Parkway, technically in Prospect Heights, a short walk from the Franklin Avenue bars.
