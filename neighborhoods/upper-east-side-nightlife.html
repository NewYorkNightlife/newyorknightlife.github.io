Upper East Side Nightlife: Bemelmans, the 4am Bars, and Why There Is No Club Up Here

A murals-and-martinis piano bar with a published cover, a bar on Second Avenue that actually uses New York's 4am last call, museums open until 9pm — and, according to the state's own liquor licence records, not one nightclub in the entire district. Here is what is open in 2026 and what it charges.

The Upper East Side is the most misdescribed nightlife neighborhood in Manhattan. Guides either dismiss it as dead after 10pm or oversell it as a scene. Both are wrong, and the truth is easy to state: the Upper East Side has a handful of genuinely late bars, a world-class piano-bar tradition, the city's best museum evening hours — and no dance floor whatsoever.

That last claim is not an impression. New York State's public liquor licence records for the five core Upper East Side ZIP codes list 339 restaurant licences, 56 additional-bar licences, 20 "club" licences — every one of which is a private members' social club such as the Union Club, the Colony Club or the Knickerbocker — and exactly one cabaret licence, which belongs to the operator of Wollman Rink in Central Park. There is no public dance venue in the district. If you want to dance, eat and drink extremely well up here and then go downtown. See the NYC clubs guide or the Meatpacking guide for where that night actually is.

The rough geography: Second Avenue from 76th to 89th is the real bar corridor and holds every late licence worth knowing. Fifth and Madison from 65th to 91st is Museum Mile, which is where the genuinely interesting evenings are. Third and Lexington hold the neighborhood restaurants. East 60th to 65th is the fine-dining edge and is arguably Lenox Hill rather than the Upper East Side proper. For the wider map see the Manhattan nightlife guide, neighbouring Upper West Side and Harlem, or the neighborhoods hub.

Bemelmans, and the only cover charge in New York worth paying

Bemelmans Bar at 35 East 76th Street, inside the Carlyle, is the single best argument for an Upper East Side night and one of the very few New York bars that publishes its cover charge, so you can decide before you queue.

Live music starts at 5:30pm and the charge applies from then. At a table it is $25 to $35 per person; at the bar it is $20 to $25 per person, varying by performer. Piano runs from 5:30pm and a jazz trio takes over from around 9pm. Hours are Sunday and Monday to midnight, Tuesday to Thursday to 12:30am, and Friday and Saturday to 1am. There is a dress code, stated on the hotel's own site in the politest possible terms: athletic and casual leisure wear "are better suited for other settings."

Two warnings. First, a widely-copied "$10 piano cover at 5:30pm" figure circulates on blogs and is not on the venue's own page — treat it as stale. Second, the bar takes no reservations, so the cover is the cheap part; the wait is the real cost.

In the same building, Café Carlyle is the actual supper club — open since 1955, Vertès murals, dinner from 6:30pm and performances at 8:45pm. It publishes no prices at all, so call ahead rather than trusting a third-party figure.

The budget alternative is genuinely good: Brandy's Piano Bar (235 E 84th St) runs live piano with singing waitstaff nightly from 9:30pm to 3am, with no cover and a two-drink minimum. Its website is poorly maintained — it still carries a proof-of-vaccination notice — so confirm the schedule by phone before you build a night on it.

The bars that actually stay open

New York permits a 4am last call. On the Upper East Side, one bar genuinely uses it.

The Penrose (1590 Second Ave, between 82nd and 83rd) runs Monday to Friday 11:30am–4am and Saturday and Sunday 9:30am–4am — the only 4am close we could verify in the neighborhood. It also runs the best-value late offer up here: happy hour Monday to Friday 3–6pm at $12 cocktails, $12 wine and $5 Spaten draft, then a late-night happy hour after 10pm nightly at $10, with a late food menu from 11pm to 2am. Note it goes strictly 21+ after 8pm.

Dorrian's Red Hand (1616 Second Ave) opens at noon and runs to 3:45am, has been there since 1960, and has run a Tuesday karaoke night for more than twenty years. J.G. Melon (1291 Third Ave) is the burger institution and stays open Monday to Saturday until 3am and Sunday until 1am — with two rules that catch people out every night: no reservations and cash only.

Uva Wine Bar (1486 Second Ave) pours more than forty wines by the glass, Monday 3pm–midnight, Tuesday to Friday 3pm–1am and weekends 11am–1am. Bondurants (303 E 85th St) is the whiskey-and-cocktail room, to 2am Friday through Sunday and midnight midweek. The Sefton is open but has moved to 1373 First Avenue — the old Second Avenue address no longer has a licensed venue at all, so ignore any listing that sends you there. Its published hours are internally contradictory, so we are not quoting them.

Auction House (300 E 89th St) is the candlelit Victorian-parlour bar, and we can confirm it is operating from its active state liquor licence and city health-inspection record. We are deliberately not linking its website: the domain that looks like its official address has been taken over by an unrelated affiliate site selling bar supplies. Find it on Instagram or walk in. For more of the city's rooms see our best NYC bars guide.

Dinner, and three addresses that moved

The Upper East Side's restaurants are the real draw, and three of the best-known have changed address in ways that almost every guide has missed.

Daniel (60 E 65th St) is unmoved and, despite a widely-misread building sale in March 2026, is staying — it signed a twenty-year lease. It serves Tuesday to Sunday 5pm–9:30pm and strongly recommends formal or business attire. Café Boulud, by contrast, has relocated to 100 East 63rd Street; its old Surrey Hotel address on East 76th is now a different business entirely. It serves Monday to Thursday 5–9pm and Friday and Saturday 5–9:30pm, with the bar opening at 6pm.

Elio's (1621 Second Ave) is the enduring neighborhood Italian, open since 1981 and running Monday to Saturday 5pm–11:30pm and Sunday from 4:30pm — one of the latest kitchens in the district. Hoexter's (174 E 82nd St) is the brasserie, Sunday to Thursday 5–10pm and Friday and Saturday to 11pm. Flex Mussels has moved to 1431 Third Avenue — its old East 82nd Street address is now Hoexter's, which is exactly the kind of swap that produces wrong listings. It runs happy hour twice a night, 4–6pm and again 9–10:30pm.

Jones Wood Foundry (401 E 76th St) is the British pub with cask ale and a garden; the kitchen stops at 10pm but the bar runs to midnight. The Jeffrey (311 E 60th St) is the craft-beer room by the bridge — its own site says open every day 11am to 2am, while aggregators disagree, so confirm before making it the last stop. Sfoglia (1402 Lexington Ave) serves Monday and Sunday 5:30–9:30pm and Tuesday to Saturday to 10pm — call first, because we could find no active liquor licence at that address, so do not assume a wine list.

Museum Mile is the real Upper East Side night

This is the part the nightlife guides skip, and it is the neighborhood's genuine advantage over anywhere else in the city.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art (1000 Fifth Ave) is open Friday and Saturday until 9pm, and closed Wednesdays — the rest of the week it runs 10am to 5pm. Admission is $30 for adults, $22 seniors and $17 students, and crucially it remains pay-what-you-wish for New York State residents and for students from New York, New Jersey and Connecticut, though you must pay something and out-of-state students need to buy in person with ID. One correction: the old "Balcony Bar with live music" is gone. The space now operates as the Great Hall Balcony Cafe, serving wine, beer and cocktails Friday and Saturday 11am–8pm, and the adjacent Balcony Lounge is members only. No live-music programme is currently published, so discount any guide still promising one.

The Frick Collection (1 E 70th St) reopened on 17 April 2025 after a long renovation and a temporary stint at Frick Madison, so pre-2025 guidance about it is all stale. It runs Monday and Wednesday to Sunday 10:30am–5:30pm, closed Tuesdays, at $30 for adults. Its evening programme is called Louis Vuitton First Fridays — free admission from 5:30 to 9pm on the first Friday of each month, except January and September. That is the best free evening in the neighborhood.

Cooper Hewitt (2 E 91st St) is open daily 10am–6pm at $20 for adults, with pay-what-you-wish from 5 to 6pm every day — a useful last-hour trick. The Guggenheim (1071 Fifth Ave) runs 10:30am–5:30pm daily; its Monday 6–8pm slot is members only, not a public evening. 92NY (1395 Lexington Ave) has a genuinely busy evening calendar of concerts, readings and talks in Kaufmann Concert Hall; we could not verify its ticket prices because the site blocks automated checks, so use its own calendar.

Neue Galerie (1048 Fifth Ave) is the trap on every current list: it is closed right now. Its galleries reopen on 12 November and Café Sabarsky, the Viennese cafe that does serve into the evening, reopens on 1 October. After that the cafe runs Wednesday to Sunday 9am–9pm and the museum charges $28. Anyone who turns up this month on the strength of a listicle will find the doors shut.

The honest downsides

  • There is no club, and the licence data proves it. All twenty "club" licences in the district belong to private members' societies, and the single cabaret licence belongs to Wollman Rink. Anything sold as an "Upper East Side nightclub" does not exist.
  • The middle of the market shuts at midnight. Below the three 3–4am bars, most Upper East Side rooms close at or before midnight, and the restaurants stop serving between 10 and 11:30pm. There is a real gap between dinner ending and the late bars being the only option.
  • It is expensive without being exclusive. Bemelmans adds $20–35 per person before you have had a drink, and Time Out puts the average drink there at around $20. You are paying for the room, not for access.
  • J.G. Melon is cash only. Every night somebody discovers this at the end of the meal. There is no card machine.
  • Several venue websites are dead or hijacked. The domains that look official for Auction House, Dorrian's, Eli's Night Shift and several others either fail to resolve or have been taken over. Do not follow a venue link from an old listicle without checking where it goes.
  • Addresses on this side of the park are unusually unreliable. Four of the venues on our own previous version of this page were at the wrong address. If a guide has not been re-checked this year, assume at least one address on it is wrong.

Recently closed and moved: how to spot a stale Upper East Side guide

If a guide lists any of these as current, it has not been checked this year — discount the rest of it accordingly.

  • Café Boulud is no longer at 20 East 76th Street. It moved to 100 East 63rd Street; the old Surrey address now trades as a different business.
  • Bar Pleiades, which shared that Surrey address, is gone entirely. It still appears on Upper East Side cocktail lists.
  • Neue Galerie and Café Sabarsky are closed for renovation right now, reopening 1 October and 12 November respectively.
  • Flex Mussels moved from 174 East 82nd Street to 1431 Third Avenue. The old address is now Hoexter's.
  • The Sefton moved from 1150 Second Avenue to 1373 First Avenue. The old address has no licensed venue at all.
  • The Met's Balcony Bar no longer exists in the form guides describe. It is now the Great Hall Balcony Cafe, with no published live-music programme, and the neighbouring Balcony Lounge is members only.
  • Luke's Bar & Grill, Due and Eli's Night Shift are all still open, but a developer bought their buildings and displacement was announced on 5 August 2026, with seven businesses across four buildings affected. No closing date has been published. We have left Eli's Night Shift off our map for that reason — check before you go, and note its address is 189 East 79th Street, not the Madison Avenue address many guides carry.
  • Bar Boulud and Boulud Sud closed on 23 June 2026 — but note these were Upper West Side rooms, not Upper East Side, despite the Boulud name being associated with this side of the park.
  • Elsewhere in the city, these are confirmed shut and still circulate: Brooklyn Mirage (the site now operates as Pacha New York), Up&Down, Therapy, Copacabana Times Square, Fort Defiance, Port Morris Distillery, Faro, Barbetta, Rockwood Music Hall, Pig and Khao, Paris Blues, The Rookery and Ippudo Westside. The Press Lounge now trades as Hudson VU. Minton's Playhouse in Harlem has a contested status — we can confirm neither that it is open nor that it is permanently closed, so treat any confident claim either way with suspicion.

FAQ

Does the Upper East Side have any nightclubs?

No. New York State's public liquor licence records for the five core Upper East Side ZIP codes show twenty "club" licences, and every one of them belongs to a private members' social club such as the Union Club, the Colony Club or the Knickerbocker. There is exactly one cabaret licence in the district and it belongs to the operator of Wollman Rink in Central Park. There is no public dance venue on the Upper East Side, so if dancing is the point of your night, eat and drink well up here and then head downtown.

How much is the cover charge at Bemelmans Bar?

Bemelmans publishes its music charge, which applies from 5:30pm when live music begins. At a table it is $25 to $35 per person, and at the bar it is $20 to $25 per person, varying by performer. Piano runs from 5:30pm and a jazz trio takes over from around 9pm. A widely-copied "$10 piano cover" figure appears on blogs but is not on the venue's own page, so treat it as stale. The bar takes no reservations, so budget for a wait as well as the charge.

How late do Upper East Side bars stay open?

Only one venue we could verify uses New York's 4am last call: The Penrose at 1590 Second Avenue, open Monday to Friday from 11:30am and weekends from 9:30am, until 4am daily. Dorrian's Red Hand at 1616 Second Avenue runs to 3:45am, and J.G. Melon at 1291 Third Avenue serves until 3am Monday to Saturday and 1am on Sunday. Below those three, most Upper East Side bars close at or before midnight and the restaurants stop serving between 10 and 11:30pm.

Which Upper East Side museums are open at night?

The Metropolitan Museum of Art is open until 9pm on Friday and Saturday and closed on Wednesdays, with adult admission at $30 and pay-what-you-wish admission for New York State residents. The Frick Collection runs Louis Vuitton First Fridays, free from 5:30 to 9pm on the first Friday of each month except January and September. Cooper Hewitt is pay-what-you-wish from 5 to 6pm daily. The Guggenheim's Monday evening slot is members only, and Neue Galerie is closed for renovation until 12 November.

Which Upper East Side venues have closed or moved recently?

Mostly moves rather than closures, and they trip up nearly every guide. Café Boulud relocated from 20 East 76th Street to 100 East 63rd Street, and Bar Pleiades, which shared the old address, is gone entirely. Flex Mussels moved from 174 East 82nd Street to 1431 Third Avenue, and its old space is now Hoexter's. The Sefton moved from 1150 Second Avenue to 1373 First Avenue. Neue Galerie and Café Sabarsky are closed for renovation right now. Luke's Bar & Grill, Due and Eli's Night Shift are still open but a developer buyout displacing them was announced on 5 August 2026.
