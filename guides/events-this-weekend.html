The 10-minute routine: how to find any NYC weekend

Every weekend picks page on this site starts from the same routine. Run it Tuesday or Wednesday for the coming weekend and you'll know more than most promoters' followers do.

MinutesWhereWhat you're doing
0–3Resident Advisor (ra.co)Filter New York + your dates. The deepest club/DJ listings in the city — lineups, set times, tiered prices, RSVP discounts.
3–5DiceSecond sweep for club nights and gigs; some Brooklyn rooms sell exclusively here. Prices are all-in (fees included).
5–7Eventbrite + TicketmasterOne-off parties, themed events, and touring concerts that never appear on RA.
7–9Venue calendars & InstagramCheck the official calendars of 2–3 venues you actually like — some rooms announce only on their own channels or IG stories.
9–10Our venue facts pagesConfirm cover, dress code, age policy, and door style before you commit.

Why RA first: for electronic music it's close to exhaustive — nearly every serious club night in Brooklyn and Manhattan lists there, and the RSVP lists ("free/reduced before 11pm with RSVP") are real money savers. Why Dice second: its catalog overlaps RA but not completely, and its waiting-list feature returns face-value tickets when sold-out events get refunds. Neither covers everything: cocktail bars, comedy-adjacent nights, and some door-only rooms never list anywhere — that's what venue calendars and Instagram are for.

Ticket timing: when advance beats door

Buy advance when: it's a Saturday at a big room, there's a headliner on the bill, or the event uses tiered pricing — tiers only go up as they sell, and the gap between first tier and door can be double. Big weekend bills at major rooms routinely sell out days ahead. Advance also beats door any time entry matters more than flexibility: if missing the door sinks the night, spend the extra dollar on certainty (the same logic that drives table buying).

Pay at the door when: it's a weeknight or a Sunday, the room is a neighborhood bar or mid-size club with no headliner, or the event runs an RSVP list — "free or reduced before 11pm with RSVP" is common on RA listings and is the single best value mechanic in NYC nightlife. Some rooms are door-only by design and never sell advance tickets at all; for those, arriving before the line forms is the ticket strategy. Check the venue's door style on our venue facts pages before you go.

The Tuesday rule: if an event you care about is on sale for the coming weekend, buy it by Tuesday or Wednesday. You'll catch a cheaper tier, and you convert your weekend from "hoping" to "planned" — which is the entire point of the routine above.

Reading the weekend itself

Thursday is the sleeper: industry crowds, easier doors, and programming that's often more interesting than Friday's. Friday is peak supply — the most events, but also the most competition for the good ones. Saturday is peak demand: the biggest bills, the longest lines, the strongest case for advance tickets. Sunday belongs to day parties and early outdoor sessions in the warm months, and to relaxed, cheap nights the rest of the year. Build the weekend around one anchor event you've ticketed, keep one nearby fallback, and let our weekly picks or the guides hub fill the gaps — door tactics here if the anchor has a hard door.

FAQ

Where can I see actual listings for this weekend?

Our weekly picks page publishes dated, sourced selections linking to real ticket pages, and Tonight covers same-day. For raw listings: RA for club/DJ events, Dice for a broad mix, Eventbrite and Ticketmaster for one-offs and concerts.

Should I buy advance tickets or pay at the door?

Advance for Saturdays at big rooms, headliners, and anything tier-priced. Door for weeknights, neighborhood rooms, and RSVP-list events. Door-only venues decide for you — arrive early instead.

What's the best site for NYC club and DJ events?

Resident Advisor (ra.co) is the deepest for electronic music; Dice is the essential second source, and some venues sell exclusively through one or the other. Neither replaces checking a favorite venue's own calendar.

How far ahead do events sell out?

Big Saturday bills: often days ahead, with cheap tiers gone a week-plus out. Weeknights and smaller rooms: usually fine at the door. When in doubt, apply the Tuesday rule.
