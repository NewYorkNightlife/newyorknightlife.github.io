The 10 Best Rooftops in NYC, Ranked (2026)

These are the 10 best rooftop bars in New York City right now, ranked — with view quality, price bands, the reservation reality at each, and which ones actually stay open in winter. Prices and policies come from official venue pages wherever possible; third-party figures are marked as reported.

Ranking criteria: the view first, then atmosphere, value, and how painful entry actually is — full rubric on our methodology page. For rooftops organized by scene and season rather than ranked, see the rooftop bars category guide.

1. Westlight — Williamsburg

View: the best full-frontal Manhattan skyline panorama in the city, 22 floors up at The William Vale. Price band: no cover; cocktail-bar pricing. Reservations: recommended, walk-ins per availability; under-21s admitted until 6pm, 21+ after (official). Seasonal status: year-round — the indoor room has wraparound windows.

Why it's No. 1: It's an Andrew Carmellini cocktail bar that happens to have the city's best view, not a view charging you for mediocre drinks. That combination is rarer than it should be.

Honest downside: Sunset slots on weekends book out, and the outdoor terrace rail gets three-deep at golden hour. Best night: a clear weeknight, booked 30–45 minutes before sunset. Details: Westlight venue page.

2. Le Bain — Meatpacking District

View: Hudson River and downtown from The Standard, High Line's penthouse and roof. Price band: no cover for standing room and bar service — the venue states this in writing; tables optional with minimums. Reservations: tables via Resy; otherwise just show up early. Seasonal status: year-round club; roof (and its famous sunken hot tub) shine in summer — open to 4am Wednesday–Saturday.

Why it ranks here: The only rooftop on this list that's also a genuinely great nightclub, with serious DJ residencies (Eli Escobar, Susanne Bartsch, François K).

Honest downside: The discretionary door gets harder as the night goes on (reported) — dress to impress, no sportswear — and the crowd is a scene. Best night: Wednesday or Thursday from 10pm. Details: Le Bain venue page.

3. Overstory — Financial District

View: 360 degrees from a 64th-floor open-air terrace at 70 Pine — the highest and most dramatic on this list. Price band: premium: $75-per-person minimum spend for reserved seating (food and drink count toward it), cocktails roughly $25–$35. Reservations: Resy, 120-minute windows, weather-dependent. Seasonal status: terrace is weather-dependent; the interior bar operates year-round.

Why it ranks here: World-class cocktails at a height nothing else on this list approaches. It doubles as a top-10 bar, full stop — see our best bars in NYC ranking.

Honest downside: The minimum makes casual drop-ins expensive, and wind or rain can wipe out the terrace on the night you booked. Best night: a clear weeknight sunset slot.

4. Harriet's Rooftop — Dumbo

View: Brooklyn Bridge, East River, and lower Manhattan from the 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge — the best bridge view in New York. Price band: no stated cover, but a suggested 20% gratuity is auto-added; bottle packages published at $2,000–$10,000 if you want a table blowout. Reservations: SevenRooms/Resy, or join the walk-in queue. Seasonal status: weather-permitting outdoor deck; closes 11pm–midnight, so it's a start-the-night spot.

Why it ranks here: Golden hour here is the single best photo-op in NYC nightlife, and the DJ series (Fridays–Sundays) gives it real energy without club friction.

Honest downside: Early closes, weather cancellations, and evening door commonly reported 21+ (~9pm cutoff, not official). Best night: Friday sunset into the DJ set. Details: Harriet's venue page.

5. Magic Hour Rooftop — Times Square

View: the Empire State Building, close enough to feel it, from the Moxy's 18th floor. Price band: no general cover and no fee to reserve (official FAQ); table minimums apply after 9pm Wednesday–Saturday (reported); cocktails ~$18–$22 (reported). Reservations: official widget, 90-minute blocks, $30 no-show fee. Seasonal status: year-round — 10,000 square feet indoor/outdoor with rotating seasonal themes; open to 4am Friday–Saturday.

Why it ranks here: The "urban amusement park" gimmicks (carousel seating, mini-golf) plus a real 4am license make it the most fun-per-dollar rooftop in Midtown.

Honest downside: It's a tourist magnet, and weekend nights enforce dress (no shorts/sandals after 9pm, reported) with long elevator waits. Best night: Thursday for the view without the worst crush. Details: Magic Hour venue page.

6. Somewhere Nowhere — Chelsea

View: 360-degree Manhattan views from the 38th–39th floors of the Renaissance Chelsea. Price band: GA and bar-reservation tiers typically ~$20–$50 per third-party listings (unverified); cabana and daybed daylife sold via ResortPass in season. Reservations: tickets via the venue's weekly events page; bottle service by email. Seasonal status: rooftop pool is summer-only; the club levels run to 4am Friday–Sunday year-round.

Why it ranks here: The only true pool-party-plus-nightclub combo in Manhattan, with a neon-garden aesthetic built for birthdays.

Honest downside: Strictly enforced smart casual (their words) — no shorts at night, no athletic wear, no pool slides — and pricing is opaque until you're in the checkout flow. Best night: Saturday; summer Sundays for the pool. Details: Somewhere Nowhere venue page.

7. Mr. Purple — Lower East Side

View: downtown-looking-uptown panoramas from the 15th floor of Hotel Indigo. Price band: no stated cover; at night, seating effectively means bottle service (reported). Reservations: SevenRooms. Seasonal status: year-round bar, seasonal pool terrace; open to 4am Friday–Saturday.

Why it ranks here: The LES scene rooftop — DJs, day parties, and a young crowd that treats it as a club with a view.

Honest downside: The official dress code ("upscale and fashionable," admission at the host's discretion) is enforced hard on weekend nights, and standing room gets packed. Best night: Friday sunset before the door tightens. Details: Mr. Purple venue page.

8. 230 Fifth — NoMad

View: the Empire State Building filling the sky from NYC's largest rooftop (33,000 sq ft). Price band: $15 cover Friday–Saturday from 8pm, $35 skip-the-line option (official); otherwise free. Reservations: walk-ins welcome; groups of 10+ via SevenRooms. Seasonal status: genuinely year-round — heated igloos November–April.

Why it ranks here: Zero-friction capacity: no reservation needed, all ages until 7pm on weekends, and room for your entire birthday group when everywhere else says no.

Honest downside: It's a volume operation — drinks and service are fine, not destination-grade, and weekend nights are a tourist crush. Best night: a weeknight sunset, or an igloo session in January. Details: 230 Fifth venue page.

9. Ophelia Lounge — Midtown East

View: East River and Midtown from the 26th floor of the 1928 Beekman Tower. Price band: no cover; cocktail-lounge pricing with a Social Hour. Reservations: OpenTable; window tables may carry minimums. Seasonal status: mostly enclosed — a legitimate year-round rooftop.

Why it ranks here: The most romantic room on this list: Art Deco bones, supper-club atmosphere, and a crowd that's there for conversation, not content.

Honest downside: Limited outdoor space, and it's a lounge — come expecting seated pacing, not a party. Best night: a Thursday or Friday golden-hour reservation. Details: Ophelia venue page.

10. PHD Rooftop Lounge — Chelsea

View: Midtown skyline from the 12th floor of the Dream Downtown. Price band: GA cover reported from ~$50; tables reported from ~$1,000 minimum (third-party, unverified — Tao doesn't publish pricing). Reservations: tables via Tao; guest-list culture is heavy. Seasonal status: retractable canopy keeps it running Wednesday–Saturday, 10pm–4am, year-round.

Why it ranks here: When you want the rooftop and the club to be the same room at 2am, this is the one.

Honest downside: Promoter-driven, expensive, and the dress code (no sneakers, athletic wear, or hats — reported) is enforced selectively. Best night: Thursday. Details: PHD venue page.

Rooftop strategy notes

Book the sunset slot, not the 9pm slot — every rooftop on this list is at its best in the hour before dark, and reservations are easiest to get on weeknights. Always have an indoor fallback within 15 minutes for weather (Westlight, Ophelia, and Magic Hour are the safest rain picks). FAQ

Do NYC rooftop bars charge a cover?

Mostly no. Westlight, Le Bain, Harriet's, Magic Hour, Ophelia, and Mr. Purple have no standard cover. The exceptions: 230 Fifth charges $15 after 8pm Friday–Saturday, PHD runs a reported ~$50 GA on club nights, and Overstory requires a $75-per-person minimum for reserved seating.

Are NYC rooftops open in winter?

Many are. 230 Fifth runs heated igloos November–April, Westlight and Ophelia have full indoor rooms with skyline windows, and Magic Hour is indoor/outdoor. Pure outdoor decks like Harriet's are weather-permitting and effectively seasonal.

Which NYC rooftop has the best view?

For the full Manhattan skyline, Westlight. For height and 360-degree drama, Overstory. For the Brooklyn Bridge, Harriet's. For an Empire State Building close-up, 230 Fifth or Magic Hour.

Do I need a reservation?

For sunset hours Thursday–Saturday, book everywhere that takes them. Walk-ins work at 230 Fifth and often at Le Bain's standing room. At Mr. Purple and PHD, night entry is door-managed and seating usually means bottle service.
