The 10 Best Bars in NYC, Ranked (2026)

These are the 10 best bars in New York City right now — ranked, with real prices, honest downsides, and the smartest night to go to each. Every entry below is either verified against the venue's own published info or clearly marked as reported, and the list spans cocktail dens, speakeasies, a hotel-bar legend, and one gloriously cheap dive.

How we rank: drink quality first, then consistency, value, and how hard it is to actually get in. Full criteria are on our methodology page. Looking for a scene-by-scene breakdown instead of a ranked list? That's our NYC bars category guide.

1. Attaboy — Lower East Side

Why it's No. 1: No menu, no cover, no attitude. You describe a mood or a spirit and the bartenders at 134 Eldridge — successor to the legendary Milk & Honey — build you something custom, usually brilliant, for about $20. It made the World's 50 Best Bars extended list again in 2025 (No. 72), more than a decade in, and the drinks still justify the pilgrimage. Open daily 5pm–3am, one of the latest-closing craft rooms in the city.

Honest downside: The wait. Weekend peaks run 30–60+ minutes for ~28 seats, and a no-menu $20 pour is a small gamble every time (it almost always pays off).

Best night: Sunday–Tuesday, or right at 5pm / after 1am on weekends. Limited OpenTable reservations now exist for parties of 6 or fewer. Full details: Attaboy venue facts.

2. The Dead Rabbit — Financial District

Why it ranks here: The most complete bar in New York. Downstairs is a fast, loud modern Irish taproom; upstairs, the Parlor is a multiple "World's Best Bar" winner. No cover anywhere in the building, cocktails in the high teens (reported), and a happy hour with $8 Guinness and $2 oysters Sun–Thu (reported, June 2026).

Honest downside: The FiDi after-work crush Thursday–Friday 5–8pm is real, weekend afternoons run tourist-heavy, and prime Parlor slots book out fast on Resy.

Best night: Tuesday or Wednesday evening — book a Parlor seating, then finish in the Taproom. See The Dead Rabbit venue facts.

3. Double Chicken Please — Lower East Side

Why it ranks here: The most creative cocktail program in the city — drinks riffing on cold pizza, Waldorf salad, and Japanese cold noodle, plus famous fried-chicken sandwiches. The credentials are absurd: it debuted at No. 6 on the World's 50 Best Bars in 2022, was named the best bar in North America in 2023, and still sits at No. 41 on the 2025 world list. The Front Room is walk-in; the Back Room takes reservations.

Honest downside: Lines form before opening and the walk-in Front Room fills within minutes on weekends. It's also firmly on every visitor's checklist now, so the crowd skews scene-y.

Best night: A weeknight, arriving before doors open. Pair it with The Back Room (No. 6 below) for a two-stop LES night.

4. Death & Co — East Village

Why it ranks here: The bar that helped launch the modern cocktail renaissance (NYE 2006/07) is still one of the most reliable world-class rooms in NYC. Cocktails run $17–$26 (most $19–$23), with a legitimately good zero-proof list at $17–$19. No cover.

Honest downside: A strict 2-hour table clock, doorstep waits of an hour-plus on weekends, and a room now firmly on the tourist circuit.

Best night: Sunday–Wednesday, arriving at 6pm sharp when doors open. Details: Death & Co venue facts.

5. Overstory — Financial District

Why it ranks here: Sixty-four floors up at 70 Pine Street, Overstory pairs a genuinely elite cocktail program with a 360-degree wraparound terrace view that flattens every rooftop bar in the city. This is the "special occasion" entry on the list.

Honest downside: The price of admission: reserved seating carries a $75-per-person minimum spend (food and drink count toward it), bookings are 120-minute windows via Resy, cocktails run roughly $25–$35, and the outdoor terrace is weather-dependent.

Best night: A clear weeknight, booked for the hour before sunset. If views are the whole mission, cross-check our best rooftops ranking.

6. The Back Room — Lower East Side

Why it ranks here: One of the few genuine Prohibition-era speakeasies still operating — enter through a toy-company gate and down an alley, then drink cocktails from teacups and beer in paper bags. Entry is free ("Always Free," per the official site), with DJs Thursday–Saturday and free live jazz on Mondays.

Honest downside: The novelty draws crowds, weekend nights get packed, and the door enforces an unusual rule: 21+ Sunday–Thursday but 25+ on Friday and Saturday. Hats, flip-flops, and tank tops are banned.

Best night: Monday jazz night (9pm–12:30am) — the connoisseur move. See The Back Room venue facts.

7. Please Don't Tell (PDT) — East Village

Why it ranks here: The genre-defining modern speakeasy (est. 2007): enter through the phone booth inside Crif Dogs, drink ~$20 cocktails in a tiny taxidermy-lined den, order a hot dog with your julep. Still executing at a very high level nearly two decades in.

Honest downside: Resy-only with no standing room — reservations evaporate, and walk-in bar seats go within minutes of opening. This is the hardest "just show up" on the list.

Best night: Whatever weeknight you can actually book. Set an alarm for the reservation drop. Details: PDT venue facts.

8. Employees Only — West Village

Why it ranks here: The city's great late-night cocktail bar: open until 4am daily (reported) with a real kitchen running late, an in-house psychic, and post-midnight crowds full of off-duty bartenders — always the best endorsement. Cocktails average about $17, signatures around $20 (reported). No cover.

Honest downside: It gets loud and elbow-to-elbow after 10pm, and dinner tables are bookable by phone only, which is either charming or maddening.

Best night: Friday or Saturday after midnight for the industry crowd; a weeknight if you want to hear your date. See Employees Only venue facts.

9. Bemelmans Bar — Upper East Side

Why it ranks here: The hotel-bar entry, and the least replaceable room on this list: 1940s Madeline murals by Ludwig Bemelmans, white-jacketed waiters, and nightly live piano inside The Carlyle. It is old New York, undiluted.

Honest downside: The cost of the time machine — a music cover of about $10 per person at the early set, rising to roughly $20–$35 later in the evening (reported), on top of some of the priciest martinis in Manhattan. Lines to get in are now common since its post-2020 social-media boom.

Best night: A weekday early set around 5:30pm — smaller cover, smaller crowd, same murals.

10. Rudy's Bar & Grill — Hell's Kitchen

Why it ranks here: Every honest "best bars" list needs a dive, and Rudy's is the dive: pouring since 1933, house Rudy's Blonde for a few dollars, pitchers as the default order, and a free hot dog with every drink. Open 8am–4am Monday–Saturday (from noon Sunday).

Honest downside: Cash only, duct-taped booths, sticky floors, and zero pretense of craft. That's the point — but know what you're walking into.

Best night: Any night. It anchors a Hell's Kitchen crawl perfectly — see our NYC bar crawl guide for routes.

How to actually use this list

Don't try to hit more than two or three of these in one night — the top cocktail rooms each deserve 90 minutes-plus, and most cluster conveniently: Attaboy, Double Chicken Please, and The Back Room are all within a few blocks on the Lower East Side. FAQ

Do I need reservations for the best bars in NYC?

For PDT, yes — it's Resy-only. Death & Co, The Dead Rabbit's Parlor, and Overstory strongly reward booking ahead. Attaboy, The Back Room, Employees Only's bar, Bemelmans, and Rudy's all take walk-ins, though Attaboy and Bemelmans can mean 30–60 minute waits at peak.

How much do cocktails cost at NYC's top bars?

Roughly $17–$26 at most of the top cocktail rooms (Death & Co publishes $17–$26; Attaboy and PDT run about $20). Overstory is the outlier at roughly $25–$35 plus a $75-per-person minimum for reserved seats. At the dive end, Rudy's pours a house beer for a few dollars.

Which bar should a first-time visitor pick?

The Dead Rabbit. It takes walk-ins, works for groups or dates, has no cover, and gives you two experiences in one building — a loud Irish taproom and a refined upstairs cocktail parlor.

Are NYC speakeasy bars worth the hype in 2026?

The originals still are. Attaboy, PDT, and The Back Room deliver genuinely great drinks, not just a hidden door. The trade-off is friction: waits, tight rooms, and strict ID checks.
