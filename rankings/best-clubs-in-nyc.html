The 10 Best Clubs in NYC, Ranked (2026)

These are the 10 best nightclubs in New York City right now, ranked — with real cover ranges, door difficulty ratings, honest downsides, and the best night to show up at each. Covers and policies below are pulled from official venue pages and ticket listings where possible; anything third-party is marked as reported.

Ranking criteria: music and production quality first, then crowd, sound, value, and entry friction — the full rubric is on our methodology page. If you want clubs organized by scene and strategy rather than a ranked list, use the NYC clubs category guide.

1. Pacha New York — East Williamsburg

Cover: $50–$130 GA depending on headliner; "Backstage" VIP from $175–$850 per person, listed on the official site. Door difficulty: easy with a ticket — headline events have been 21+.

Why it's No. 1: The former Brooklyn Mirage site (Mirage itself is closed after Avant Gardner's 2025 bankruptcy) relaunched under the Ibiza Pacha brand, and nothing else in New York matches its scale: a seasonal open-air main stage (roughly June–October) plus the indoor Great Hall, pulling bookings like Kaskade, Justice, and Black Coffee.

Honest downside: Premium pricing on everything, huge crowds, a schlep for Manhattan-based visitors — and a venue still proving itself operationally after the ownership chaos.

Best night: A Saturday open-air headliner between June and October. Facts and updates: Pacha New York venue page.

2. Nebula — Midtown

Cover: GA tickets roughly $20–$60 by event via the official site's ticketing. Door difficulty: hard — upscale dress enforced (collared shirts/dress shoes reported for men; no athletic wear or distressed sneakers), 21+.

Why it ranks here: The biggest club to open in Midtown in years — 11,000 square feet, ~800 capacity, 35 bottle-service tables, and a polished LED-heavy production that's the closest thing NYC has to a Vegas residency room. EDM and house headliners run Friday–Saturday 11pm–4am.

Honest downside: Times Square-adjacent crowd (tourists and birthday crews), premium bottle pricing, and a strict door that will bounce you for sneakers.

Best night: Friday headliner nights. Details: Nebula venue page.

3. Marquee New York — Chelsea

Cover: typically reported around $30–$70 depending on the DJ (Tao Group prices per event). Door difficulty: hard — selective, dressed-up, 21+, and unapologetic about it.

Why it ranks here: The archetypal Manhattan mega-club came out of its 2025 renovation stronger — expanded dance floor, upgraded LED rig, A-list DJ bookings Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday.

Honest downside: Long lines, expensive drinks, and a night that punishes poor planning: show up late in sneakers with six guys and you're not getting in.

Best night: Wednesday — the industry/local night — or a Saturday headliner if you've bought tickets ahead. See Marquee venue page, and our VIP club access guide if you're considering a table.

4. House of Yes — Bushwick

Cover: tiered presales roughly $0–$40+, rising closer to the date. Door difficulty: easy with a ticket — but effort in your outfit is functionally part of the entry criteria.

Why it ranks here: NYC's flagship costume-party club: aerialists over the dance floor, burlesque, themed nights, and a crowd that commits completely. Expressive, queer-friendly, and consistently the most fun room in Brooklyn. Not a bottle-service scene.

Honest downside: Good nights sell out in advance, and showing up in street clothes to a themed party is a worse vibe than any hard door.

Best night: Friday or Saturday themed parties — book ahead. Details: House of Yes venue page.

5. Elsewhere — Bushwick

Cover: tickets typically $15–$40 by event. Door difficulty: easy — no dress code; age policy varies 16+ to 21+ per event with strict ID rules (US government ID or passport).

Why it ranks here: The best multi-room value in the city: The Hall for live acts, Zone One for underground club nights, The Loft, and a seasonal rooftop. House, techno, bass, and global/Latin programming until 4am on weekends.

Honest downside: Room quality varies by night — check which space your event is actually in before buying, or a "club night" can turn out to be a small side room.

Best night: Saturday, when all rooms fire at once; summer Fridays for the rooftop. See Elsewhere venue page.

6. Knockdown Center — Maspeth, Queens

Cover: roughly $25–$70 via Dice (official ticketing), tiered presales cheaper. Door difficulty: easy with a ticket; most events 21+, valid ID always required.

Why it ranks here: Warehouse-scale bookings — Underworld, Honey Dijon, Joy Orbison — in a former glass factory, plus the outdoor Ruins courtyard series and Basement, its strict no-phones techno club.

Honest downside: Maspeth is genuinely out of the way — budget a rideshare — and headliners often don't start until after midnight.

Best night: Saturday marathon club shows, or a Sunday daytime Ruins party outdoors. Details: Knockdown Center venue page.

7. Public Records — Gowanus

Cover: free to ~$42 all-in on Dice; most Sound Room nights start around $22 first tier. Door difficulty: easy and friendly; Sound Room nights reported 21+.

Why it ranks here: The best-sounding room in New York — a custom hi-fi system in the Sound Room, plus the Atrium restaurant, UPSTAIRS listening bar, and the seasonal open-air Nursery. Booking taste (Leon Vynehall, Erol Alkan, Benji B) is impeccable.

Honest downside: It's a listening-first crowd — if you want bottle sparklers and Top 40, this is the wrong room. Summer Nursery Sundays sell out fast.

Best night: Friday or Saturday in the Sound Room; Sunday Nursery daytime in summer. See Public Records venue page.

8. Good Room — Greenpoint

Cover: reported roughly $10–$30 via Resident Advisor, presale cheaper than door. Door difficulty: easy — 21+ with ID; arrive before midnight to skip the line.

Why it ranks here: A decade-plus of consistency: two rooms (Good Room / Bad Room), d&b audiotechnik sound, and residencies — FIXED, The Carry Nation, Eli Escobar — that make it the most reliable house-and-disco night in Brooklyn.

Honest downside: Popular nights sell through, and the main room gets tightly packed by 1am.

Best night: A FIXED Friday or an Eli Escobar all-nighter. Details: Good Room venue page.

9. Bossa Nova Civic Club — Bushwick

Cover: free many weeknights and early hours; typically ~$10–$15 cash at the door (reported). Door difficulty: easy — first-come, cash and a 21+ ID; expect a wait after 1am on weekends.

Why it ranks here: The beating heart of cheap, no-frills Bushwick techno: ~140 capacity, heavy fog, good sound for the size, zero pretense, open roughly nightly until 4am. Pound for pound the best value on this list.

Honest downside: Tiny and genuinely cramped at peak, hot and foggy, bathroom lines, cash-only covers.

Best night: Late on a weeknight, or 1am+ Friday–Saturday. See Bossa Nova venue page.

10. Paragon — Bushwick

Cover: reported $28–$45, tiered presales on RA/Dice cheaper. Door difficulty: medium — walk-up without an RSVP is explicitly not allowed; 21+ with a physical state ID.

Why it ranks here: A midsize weekend-only room punching above its weight on programming — techno, house, drum & bass, and Jersey club, including Kevin Saunderson's Pressure parties.

Honest downside: The RSVP/ticket requirement kills spontaneity, and it's weekends-only.

Best night: Friday, ticket bought in advance. Details: Paragon venue page.

FAQ

How much is cover at NYC clubs in 2026?

Roughly $0–$130 depending on the room. Bushwick spots like Bossa Nova run $0–$15 cash; midsize Brooklyn clubs (Good Room, Elsewhere, Public Records, Paragon) run about $10–$45 via presale; Manhattan mega-clubs (Nebula, Marquee) run roughly $20–$70; Pacha New York headliners run $50–$130 GA.

Which NYC clubs have the hardest doors?

Marquee and Nebula — both enforce upscale dress codes and selective entry, especially for larger all-male groups. Ticketed venues like Pacha, Knockdown Center, Elsewhere, and Public Records are easy if you're 21+ with a valid physical ID and a ticket.

What happened to Brooklyn Mirage?

Brooklyn Mirage closed after parent company Avant Gardner's 2025 bankruptcy. The site at 140 Stewart Ave relaunched in 2026 under the Ibiza brand as Pacha New York, keeping the open-air main stage format.

What should I wear?

Manhattan bottle-service clubs expect upscale attire — collared shirts and dress shoes for men, no athletic wear. Brooklyn music venues have no dress code; all-black club casual is the norm. House of Yes actively rewards costume effort.
