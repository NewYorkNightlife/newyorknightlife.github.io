NYC Visit Essentials

The unglamorous logistics that decide whether a New York trip feels smooth or expensive: money, phones, tips, scams, and childcare. None of it is nightlife-specific — all of it shapes your nights. Six guides, each answering one question visitors actually ask.

Money

  • Best Cards for NYC Travel — NYC is heavily card-first (and tap-to-pay works on the subway); this covers which cards save you fees and where you still want some cash.
  • Money Exchange in NYC — why airport and Times Square exchange counters are the worst rates in the city, and what to do instead.
  • Tipping in NYC — the system nobody explains: bars, restaurants, cabs, and the nightlife-specific cases like bottle service and coat check.

Staying connected & staying unscammed

  • eSIM and SIM for NYC Travel — data options for international visitors; matters more at night, when your phone is your map, your ticket, and your ride home.
  • NYC Tourist Scams to Avoid — the running repertoire: costumed-character tips, fake monks, "closed subway" helpers, unsolicited taxis, and fake event tickets. Read this before your first night out.

Parents

  • Parents' Night Out: Babysitter Options — visiting with kids doesn't have to mean skipping the city at night; this covers vetted sitter services and hotel options.

How this connects to your nights

Three of the most common visitor nightlife problems are really essentials problems. First, ID: New York venues card hard, and many require physical government ID — international visitors should carry a passport (some doors won't take foreign ID cards alone; specific policies are on our venue pages). Second, payment: covers and coat checks sometimes want cash even in a card city, so carry a little. Third, the dead-phone-at-3am problem, which the eSIM guide and a $20 power bank solve completely.

Start here if you're overwhelmed

Read the scams guide and the tipping guide — those two cover 80% of visitor friction. Then let the trip cost calculator set your budget, the hotel matcher pick your base, and the visitor start-here page route you to the fun part.

The 24-hour-city caveat

"The city that never sleeps" is true of the subway and diners, less true of everything else. Most shops close by 9–10pm, pharmacies thin out overnight, and banks keep bankers' hours — so handle currency, SIM cards, and pharmacy runs in daylight and keep the nights for the good stuff. The one errand that genuinely works at 4am here is eating, and the late-night food list is the map for it.
