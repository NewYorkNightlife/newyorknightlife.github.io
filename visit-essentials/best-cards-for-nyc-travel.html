Best Cards for NYC Travel: A Strategy, Not a Sales Pitch

For a New York trip, three card features actually matter: no foreign transaction fee, contactless (tap-to-pay), and mobile-wallet support. Get those three right and your cards work everywhere from the subway turnstile to the cocktail bar — without a silent 3% tax on every purchase.

This page is deliberately not a “top 10 credit cards” list. Specific card offers change monthly and the right pick depends on where you live and what you qualify for — so we cover the strategy that stays true, and point you to comparison resources for the shopping part. Numbers last verified: 2026-08-26.

Feature 1: No Foreign Transaction Fee (International Visitors)

Many banks quietly add a foreign-transaction fee — typically up to 3% — to every purchase made abroad. On a $2,000 NYC trip, that’s $60 for nothing. Before you fly, check every card you plan to bring; if they all charge FTFs, it’s genuinely worth getting a no-FTF card (or a travel-money app card) before the trip. Two companion rules squeeze out the remaining leaks: always choose to pay in US dollars when a terminal offers your home currency (that “convenience” is dynamic currency conversion, with markups that dwarf any card fee), and get any walking-around cash from a bank ATM rather than an exchange counter — the full reasoning is in our money exchange guide.

Feature 2: Contactless — Because the Subway Requires It

New York’s transit system went fully tap-to-pay: OMNY replaced the MetroCard, which stopped being sold in January 2026. OMNY readers accept contactless cards on all major networks — Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, JCB, UnionPay — plus phones and watches via Apple Pay and Google Pay. The numbers, verified 2026-08-26:

  • $3.00 per subway or local bus ride (raised from $2.90 in January 2026), with free transfers between subway and bus within two hours.
  • Automatic 7-day fare cap: after 12 paid taps in a rolling 7-day window — about $35 — further rides that week are free. No pass to buy; the cap only works if you tap the same card or wallet every time.
  • No contactless card? Buy a physical OMNY card at vending machines and many shops, loadable with cash — the fallback for swipe-only cards.

For a nightlife trip this is quietly great: the subway runs 24 hours, and if you’re out most nights you’ll hit the cap by mid-week and ride free after. Route logic, safety notes, and late-night quirks live in our subway for nightlife guide.

Feature 3: Mobile Wallets Work Nearly Everywhere

Apple Pay and Google Pay are accepted at essentially every card terminal in the city, plus OMNY. A phone-first setup has real advantages on a night out: you’re not handing a physical card over a crowded bar, a lost phone can be locked remotely (a lost card is a cancellation), and wallets shield your real card number. Still bring one physical card — for stubborn terminals, hotel deposit holds, and the 2am dead-battery scenario — and a modest amount of cash.

What about Amex?

The old “nobody takes Amex” warning is dated: since 2019, roughly 99% of US merchants that accept credit cards take American Express — effective parity with Visa and Mastercard. The gaps you’ll actually encounter are cash-only holdouts (no cards at all) and the occasional dive bar or small restaurant with a handwritten “Visa/MC only” sign. Translation: an Amex is fine as a primary, but pack a Visa or Mastercard too.

Choosing an Actual Card: Use Comparison Sites, Not Listicles

We don’t name “the best card for NYC” because there isn’t one — terms, fees, and sign-up offers shift constantly, and the right answer depends on your country, bank, and credit history. What doesn’t shift is the checklist: no foreign transaction fee, contactless, wallet support, and (nice to have) travel protections. Take that checklist to independent comparison resources — NerdWallet, Bankrate, or your home country’s equivalent — and filter by what you actually qualify for. International visitors can skip US-issued cards entirely: a no-fee card from a home bank or a major travel-money app does the job.

Once the plastic is sorted, put real numbers on the trip with the NYC trip cost calculator and the nightlife budget planner, then work through the rest of the visit essentials hub — tipping norms in particular (our tipping guide) decide how much cash to pull. FAQ

Can I use my foreign credit card on the NYC subway?

Yes, if it’s contactless. OMNY readers accept contactless Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, JCB and UnionPay cards, plus Apple Pay and Google Pay. Each tap charges $3.00, and after 12 paid taps in a rolling 7-day week (using the same card or wallet) the rest of the week’s subway and local bus rides are free. Watch foreign-transaction fees, though — 3% on every tap adds up, which is one reason a no-foreign-fee card matters.

Is American Express widely accepted in New York?

Yes — about 99% of US merchants that take credit cards now accept Amex, so acceptance in NYC is near-parity with Visa and Mastercard. The exceptions you’ll actually meet are cash-only spots (which take no cards at all) and the occasional small bar or restaurant with a Visa/MC-only sign. Carrying one Visa or Mastercard as backup closes that gap completely.

Do I need a physical card, or is my phone enough?

Your phone covers 95% of the trip: OMNY, bars, restaurants, taxis, and shops all take Apple Pay and Google Pay. Carry one physical card anyway — for the rare terminal that won’t read a wallet, for hotel check-in holds, and for the night your battery dies at 2am. And carry a little cash for the holdouts.

Which specific credit card is best for a New York trip?

There’s no single answer, and any page that names one is selling something. The right card depends on your home country, credit profile, and what you already hold. Focus on the three features that objectively matter for NYC — no foreign transaction fee, contactless, mobile-wallet support — then compare current offerings on independent comparison sites, since card terms change constantly.
