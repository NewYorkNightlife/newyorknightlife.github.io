eSIM and SIM Options for NYC Travel: What Actually Works in 2026

The short answer for almost every international visitor: install a US eSIM before you fly — a 10 GB plan costs roughly $12–$25 and works the moment you land. This page gives you verified price ranges, the honest truth about subway coverage, and the exact setup steps so you're never stuck on airport WiFi.

Staying connected matters more at night than during the day in NYC: your ride home, your group chat, and your live location all run on data. Consider this the connectivity companion to the visit essentials hub.

Why eSIM Is the Default Answer in 2026

If your phone was made in roughly the last five years and is unlocked, it almost certainly supports eSIM — and that changes the math completely. You buy a plan online, scan a QR code at home, and data activates when you land at JFK. No kiosk, no plastic, no hunting for a paperclip in baggage claim. Providers like Airalo, Saily, and Ubigi resell capacity on the major US networks (typically T-Mobile or AT&T), so coverage in the five boroughs is effectively identical to what locals get.

The main decision is fixed data versus unlimited. Most visitors burn 1–2 GB a day on maps, rideshares, and social apps, which makes a $12–$25 10 GB plan the sweet spot for a week. If you stream video constantly or work from your phone, Holafly's unlimited plans (about $37 for 10 days) remove the anxiety for a modest premium. One real limitation: most travel eSIMs are data-only — no US phone number. That's fine for WhatsApp, Uber, and OpenTable, but a few US services (some restaurant waitlists, some venue guest lists) want a US number for SMS.

US Carrier Prepaid: When It Beats an eSIM

For stays of a month or more, or if you genuinely need a US phone number, walk into a T-Mobile or AT&T store in Manhattan (they're everywhere in Midtown) and buy prepaid. T-Mobile Prepaid runs about $40/month for 10 GB or $50/month unlimited; AT&T Prepaid lands in a similar $40–$65 band for a single month. Mint Mobile is the famous cheap option at ~$15/month, but it's sold in multi-month bundles — great for a semester in New York, pointless for a long weekend.

The airport kiosk warning: SIM vending machines and "tourist SIM" counters at JFK and Newark charge a convenience markup that's hard to justify — packages at $40–$60+ that mirror what $15–$25 buys online or at a carrier store in the city. If you land without a plan, use the free airport WiFi to buy and install an eSIM from your phone right there in arrivals. It takes ten minutes and beats the kiosk price by half or more.

Coverage Reality: The Subway Question

Here's the honest picture, because it surprises most visitors. Every underground station in the system has had cellular service and free WiFi since 2017 — you'll have full bars while waiting on the platform. The tunnels between stations are a different story: on most lines, service still drops the moment the train leaves the platform.

That's changing. The MTA and Boldyn Networks are wiring all 418 track-miles of subway tunnel for cellular service — a $600 million project rolling out over roughly a decade. As of mid-2026, live tunnel segments include the 42nd St Shuttle, the L train between Bedford Av (Brooklyn) and 1st Av (Manhattan), the G train between Court Square and Hoyt–Schermerhorn, and the 4/5 through the Joralemon Street tunnel between Brooklyn and Lower Manhattan — with AT&T typically live first and other carriers following (Last verified: 2026-08-26). Practical takeaway: send the "on my way" text on the platform, not mid-tunnel, and download offline maps before a long ride. If you're heading home late, our safe late-night transport guide covers when the subway makes sense after midnight.

Free WiFi above ground: LinkNYC kiosks — the tall gray pillars on hundreds of sidewalks — offer free gigabit WiFi, free US phone calls, and USB charging. Handy as a backup, but treat any public WiFi accordingly: fine for maps, not for banking.

Setup Steps Before You Fly

  1. Confirm your phone is unlocked and eSIM-capable. Carrier-locked phones can't add a travel eSIM. Check with your home carrier a week before the trip, not at the gate.
  2. Buy and install the eSIM at home on real WiFi. Install the profile before departure but leave the line switched off until you land.
  3. Label your lines. Name them "Home" and "USA Data" in settings so you can see at a glance which is doing what.
  4. Set the travel eSIM as your default data line, and turn data roaming OFF on your home SIM. This is the step that prevents surprise roaming bills.
  5. Download offline essentials: Google Maps offline area for Manhattan and Brooklyn, your ticket PDFs, and your hotel address.
  6. On landing: toggle the US line on, confirm data works, then check in with whoever's tracking your trip.

The dual-SIM WhatsApp tip: keep your home SIM active alongside the US eSIM and don't re-verify WhatsApp — it stays tied to your original number while using US data. You keep every chat and group, and calls to your home number still ring (just don't answer on roaming rates unless it matters).

FAQ

Will my phone work in the NYC subway?

In stations, almost certainly — all underground stations have had cellular service and free WiFi since 2017. In the tunnels between stations, mostly not yet: the MTA and Boldyn Networks are wiring all 418 track-miles of tunnel over roughly a decade, and as of mid-2026 only a handful of segments are live, including the 42nd St Shuttle, the L train between Bedford Av and 1st Av, parts of the G, and the 4/5 through the Joralemon Street tunnel. Expect service to drop between stations on most lines.

Should I buy a SIM card at the airport when I land?

Only as a last resort. Airport kiosks and vending machines routinely charge $40–$60+ for tourist SIM packages that cost half that from a carrier store in Manhattan — and a fraction of that as an eSIM bought online before you fly. If your phone supports eSIM, install one at home and you'll have data the moment you land.

How do I keep my WhatsApp number while using a US eSIM?

WhatsApp stays tied to the phone number it was registered with, not the SIM currently providing data. Keep your home SIM active in your phone (with data roaming off), set the travel eSIM as your data line, and don't re-verify WhatsApp. You'll keep your number for messages while all data flows through the cheap US plan.

How much data do I need for a week in NYC?

Most visitors use 1–2 GB per day between maps, rideshare apps, social posting, and translation — call it 5–10 GB for a week if you lean on hotel and LinkNYC WiFi, more if you stream or upload a lot of video. A 10 GB plan (roughly $12–$25 from major eSIM providers) is the safe buy for a one-week trip.
