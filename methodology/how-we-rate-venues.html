Methodology

How We Rate Venues

Every venue verdict on this site — the ratings on our venue fact pages, the ordering of lists like the best VIP experiences ranking — comes out of the same rubric. This page is that rubric, in full, along with the rules for how facts get verified, updated, and — when a venue stops deserving your money — removed. It works alongside our editorial policy, which governs sourcing and labeling site-wide.

Last verified: 2026-08-26

The six criteria

We rate what a reader can actually feel on a night out, not what a press release promises. Six things, always the same six:

CriterionThe question we askWhat sinks a score
Door fairnessIf you follow the stated rules — dress code, age, arrival time — do you get in?Arbitrary rejections, unstated "ratio" policies, pay-to-pass doors that contradict the published policy
Price honestyDoes the bill match what was advertised or quoted?Unpublished minimums quoted high on the night, surprise fees, covers that jump above the listed range
Energy consistencyIs a Tuesday version of this room recognizably the same product as the Saturday version?Rooms that are only good one night a week but market every night equally
SoundCan you hear the music the venue claims to be about — clearly, at safe levels, from most of the room?Dead zones, conversation-crushing volume in a venue billed as a lounge, neglected systems
SafetyWould we send a solo friend here without a second thought?Overcrowding past comfort, aggressive-crowd tolerance, poor egress, unlit surroundings with no transit plan
StaffDo security, bar, and floor staff treat guests like customers rather than obstacles?Hostile security as policy, disappearing service at tables that already carry a 20% auto-gratuity

We don't publish a fake-precision composite score out of 100. A venue that fails door fairness or safety can't buy the difference back with a good sound system — those two act as caps on any verdict, because they're the two failures that ruin a night outright.

The "Last verified" stamp

Every venue page and pricing guide carries a visible "Last verified" date. It means exactly one thing: on that date, we re-checked the page's facts — hours, covers, minimums, dress code, age policy — against their sources. It is not a review date and not a decoration. Nightlife facts rot fast; the stamp lets you judge freshness yourself, and a stale stamp is a signal to double-check the venue's own channels before you commit. When a re-check changes a fact, the page and its stamp update together.

The 3-source rule for commercial claims

Commercial claims — covers, table minimums, bottle prices, package rates — get the strictest handling, because they're the claims that cost you money when they're wrong. Per our editorial policy, every claim is labeled verified or reported:

  • Verified: published by the venue itself — site, FAQ, or booking system — and fetched by us on a stated date.
  • Reported: not published by the venue. To print a reported number at all, we require it to be consistently described by at least three independent third-party sources (booking platforms, press, credible reviews). Two sources repeating each other don't count as independent.

If a number can't clear either bar, we write "not published" — we never fill the gap with a guess. When you see "reported ~$500+ minimum," that phrasing is deliberate: it tells you the venue won't put the number in writing and that the figure moves by night.

How venue facts get updated

Three triggers, in priority order. Reader corrections first: if you tell us a cover, policy, or closure changed, we re-check and correct before anything else in the queue — contact us. Scheduled re-verification second: high-traffic venue pages get periodic re-checks against their sources, which is what refreshes the "Last verified" stamps. Event-driven checks third: a renovation, ownership change, or news event puts a venue's whole page back under review. Time-sensitive pages like this weekend's picks and Tonight are rebuilt on their own cycle rather than patched.

What gets a venue delisted

Removal is rare and deliberate. A venue comes off our lists when any of these holds:

  • It closes — permanently, or indefinitely without a stated reopening.
  • Safety failures persist: credible, repeated reports of unsafe crowding, security violence, or drink-safety incidents that the venue doesn't address.
  • The door breaks faith as policy: sustained evidence of discriminatory or bait-and-switch door practices, not one bad bouncer on one bad night.
  • Facts can't be kept honest: if a venue's pricing and policies churn so unpredictably that no verified or 3-source figure survives a verification cycle, we'd rather not list it than mislead you.

A bad review is not a delisting. Mediocre venues stay listed with honest verdicts — that's the point of having verdicts.

No pay-to-play

Venues cannot pay to be included, ranked higher, or reviewed more kindly — and being an advertiser or affiliate partner buys exactly nothing in the rubric above. The site earns from ads and affiliate booking links, disclosed in our affiliate disclosure; rankings and ratings are produced by the criteria on this page and nothing else. Our venue verdicts include downsides on purpose. If a verdict ever reads like an ad, hold us to it: tell us.
